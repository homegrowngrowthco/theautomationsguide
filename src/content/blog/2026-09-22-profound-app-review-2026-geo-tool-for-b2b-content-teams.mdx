## Why GEO Is No Longer Optional for B2B Content Teams

The traditional SEO stack (keyword research, on-page optimization, rank tracking) was built for a world where buyers typed queries into Google and clicked blue links. That world is eroding fast. [BrightEdge research on AI search behavior](https://www.brightedge.com/resources/research-reports) puts AI Overviews in over 40% of informational queries now, and Perplexity's monthly active user count has grown faster than any search engine since Google itself. For B2B software buyers doing vendor research, the pattern is clear: ChatGPT and Perplexity get the first-draft shortlist, Google gets the validation click later.

The operational problem this creates for content teams is measurement. Your traditional rank tracker tells you nothing about whether Perplexity cited your "best CRM for outbound" article when a sales leader asked it to compare vendors. Profound plugs that gap. It runs structured queries across AI engines and records whether your brand was cited, how accurately it was described, which competitors appeared alongside you, and what source content the AI actually pulled from.

My opinion: most content teams are treating this as a future problem. It is not. The window to establish AI citation baseline data before your competitors do is closing faster than the window to rank a new blog post closed in 2019.

## How Profound Actually Works

I spent several weeks inside Profound's dashboard, running it against a set of B2B SaaS categories. The core workflow is less complicated than the category name implies.

You define a set of topics or question prompts that your buyers are likely to ask an AI. Think "what is the best sales engagement platform for mid-market" rather than "sales engagement platform." Profound queries AI engines on a scheduled cadence, captures the responses, and scores your brand presence: were you mentioned, were you cited as a source, were you described accurately, and where in the response did you appear.

The output is a visibility share metric (Profound calls it "AI Share of Voice") that you can trend over time and compare against named competitors. Short answer: it works. The "Source Attribution" report is the one I find most operationally useful. It shows which of your URLs are actually being pulled into AI responses, so your content team can double down on formats and structures that AI engines favor. Typically that means structured definitions, comparison tables, and FAQ blocks, which tracks with what [Google's own documentation on AI-ready content](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-overviews) surfaces.

Skip the vanity "mention count" metric. The Source Attribution view is where the real editorial signal lives.

## How to Choose

The selection between these tools is not really a competition. It is a sequencing question. If you are a B2B content team in 2026 with zero AI search monitoring in place, Profound is the gap you fill first. You cannot optimize what you cannot measure, and right now most content teams are flying blind on AI citation performance while their buyers form vendor shortlists in ChatGPT.

Profound does not replace an on-page optimization tool. The practical stack is Profound for GEO monitoring plus one of Surfer, Frase, or Clearscope for content production. Which production tool depends on team size and workflow: [Surfer](/tools/surfer/) for mid-size teams wanting tight editorial guardrails, [Frase](/tools/frase/) for leaner teams who need faster briefs at lower cost, and [Clearscope](/tools/clearscope/) for enterprise content operations with dedicated editors. If you want to think about how these fit into a broader content-driven GTM motion, the principles I laid out in [how to build a $300/mo outbound stack](/blog/2026-09-18-how-to-build-a-300mo-outbound-stack-in-2026/) apply to content budget allocation too: layer tools by the job they do, not the category they claim.

The one scenario where I would skip Profound for now: if your audience is exclusively in a vertical where AI search adoption is still nascent, think highly regulated industries where buyers default to direct referrals. For the majority of B2B SaaS and tech-adjacent audiences, that window has already closed.

Profound is the first tool built for the measurement problem that B2B content teams actually have in 2026, not the one they had in 2022.
