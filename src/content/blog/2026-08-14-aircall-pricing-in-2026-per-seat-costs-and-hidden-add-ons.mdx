## The True Cost: Real Math for a 10-Seat Sales Team

Let me work through what my clients actually see on their invoices. A 10-seat outbound sales team on the Professional plan is where most SDR and AE teams land, because they need Salesforce integration and call monitoring. That's not a nice-to-have for those teams. It's a hard requirement.

**Base plan:** 10 seats x $50/month = $500/month (annual billing)

Clean number. Here is where it gets messier.

**SMS add-on:** Aircall's SMS functionality is not unlimited. Each plan includes a limited credit pool, and outbound-heavy teams burn through it fast. Expect $20 to $40/month in SMS overage for an active 10-seat team.

**AI features (transcription and coaching):** Aircall's AI add-on, which covers real-time transcription, call summaries, and coaching cues, is a separate line item not included in Professional. [Aircall's help center](https://help.aircall.io/) lists this as an optional add-on priced per seat. I've seen clients pay an additional $8 to $15/seat/month for this, adding $80 to $150 to the monthly bill.

**Additional phone numbers:** Each seat comes with one number. Teams that want local presence numbers in multiple area codes pay per number. At $6/number/month, a 10-seat team running two numbers each adds $120/month.

**International calling overages:** The plans cover "unlimited" calling within a defined country list. Calls outside that list, common for teams selling into LATAM or Southeast Asia, are billed per minute at rates ranging from $0.02 to $0.12 depending on destination.

**Realistic total for a 10-seat Professional team:** $500 base + $40 SMS + $100 AI + $60 extra numbers = **$700/month**, before any international overage. That's $84/seat/month, nearly 70% above the advertised $50.

## Where Aircall's Pricing Is Fair vs. Where It Stings

The Professional plan's integration depth is genuinely good. I've run Aircall's HubSpot sync across multiple client stacks, and the call logging, contact association, and deal-linked recording retrieval work more reliably than most competitors I've tested. If your RevOps motion depends on call data flowing cleanly into your CRM without constant manual cleanup, that reliability has real dollar value. [JustCall](/tools/justcall/) has closed the gap meaningfully in 2026, but Aircall still wins on edge cases.

Where the pricing structure frustrates me: AI transcription should be included at the Professional tier by now. Full stop. Every major competitor, from [Aloware](/tools/aloware/) to Dialpad, bundles AI call summaries. Charging per seat on top of an already mid-market price point feels like a holdover decision Aircall hasn't bothered to revisit. Budget for the AI add-on from day one. Do not treat it as optional if rep coaching matters to your team.

[KrispCall](/tools/krispcall/) includes noise cancellation and basic transcription at its base tier. That matters for distributed teams on inconsistent connections. I pointed two early-stage clients toward KrispCall in 2026 specifically because the all-in monthly bill came in 40% lower than Aircall Professional at comparable call quality. The [G2 category page for cloud telephony](https://www.g2.com/categories/cloud-telephony) is useful for cross-referencing user-reported pricing experiences, since vendors sometimes quote different rates than what shows up in actual contracts.

<MyTake>
Aircall's pricing isn't predatory, but it's architected to look cheaper than it is. The 3-seat minimum, the AI add-on gate, and the SMS credit model all push real monthly costs 50 to 70% above the headline number. I'd respect the product more if they just published an honest all-in price instead of making buyers reverse-engineer it. Teams that need ironclad HubSpot or Salesforce logging should still consider it seriously. But anyone evaluating purely on the pricing page is going to get surprised at invoice time.
</MyTake>

## One More Cost Factor: Annual Lock-in

Aircall's pricing assumes annual billing. Month-to-month bumps every tier by roughly 20%, so Essentials goes to $36/seat and Professional to $60/seat. For teams in early growth mode where headcount is unpredictable, that lock-in is a real consideration, not a footnote.

[Aircall's pricing page](https://aircall.io/pricing/) lets you toggle between monthly and annual. Run both numbers against your 12-month headcount forecast before signing. If your team is likely to grow from 8 to 15 seats mid-year, locking in at 8 seats annually and paying monthly for overage seats is often cheaper than upgrading a full annual contract mid-cycle. I've modeled this for clients and the savings can be $200 to $400 over the contract period depending on growth pace.
