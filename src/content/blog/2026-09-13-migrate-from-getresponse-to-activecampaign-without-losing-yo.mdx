## Before you migrate

Do not click anything in ActiveCampaign until you have completed a full audit inside GetResponse. I have watched teams skip this and spend three days reverse-engineering their own automations because they canceled the GetResponse account too early. Not a fun call to get on a Friday.

**What to export and document:**

- Full contact list export as CSV, including all custom fields (go to Contacts > Export, select all custom fields explicitly, not just defaults)
- Every automation workflow, with screenshots of trigger conditions, wait steps, and branching logic. GetResponse lets you export a JSON summary but no other platform can read it
- All active email sequences with send schedules, subject lines, and delay intervals written out
- Segment definitions: the filter logic, not just the segment name
- Form embed codes and confirmation page URLs, because those will break on cutover
- Your current sending domain and DKIM/SPF records pulled from your DNS provider directly, not from GetResponse's dashboard
- Suppression list and unsubscribe list, exported separately

Pause any sequences touching mid-funnel leads before you flip DNS. A lead receiving email step 3 from a domain that has not finished authenticating in ActiveCampaign is a deliverability problem you absolutely do not want to diagnose under pressure.

## What breaks and what does not transfer

Two migration gotchas I always flag before a client starts: webinar triggers and historical engagement scoring.

GetResponse has a native webinar product. If you built automations that fire off webinar attendance, those triggers simply do not exist in ActiveCampaign. You will need a webhook from your webinar tool (Zoom, Demio, or Livestorm all support this) into AC to replicate that behavior. Not difficult, but it adds setup time that teams consistently forget to budget for.

The second one stings more. ActiveCampaign's lead scoring is forward-looking. Contacts imported with high engagement history in GetResponse start at zero in AC, which immediately breaks any score-gated automations you try to carry over. Don't bother trying to reconstruct historical scores manually. Instead, run a 30-day re-engagement sequence in AC first, let contacts self-select by clicking, and build scores from live behavior before you gate anything on a score threshold.

    **The fix: segment by sequence position before exporting**

    In GetResponse, filter contacts by which automation step they are currently on. Export each cohort with a tag recording their position, for example `seq-nurture-step4`. In ActiveCampaign, add a conditional branch at the start of the rebuilt automation: if tag contains `seq-nurture-step4`, skip to step 4 using a "Go to" action. Thirty extra minutes per sequence. Saves you a wave of unsubscribes.

    ```
    AC Automation entry:
    Trigger: Tag added (gr-migrated)
    Step 1: IF tag = seq-nurture-step4
              → Go to: Step 4
            ELSE IF tag = seq-nurture-step3
              → Go to: Step 3
            ELSE
              → Continue from Step 1
    ```

## Verify it worked

Before you cancel GetResponse, run these four checks:

- **Send a test through every rebuilt automation.** Use a seed address and trigger each workflow manually. Confirm timing, branching, and personalization tokens render correctly. ActiveCampaign uses `%FIRSTNAME%` syntax where GetResponse uses a double-brace format. One missed swap means every recipient sees a raw token in their inbox.
- **Check bounce categories in your first real send.** Soft bounces above 3% or hard bounces above 0.5% in your first campaign point to a list quality problem or an authentication issue. Neither is a migration success.
- **Confirm forms write to the right list and apply the right tags.** Submit each form yourself and verify the contact record in AC shows the expected list membership, tag, and custom field values.
- **Verify unsubscribes suppress correctly.** Trigger an unsubscribe via a test contact and confirm they are suppressed across all lists in ActiveCampaign, not just the one they unsubscribed from.

## The migration is worth doing right, not fast

GetResponse is a capable tool for simple broadcast lists and basic drip sequences. Where it consistently loses to ActiveCampaign is automation logic depth: multi-condition branching, deal-stage triggers, and CRM-connected workflows that scale with a real GTM motion. I have run this migration for clients with lists ranging from 5,000 to 120,000 contacts, and the pattern holds every time. Teams that do the prep work (field mapping, sequence position tagging, domain auth first) are live in ActiveCampaign and sending confidently within a week. Teams that skip steps and import fast spend the next month cleaning up [deliverability problems](https://help.activecampaign.com/hc/en-us/articles/221551887-Email-authentication-DKIM-SPF-DMARC) and re-engaging leads who received duplicate emails. Do the prep. According to [G2's 2026 ActiveCampaign reviews](https://www.g2.com/products/activecampaign/reviews), the automation builder is the most consistently praised feature across mid-market users. That reputation is earned. The switch is worth it.
