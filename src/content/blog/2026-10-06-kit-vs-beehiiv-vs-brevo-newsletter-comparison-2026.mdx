Most RevOps teams picking a newsletter platform in 2026 are staring down one of two very different problems. Either they want an audience engine that converts subscribers to revenue, or they need to bolt a newsletter layer onto an existing CRM and outbound stack without blowing the budget. [Kit](/go/kit/), [Beehiiv](/go/beehiiv/), and [Brevo](/go/brevo/) each solve one of those problems cleanly. Each also fails hard when used for the wrong one. This post breaks down exactly where each tool wins, where it breaks, and which stack profile maps to which platform.

## How to choose

The honest question here is not about features. It is about what your newsletter is actually supposed to do.

If the goal is audience monetization, a subscription revenue line, or rapid organic growth through cross-promotion, Beehiiv is the clear choice. The Boosts network alone is a growth lever that neither Kit nor Brevo can match. The analytics depth also makes it straightforward to prove newsletter ROI to a board or an investor. For teams that have run into [conversion problems with Beehiiv's default setup](/blog/2026-09-30-beehiiv-not-converting-revenue-3-fixes-that-work/), the platform tooling is there. It usually just needs configuration.

Kit is the right call when your newsletter is a pipeline feeder, not a standalone business. The automation flexibility lets you build genuinely sophisticated nurture logic: tag a subscriber who clicks a pricing link, drop them into a shorter cadence, suppress them from the next broadcast once they book a demo. That kind of workflow requires conditional branching and action-based triggers. Brevo's automation handles that more rigidly, and Beehiiv barely touches it.

Brevo wins on the budget-plus-CRM-sync angle. If you are running a list of 40,000 contacts but only sending twice a month, and you need that engagement data inside [HubSpot](/tools/hubspot/) or [Salesforce](/tools/salesforce/) without building a custom Zapier chain, Brevo's native integrations and volume pricing make it the cheapest capable option in this tier. Teams that recently [migrated from Mailchimp to Kit](/blog/2026-10-01-mailchimp-to-kit-migration-keep-segments-and-automations/) and then found the CRM sync lacking are exactly the profile Brevo fits.

Kit gets slotted as the "creator" tool and dismissed by B2B RevOps teams who should actually be running it. The tag-based subscriber model maps almost one-to-one to how a modern lead scoring system works. It is also significantly cheaper than adding a dedicated nurture layer to your HubSpot instance. ## The practical bottom line

All three tools are competent ESPs. The differentiators are the growth mechanics (Beehiiv), the automation depth and ecosystem integrations ([Kit](/tools/kit/)), and the pricing model plus CRM sync ([Brevo](/tools/brevo/)). Pick the one that matches what your newsletter is supposed to generate. Audience monetization points to Beehiiv. Pipeline nurture points to Kit. Budget-plus-CRM-sync points to Brevo.

Getting this wrong and rebuilding six months later is the most common and most avoidable failure in GTM newsletter strategy. I'd push every team to answer one question before signing up for anything: is this newsletter a revenue line or a pipeline channel? The answer tells you which platform to pick.
