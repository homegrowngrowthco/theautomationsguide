The question GTM operators should actually be asking is simpler and harder: which tool produces the lowest cost-per-meeting booked relative to what you are already paying for seats, domains, and enrichment? [Lemlist](/go/lemlist/), [Brevo](/go/brevo/), and [Smartlead](/go/smartlead/) occupy three very different positions on that ROI curve. Picking the wrong one does not just waste a $99/month SaaS line item. It quietly inflates your cost-per-pipeline-dollar across the entire outbound motion.

## The ROI math behind each tool

Before comparing the tools side by side, it helps to frame exactly what goes into cost-per-meeting for a cold email motion. Your fully loaded monthly cost is the tool subscription plus domain and inbox costs (usually $3 to $6 per inbox per month on Google Workspace or comparable) plus enrichment (Clay, Apollo, or similar for verified contacts) plus the rep's time on sequence management. Divide that total by meetings booked in the month. That ratio, not open rates or even reply rates, is what tells you whether the tool is working.

I'd argue most teams are tracking the wrong metric entirely. Open rates are a vanity number. Reply rates are directionally useful. Cost-per-meeting is the only figure that connects your outbound tool spend to actual revenue.

## How to choose

The right tool depends almost entirely on how you think about your outbound economics. If your team runs a tight email-only motion and needs to maximize inbox rotation to protect your primary domain reputation, [Smartlead](/tools/smartlead/) wins before you even open the feature comparison. Unlimited email accounts at base tier, purpose-built warmup infrastructure, and per-active-lead pricing means your cost scales with output, not headcount. That is the right model for a lean outbound team or an agency running multiple client campaigns. For context on how deliverability infrastructure separates tools at this level, our [Mailreach review](/blog/2026-11-04-mailreach-review-2026-honest-take-best-alternatives/) covers what good warmup economics look like in practice.

If your reps live on LinkedIn and you are willing to hold them accountable to actually completing multichannel tasks (the usual failure here: sequences with LinkedIn steps that nobody touches), then Lemlist's ROI case becomes real. Per [Lemlist's published case study data](https://www.lemlist.com/case-studies), multichannel sequences consistently outperform email-only on reply rate. The question is whether that reply rate lift is enough to offset the per-seat premium over Smartlead. My read is that it usually is for teams with 1 to 5 reps targeting SMB and mid-market, where individual relationship-building matters. It stops making sense when you are sending at true volume (think 50,000 plus emails per month) and LinkedIn steps become operationally unmanageable. For a deeper look at how [Lemlist](/tools/lemlist/) stacks up on the LinkedIn dimension specifically, see our [Dripify vs Lemlist vs Taplio comparison](/blog/2026-11-05-dripify-vs-lemlist-vs-taplio-best-linkedin-outreach-2026/).

Brevo is the wildcard. It is not a purpose-built cold outreach tool, and I would not pitch it as one. But if a startup is currently paying for ActiveCampaign or GetResponse for marketing automation, Mailchimp for transactional email, and Lemlist or Instantly for cold outreach, the consolidation math on Brevo can get interesting fast. Brevo's platform covers transactional, marketing, and limited outbound sending under a single contact-volume model. One bill instead of three has real CFO appeal, especially at the Series A stage when every SaaS line item is under scrutiny.

## The number that settles it

Pull your last 90 days of outbound data. Take your total tool spend (including domain and enrichment costs, not just the sequencer subscription) and divide by meetings booked. If that number is above $400 for a mid-market ICP, the tool is probably not the problem. Your sequence copy, targeting, or rep follow-through is. If your cost-per-meeting is in range but you are burning budget on features you do not use, that is a tool problem worth fixing.

The first thing I'd check is whether your inbox count matches your sending volume. Teams running a single domain at 500 emails per day are asking for deliverability problems that no sequencer can fix.

For most high-volume, email-first teams, [Smartlead](/tools/smartlead/) is the structurally cheapest path to a lower cost-per-meeting. For teams where multichannel is real and reps are accountable, Lemlist earns its premium. And for teams that want to simplify their stack rather than optimize a single channel, [Brevo](/tools/brevo/) is the consolidation bet worth running the numbers on.
