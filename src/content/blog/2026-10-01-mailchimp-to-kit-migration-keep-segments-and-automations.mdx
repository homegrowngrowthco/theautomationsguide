## Before you migrate

Do not touch Kit until this checklist is done. Skipping prep is how you arrive at Kit with a flat CSV and no idea which contacts were in your welcome series.

- **Audit your Mailchimp account.** List every active automation, its trigger, and its branch logic. Screenshot each journey. Mailchimp does not export automation logic in any usable format, so screenshots are your source of truth.
- **Identify every segment.** Go to Audience > Segments and write down the segment name, the conditions, and the approximate contact count. You will export each one as a separate CSV.
- **Map your merge fields.** Open Settings > Audience fields and note every custom merge field beyond `FNAME`, `LNAME`, and `EMAIL`. You will create matching custom fields in Kit before importing.
- **Pause sequences that are mid-flight.** Any subscriber currently inside a Mailchimp automation journey should finish or be manually exited before you move them. Moving a contact mid-sequence creates duplicate touch problems.
- **Verify your sending domain.** You will re-authenticate SPF and DKIM inside Kit. Pull your current DNS records from your registrar now so you have them ready.
- **Grab your suppression list.** Export unsubscribes and bounces from Mailchimp. Kit calls them "cold subscribers." Suppress these on import to protect deliverability.

## What breaks and what does not transfer

Mailchimp and Kit have genuinely different data models. Some things port cleanly. Others need a workaround. I've mapped out the four problem areas that trip people up every time.

<SideBySide>
  <Fragment slot="left">
    **What breaks**

    - **Mailchimp groups.** Groups are not the same as segments. Kit has no groups concept. Map groups to tags manually, or collapse them into a custom field with a dropdown value.
    - **RSS-to-email campaigns.** Mailchimp's RSS automation is drag-and-drop. Kit's equivalent requires a custom RSS feed connected via [n8n](/go/n8n/) or Zapier that fires a broadcast via API. Not hard, but not automatic.
    - **Purchase event triggers from Mailchimp's e-commerce integrations.** If you used Mailchimp's WooCommerce or Shopify native connection to trigger automations on purchase, those integrations do not exist in Kit natively. You need a Zapier zap or an n8n workflow to push purchase events as tags into Kit.
    - **A/B test data.** Historical split test results stay in Mailchimp. Kit starts fresh.
  </Fragment>
  <Fragment slot="right">
    **n8n workflow: push Shopify purchase events to Kit as tags**

    ```json
    {
      "trigger": "Shopify: Order Paid webhook",
      "steps": [
        {
          "node": "HTTP Request",
          "method": "GET",
          "url": "https://api.kit.com/v4/subscribers",
          "params": { "email_address": "{{ $json.email }}" }
        },
        {
          "node": "HTTP Request",
          "method": "POST",
          "url": "https://api.kit.com/v4/tags/YOUR_TAG_ID/subscribers",
          "body": {
            "subscriber": {
              "email_address": "{{ $json.email }}"
            }
          }
        }
      ]
    }
    ```

    This pattern fires on every paid order, looks up the subscriber in Kit by email, and adds a product-purchase tag. The Kit sequence trigger watches for that tag. No native integration needed.
  </Fragment>
</SideBySide>

In my testing, the groups-to-tags mapping is the step that takes the longest. Budget an extra hour if you have more than five Mailchimp groups with overlapping membership.

## Verify it worked

Before you cancel Mailchimp, run through these four checks. Skip them and you will discover the problem six weeks later when a subscriber complains they stopped getting your newsletter.

1. **Subscriber count matches.** Pull total subscribers in Kit and compare against your Mailchimp export totals. A variance of more than 2 percent means a failed import row. Re-check that CSV for formatting errors.
2. **Tags are populated correctly.** Pick three contacts you know were in a specific Mailchimp segment. Confirm they have the corresponding Kit tag. If they do not, the import mapping failed for that file.
3. **Sequences fire on trigger.** Add a fresh test email address, submit it through your live opt-in form, and confirm the welcome sequence starts within five minutes. Check the sequence activity log in Kit to see each step fire.
4. **Custom fields render in emails.** Open a sent test email from Kit and confirm the merge values populated. Empty fields almost always mean the CSV column header did not match the custom field key exactly.

## The migration is the easy part

The hard part is deciding to do it. Once you have the CSVs exported, the custom fields mapped, and the sequences documented, the actual Kit setup takes less time than you expect. [Kit](/tools/kit/) is genuinely well-designed for creator and B2B content workflows, and the visual sequence builder makes Mailchimp's classic automation editor feel like it was designed in 2014 (because it was). Give yourself a week of parallel running, verify the four checks above, and then let Mailchimp's subscription lapse with confidence.
