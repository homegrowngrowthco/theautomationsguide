## What each tool actually does

Before comparing features side by side, be precise about the job each tool was designed to do. The category mismatch is where SDR teams waste budget.

JustCall is a cloud phone system built for outbound sales. It places calls, sends SMS, logs activity to your CRM, and layers in AI summaries and coaching scorecards on the AI plan. It is the thing your reps use every day to hit dial counts. Gong sits downstream: it ingests recordings from whatever dialer or conferencing tool you use, transcribes them, extracts patterns, and surfaces coaching recommendations to managers. Gong does not place a single call. It is a post-call analytics and coaching layer, full stop.

Aircall sits in the same category as JustCall but skews toward customer support and inside sales teams with more complex routing needs. Kixie overlaps heavily with JustCall but adds a true parallel dialer (up to 10 lines simultaneously) and native LinkedIn steps inside cadences. That matters for SDR teams running multichannel sequences.

## How to choose

The decision comes down to two questions: what stage is your team at, and does your manager have the bandwidth to act on conversation intelligence?

If your SDR team is under 15 reps, my read is that Gong is almost always premature. The platform's value compounds with volume. Pattern detection, deal risk flags, and rep benchmarking all require enough calls per week to make the signals statistically meaningful. A 10-rep team doing 50 dials a day is not generating the data density Gong needs to be genuinely useful. You are paying enterprise prices for a reporting layer that surfaces what a good manager would catch on a live call listen anyway.

JustCall's AI plan, at $49 per seat per month, covers transcription, AI summaries, sentiment tagging, and scoring rubrics. According to [JustCall's own feature documentation](https://justcall.io/features/ai-powered-call-transcription.html), those features are available on the AI plan without additional add-ons. That closes a meaningful share of the coaching gap at a fraction of Gong's cost.

For teams over 20 reps with a dedicated enablement function, Gong starts to pay for itself. The deal intelligence features (talk-to-listen ratio, topic tracking, competitor mentions) give managers a lever that manual call reviews cannot match at scale. At that point, running JustCall or Kixie as the dialer and piping recordings into Gong via integration is a legitimate stack. But you are now paying for two tools, so the math needs to clear. If you are also building out your cold email infrastructure, see our [best cold email stack under $300 post](/blog/2026-10-09-best-cold-email-stack-under-300-in-2026/) for how to keep the full outbound spend rational.

One overlooked option worth naming: [Nooks](/go/nooks/) and [Orum](/go/orum/) are purpose-built AI parallel dialers with some built-in coaching features that sit between JustCall and Gong on both capability and price. If pure dial-per-hour throughput is the bottleneck, those tools deserve a look before committing to Gong.

<MyTake>
The framing of "JustCall vs Gong" is almost always the wrong question. Gong is not a dialer upgrade. It is a coaching infrastructure investment, and buying it before you have the management bandwidth to run weekly call review cadences is just burning ARR. I'd argue most teams under 25 reps should lock in a solid dialer first and revisit Gong in 12 months when the volume and management processes are actually in place to justify it.
</MyTake>

## The bottom line for SDR teams in 2026

JustCall wins the "which dialer should my SDR team use" question cleanly for most teams under 20 reps. The AI plan covers the coaching basics, sequencing is native, CRM sync with [HubSpot](/tools/hubspot/) and [Salesforce](/tools/salesforce/) is solid, and the price-to-capability ratio is hard to beat at the early stage. Gong earns its seat once the team is large enough to make pattern analysis actionable and a manager has the time to run structured programs from the data. Kixie is the right pick if parallel dialing throughput and multichannel cadences are the primary bottleneck. Aircall fits blended teams that need clean routing and do not care about native sequencing. Pick the tool that solves the job you have today, not the one you might have in two years.
