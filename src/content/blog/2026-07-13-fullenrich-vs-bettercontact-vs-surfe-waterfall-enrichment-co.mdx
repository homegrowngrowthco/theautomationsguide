## What waterfall enrichment actually means in practice

Single-provider lookups are fine when your list is clean and your ICP maps neatly to whatever database you're paying for. The problem is that no single database covers everyone. Apollo is deep on US SaaS, thin on EMEA. Hunter is strong on domains, weak on mobile. Lusha has solid mobile coverage for enterprise contacts but real gaps in SMB. When you run a list of 1,000 LinkedIn URLs through one vendor, you're leaving 30 to 40 percent of your contacts unenriched, and those contacts just disappear from your sequence.

Waterfall enrichment fixes this by running a defined cascade. You set a priority order of providers. The tool queries provider one, and if it finds a verified email, it stops and charges you one credit. Miss, and it moves to provider two. You only pay on a hit. The result is a materially higher match rate at a cost per contact that's often lower than buying a premium single-source plan.

I've watched this play out firsthand. A 400-person target list I ran through FullEnrich came back with verified emails for 91 percent of contacts after the cascade touched four different providers. Apollo alone returned 63 percent on the same list. That delta is not a rounding error. It's the difference between a working sequence and a broken one.

This is also why tools like [Clay](/tools/clay/) have made enrichment waterfalls a core selling point, as the screenshot below shows. But Clay charges for the orchestration layer on top of enrichment credits, which is exactly where dedicated waterfall tools start to look attractive.

## How to choose

The decision comes down to two variables: volume and technical capacity. If you're processing 500-plus new contacts per week and have someone who can manage an API integration, FullEnrich gives you the best hit rates and the lowest long-run cost per contact. No-code stack, existing Clay or HubSpot footprint? BetterContact is the clear path, especially if you're already paying for Clay where the [built-in enrichment workflow](/blog/2026-07-05-why-your-revops-stack-grows-too-fast-in-2026/) compounds the value. Surfe answers a specific question: your SDRs are doing manual LinkedIn prospecting and you want enrichment to happen in the moment, not as a separate batch step. LeadMagic is for anyone who needs to validate a one-time list and doesn't want to commit to a monthly subscription just for that job.

One thing worth flagging: these tools are not mutually exclusive. Several clients I've worked with run Surfe for inbound demo request enrichment (fast, one-at-a-time) and FullEnrich for outbound list builds (high volume, API-driven). The marginal cost of running both is usually justified by the coverage gain. This also matters downstream when you think about [cold email deliverability](/blog/2026-07-10-cold-email-deliverability-is-breaking-in-2026/), since enriched, verified emails reduce the bounce rates that are actively getting domains flagged right now.

<MyTake>
Most teams treat waterfall enrichment as a Clay feature rather than a standalone strategy, and that framing is expensive. Clay's enrichment credits carry a premium because you're paying for the orchestration layer, not just the data. Running FullEnrich or BetterContact directly, then feeding verified contacts into Clay only for the logic steps, cuts enrichment cost by 30 to 50 percent in my experience without touching hit rates. If you're using Clay as your enrichment provider rather than your workflow engine, you're paying for the wrong thing.

## The right enrichment stack is a sequence, not a single tool

Waterfall enrichment as a category is early enough that most teams haven't built an intentional strategy around it. The four tools in this post cover the realistic range of use cases: high-volume API-driven builds with FullEnrich, no-code integrations with BetterContact, LinkedIn-native prospecting with [Surfe](/tools/surfe/), and lightweight one-off lookups with [LeadMagic](/tools/leadmagic/). Pick based on volume and technical capacity. Not on which tool has the flashiest marketing.

The teams winning outbound right now are treating enrichment as a cascading data quality problem, not a single-vendor checkbox. And if you're already running [AI SDR tooling](/blog/2026-07-07-best-ai-sdr-tools-in-2026-aisdr-vs-11x-vs-artisan/) on top of your contact lists, a high-quality waterfall enrichment layer underneath it is what separates a working outbound engine from an expensive experiment.
