Your website is getting traffic. You just do not know who. That gap is where visitor identification tools live, and the two names coming up most in RevOps Slack channels right now are [Leadfeeder](/go/leadfeeder/) and [RB2B](/go/rb2b/). Both drop a pixel on your site and tell you who showed up. But they operate at completely different levels of resolution, and they plug into your stack in fundamentally different ways. Pick the wrong one and you either over-engineer account scoring nobody acts on, or you drown your SDRs in unqualified LinkedIn profiles. Here is how I think through the decision.

## What each tool actually does

Before the comparison, a calibration. Visitor ID tools work by matching IP addresses and browser fingerprints against a data co-op of known business entities. The quality of that match depends entirely on the size and freshness of the vendor's graph. [Leadfeeder](/tools/leadfeeder/) (rebranded as Dealfront in some markets) has been doing this since 2012 and has a deep European data graph, which matters significantly if your accounts sit outside North America. [RB2B](/tools/rb2b/) is newer and US-centric, but it does something legacy tools do not: it maps the individual session to a named person via a proprietary identity graph and serves up a LinkedIn URL, not just a company domain.

In practice, Leadfeeder gives you: "Acme Corp visited your pricing page three times this week, here are the contacts HubSpot has on file for that account." RB2B gives you: "Jane Doe, VP of Engineering at Acme Corp, visited your pricing page 22 minutes ago, here is her LinkedIn." That is a meaningfully different handoff to your sales team. One feeds a process. The other triggers a phone call.

## Integrations and workflow fit

This is where the choice usually resolves itself. The tools wire into your stack differently, and that difference is the real deciding factor.

Leadfeeder has native integrations with [HubSpot](/go/hubspot/) and [Salesforce](/go/salesforce/) that run both directions. You can pull in CRM account data to filter and score visits, then push visit activity back as timeline events or workflow triggers. I have configured Leadfeeder so that when a named account visits the pricing or demo page, it creates a HubSpot task for the owning AE and bumps the deal score in the same step. That kind of account-based trigger is where Leadfeeder earns its keep. [Leadfeeder's web visitor tracking](/go/leadfeeder-web-visitors/) is the part of the product to set up first if you are running an account-based motion in HubSpot.

RB2B takes a different approach entirely. The core delivery mechanism is a Slack notification or a webhook. You get the person's name, LinkedIn URL, company, and pages visited. From there, you can route that payload into [Clay](/go/clay/) to enrich and qualify before it hits your SDR queue, or pipe it into a sequence tool like [Smartlead](/go/smartlead/) or [Instantly](/go/instantly/) for high-volume outbound. The lighter CRM integration is a real limitation if your AEs live in Salesforce. But for SDR-led outbound it is often faster than a fully integrated account-scoring system that nobody actually checks.

One workflow I keep recommending: RB2B fires a webhook to Clay, Clay scores the person against your ICP filters (title, company size, tech stack), and only the qualified profiles drop into a dedicated Slack channel or get added to an outbound sequence. That cuts the noise significantly. It also makes the free RB2B tier surprisingly powerful for early-stage teams who cannot justify a $15,000 intent data contract.

## How to choose

The honest answer is that these tools are not competing for the same workflow. Leadfeeder is infrastructure for an account-based GTM motion. It enriches your CRM, scores account engagement over time, and feeds AE alerts inside the tools they already use. It requires more setup and ongoing maintenance (keeping ICP filters current, mapping CRM owners, tuning scoring thresholds), but that investment pays off when you have a defined set of target accounts and reps who will actually act on account-level signals. If your AEs ignore the HubSpot activity feed, Leadfeeder's value evaporates fast.

RB2B is optimized for speed. If your sales motion is "identify someone warm and reach out today," the Slack-first, person-level approach is faster to deploy and faster to act on. The US-only identity graph is a real constraint if your ICP is international. The lighter CRM integration also means you are stitching together more of the workflow yourself. But for a lean SDR team running a high-velocity outbound motion against a domestic market, RB2B's free tier gets you 80% of the value of a much more expensive stack in under an hour.

<MyTake>
Most RevOps practitioners treat this as an either/or decision when it is actually a sequencing decision. Start with RB2B's free tier to validate that your site traffic is actually your ICP. If the profiles coming through match your target titles and company sizes, you have proof the audience is there. Then layer Leadfeeder on top for the account-level CRM plumbing. Combined spend is still a fraction of a single [6sense](/go/6sense/) or [ZoomInfo](/go/zoominfo/) seat, and you get sharper activation at both the account and person level. In my experience, skipping the validation step is why so many Leadfeeder implementations get abandoned after 90 days.
</MyTake>

## The bottom line

Visitor identification is only as valuable as the workflow it triggers. Leadfeeder wins when that workflow is "alert the AE and update the CRM account." RB2B wins when the workflow is "get a name and a LinkedIn URL in front of an SDR right now." Neither replaces a proper intent data platform for top-of-funnel discovery. Both are more useful than most teams realize when paired with a good enrichment layer like Clay. If you are starting from scratch and your ICP is domestic, the RB2B free tier is the easiest no-regret first move in the visitor ID category.
