## What each tool actually does

The framing that trips up most RevOps evaluations is treating Lusha and Saleshandy as equivalent. They are not. Lusha is a B2B contact intelligence platform. You use it to find verified emails, mobile numbers, and company firmographics, and to enrich records in your CRM. Saleshandy is a cold outreach platform. You use it to run multi-step email sequences, rotate sending domains, and track reply rates at scale.

[Prospeo](/go/prospeo/) and [Apollo](/go/apollo/) round out the realistic shortlist. Prospeo is a lean email finder that pulls from LinkedIn profiles and domain-level data. No phone numbers, no sequencing. Apollo is the all-in-one play: large database, built-in sequences, CRM-lite features. It's the closest thing to a single-tool answer, which is why I keep seeing it displace both Lusha and Saleshandy on budget-constrained teams, even though it underperforms each specialist in its respective core job.

Don't bother trying to force Saleshandy into a data-sourcing role. Its Prospect database is a secondary feature, and treating it as your primary list-building tool will burn credits on contacts that don't hold up at the send layer.

## How to choose

The real decision variable is where your outbound conversion is dropping. Getting 60%+ open rates but booking fewer meetings than the math says you should? Your data is the problem. Wrong contacts, stale emails, missing mobile numbers. Lusha fixes that. Emails landing in spam or reply rates sitting below 3% on a well-targeted list? Your sequencing and deliverability infrastructure is the problem. Saleshandy fixes that, and so would any dedicated sequencing platform worth its subscription. Starting from zero and can't afford two tools? Apollo is the pragmatic call.

I've watched this specific stack fail repeatedly: teams running [Saleshandy](/tools/saleshandy/) on top of lists sourced from [Apollo](/tools/apollo/), using Apollo as the data layer and Saleshandy as the delivery layer. It's reasonable in theory. In practice, the overlap cost adds up fast. You need to be honest about whether Saleshandy's sequencing is genuinely better than Apollo's native sequences for your motion, or whether you're just paying for a comfort blanket. For teams where [sequence software is leaking leads](/blog/2026-09-16-your-sales-sequence-software-is-leaking-leads-fix-it/), the answer is sometimes yes. Often it isn't.

## The bottom line on Lusha vs Saleshandy

These two tools solve adjacent but different problems. The right answer almost always comes down to stack position. Lusha belongs at the top of your data pipeline, feeding clean, verified contacts into whatever sequencer you run. Saleshandy belongs in the delivery layer, managing sending infrastructure and sequence logic. If budget forces a single choice, look hard at Apollo before committing to either specialist. And if you're purely email-first with no phone motion, [Prospeo](/tools/prospeo/) is worth a two-week trial before you pay for Lusha's broader feature set you may not use.
