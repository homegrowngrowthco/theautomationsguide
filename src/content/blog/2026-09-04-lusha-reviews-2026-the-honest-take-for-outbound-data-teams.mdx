## What Lusha actually does well

The Chrome extension is legitimately best in class for speed. When one of my clients was running manual LinkedIn prospecting, their SDRs were pulling contact data in under three seconds per profile. No other tool in this category matches that for high-touch, rep-driven prospecting. The UI is also refreshingly clean compared to Apollo's cluttered dashboard, which matters more than most RevOps folks want to admit when you're trying to drive SDR adoption.

Lusha also does a reasonable job on direct dials for mid-market and enterprise contacts in North America and Western Europe. On a 500-contact list we pulled for a SaaS client targeting VP-level buyers at 500-5,000 employee companies, we got a 71% valid mobile rate after running the list through [Bouncer](/go/bouncer/) for verification. That's workable for outbound. You're not going to hit those numbers on SMB contacts or APAC, though.

The CRM integrations with HubSpot, Salesforce, and Pipedrive are solid and bidirectional. Enrichment sync works reliably, which is more than I can say for some of the cheaper tools in this space. If your team lives inside a CRM and wants to enrich records without leaving it, Lusha handles this cleanly.

## Where it starts to fall apart

The credit model is where Lusha quietly becomes expensive. The entry Pro plan at roughly $59/user/month gives you credits that are, by default, per-seat rather than pooled. If you have a team of four SDRs but two of them are the heavy prospectors, the other two seats are essentially wasted budget. [Apollo](/tools/apollo/) pools credits across the team from its basic paid tier. That single difference changes the unit economics significantly for teams with uneven prospecting load.

The database is the bigger problem at scale. At 36 million company records, with a contact database Lusha does not publicly size with any specificity, you will hit coverage gaps faster than with Apollo or even [ZoomInfo](/go/zoominfo/). I ran a 2,000-record prospect list through both Lusha and Apollo for a client in the legal tech vertical and Apollo returned enriched data on 23% more contacts. That gap compounds fast when you're building large TAM lists.

There's also no sequencing. Lusha is a pure data layer. You pull contacts, export them to your sequencer of choice (Smartlead, Instantly, Lemlist, whatever), and build from there. That's not inherently a flaw, but Apollo bundles sequencing and you need to weigh whether the tool consolidation is worth it for your team's workflow.

As I wrote in the [Lemlist Review 2026](/blog/2026-08-31-lemlist-review-2026-the-honest-take-for-b2b-outbound-teams/), the trend in outbound tooling is collapsing the data-to-sequence stack into fewer tools. Lusha is swimming against that current.

## How to choose

If your team is rep-led, does high-touch LinkedIn prospecting, and lives inside HubSpot or Salesforce, Lusha is a defensible choice. The extension speed and CRM sync genuinely reduce rep friction, and that has real adoption value. I've seen teams burn through Apollo credits because the tool was too complex and SDRs stopped using it entirely, making the cheaper, simpler Lusha actually deliver better ROI in practice. Adoption rate is a real metric.

If you're building any kind of automated enrichment workflow, running [Clay](/go/clay/) waterfalls, or pushing large list volumes, Lusha's credit model and database ceiling will frustrate you within sixty days. [Apollo](/tools/apollo/) is the better default for outbound teams that want a single tool. [LeadMagic](/tools/leadmagic/) is the better default if you're already enriching inside Clay and want to keep costs variable. [Prospeo](/tools/prospeo/) wins if Sales Navigator list export is the core motion and phone data is not a requirement.

According to [G2's contact data category benchmarks](https://www.g2.com/categories/sales-intelligence), accuracy and database coverage are the top two buying criteria for sales intelligence tools, above pricing. That ranking matches what I hear from clients. Start with a head-to-head coverage test on your own ICP before committing to any of these platforms. Pull the same 200-record list through Lusha's trial and Apollo's free tier and compare fill rates against your target segment. The results in your specific vertical will tell you more than any review, including this one.

## The bottom line

Lusha is not broken. It's well-built, the CRM integrations are reliable, and the Chrome extension is the fastest path from LinkedIn profile to dialed contact in the market. But the credit model punishes small teams with uneven usage, the database is materially smaller than Apollo's, and there's no sequencing layer to consolidate your stack.

Those are meaningful gaps for outbound teams building in 2026. Most clients I work with that evaluate Lusha honestly end up on Apollo or a Prospeo-plus-sequencer combination. They do not look back.
