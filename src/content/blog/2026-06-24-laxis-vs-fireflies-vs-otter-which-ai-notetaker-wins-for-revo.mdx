The AI notetaker category is genuinely crowded right now, and most head-to-heads treat all three of these tools as interchangeable. They are not. ## What separates these three tools in practice

Most reviews compare feature checklists. What I actually care about is the 15-minute window after a call ends: does the right information get into the right CRM field without a rep touching it? That is where these tools diverge sharply.

Don't evaluate transcript accuracy first. Evaluate whether the tool produces a structured artifact your CRM can consume without a human in the middle. That single question eliminates a lot of noise.

## How to choose

If your primary job is keeping CRM records accurate and giving AEs their time back, [Laxis](/go/laxis) is the only tool in this group designed for that specific outcome. The structured summaries map to deal stages. The follow-up drafts save real keystrokes. The CRM sync is two-way, not a one-way dump. The cost per seat looks higher on paper, but it consistently replaces 20 to 30 minutes of manual CRM entry per rep per day, and that is where the ROI math closes fast.

If you are running a mixed GTM and ops team, or you need call data flowing into five different downstream systems, [Fireflies](/go/fireflies) gives you more surface area to automate. Its [native API](https://docs.fireflies.ai/api-overview) and broad integration library make it the better choice when no single CRM owns the workflow. My clients who run CS, product, and sales calls through the same notetaker almost always end up here.

Otter.ai makes sense if budget is the hard constraint or your team simply needs a reliable place for meeting notes with no automation ambitions. But I would not build a RevOps workflow around it. The [HubSpot](/tools/hubspot/) integration is too shallow, and there is no path to Salesforce field-level mapping without exporting manually.

<MyTake>
Transcript accuracy is the wrong thing to optimize for. At 90-plus percent on clear audio, all three tools are good enough, and agonizing over the gap is a distraction. The real question is whether the tool generates a structured artifact your CRM can consume without a human in the middle. Laxis answers yes by default. Fireflies answers yes if your RevOps team builds the logic. Otter answers no. That hierarchy is why I almost always start revenue teams on Laxis despite the higher sticker price, and why I stop recommending Otter the moment a team has more than one AE.
</MyTake>

## The category is maturing faster than most teams are moving

The AI notetaker space has shifted from "does it transcribe well" to "does it close the loop on deal data." Full stop. [G2's conversational intelligence grid](https://www.g2.com/categories/conversation-intelligence) shows Fireflies and Laxis both gaining enterprise traction while Otter.ai remains dominant in education and SMB segments. For RevOps practitioners, that means the evaluation criteria should weight CRM integration depth, post-call automation flexibility, and AI summary structure far above raw transcription quality.

All three tools solve the "I need notes from this call" problem. Only Laxis and, to a lesser degree, Fireflies solve the "I need this call to advance my pipeline" problem. Those are different products serving different outcomes. [Salesforce's State of Sales research](https://www.salesforce.com/resources/research-reports/state-of-sales/) puts manual CRM entry as the top time drain for reps, which means the notetaker that eliminates that step is worth considerably more than the one that produces the cleanest transcript.
