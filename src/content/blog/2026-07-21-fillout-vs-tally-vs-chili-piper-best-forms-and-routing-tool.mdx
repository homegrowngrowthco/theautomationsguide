## What each tool actually does (and does not do)

Be precise here, because the marketing overlaps in ways that create real confusion. [Chili Piper](/go/chili-piper/) is not a form builder. It is a routing and scheduling layer that sits on top of your existing form, whether that is a HubSpot form, a Marketo form, or anything that fires a webhook on submit. [Tally](/go/tally/) is a form builder with strong UX and a generous free tier, but it has no native scheduling or routing logic. [Fillout](/go/fillout/) is the only one of the three that spans the full workflow from field capture through calendar booking.

I have run the Chili Piper plus HubSpot forms stack at two clients, and the integration maintenance is real. When HubSpot changes their embed behavior or Chili Piper updates their script, you get a broken booking flow on your highest-intent page at 2am on a Thursday. That operational fragility is exactly what makes Fillout's all-in-one approach genuinely compelling. My strong opinion: if you are spending more than two hours a month maintaining glue between your form tool and your scheduling tool, you are paying an invisible tax that never shows up on a vendor invoice.

## How to choose

The decision comes down to team size, routing complexity, and whether you want a consolidated tool or best-of-breed specialists.

Series A or Series B, fewer than 30 quota-carrying reps, routing logic that is territory or round-robin based: Fillout covers your needs at a fraction of the cost. Post-Series C, dedicated RevOps team, 50-plus reps, complex Salesforce routing built on custom object relationships, enterprise SLAs: Chili Piper's routing engine depth is still justified. The threshold I use with clients is roughly 35 seats. Below that, I have never seen a routing requirements document that Fillout could not handle.

Tally sits outside this decision entirely for pure inbound lead capture. It is the right answer for the other 80 percent of forms your GTM team builds: event registrations, customer feedback, partner intake, SDR coaching request forms, internal tooling. I keep Tally in the stack even at clients running Fillout for their demo request flows. Different jobs, different tools.

[Calendly](/go/calendly/) is the fourth player teams often ask about here. Calendly with routing handles the scheduling layer well but still requires a separate form builder, putting it closer to the Chili Piper architecture than the Fillout all-in-one model. If you are already deep in the [Salesforce](/tools/salesforce/) or [HubSpot](/tools/hubspot/) ecosystems, check the native routing features in those platforms before adding another vendor. Both have improved meaningfully on this front in 2025, and neither gets enough credit for it in these comparisons.

## The consolidation case is real

The forms and routing category is fragmenting in Fillout's favor right now. The tool has moved faster on CRM integrations and native scheduling than any competitor in the space. It also has almost no direct comparison coverage yet, which means the teams winning with it found it through RevOps Slack communities and word of mouth rather than vendor marketing. That information asymmetry is temporary.

If your current stack has a separate form tool, a separate scheduling tool, and a Zapier layer connecting them to your CRM, run the math on [Fillout](/tools/fillout/) before your next renewal cycle. Every team I have walked through this exercise finds they can cut $200 to $600 per month and eliminate a meaningful number of integration failure points at the same time. That is not a small thing when you are debugging a broken Zap at 9pm before a campaign launch.
