## What each tool is actually doing

These four tools are solving different problems. Comparing them on a single match-rate number misses the point entirely. [LeadMagic](/go/leadmagic/) is a pure API-first enrichment tool: you send it a LinkedIn URL (or email), you get back a verified business email and sometimes a mobile. The API is clean, the docs are solid, and the Clay integration works without hand-holding. I've used it as a first-pass enrichment step in waterfall tables before falling back to [Prospeo](/go/prospeo/) or Apollo.

[Surfe](/go/surfe/) is categorically different. It's a Chrome extension that sits on top of LinkedIn and syncs prospect data directly into your CRM (HubSpot, Salesforce, Pipedrive) as reps browse. If you're doing bulk enrichment in a table, Surfe is the wrong tool, full stop. But if your AEs manually prospect in Sales Navigator and you want that activity to land in the CRM without CSV exports, Surfe is the best solution I've seen for that specific workflow.

[FullEnrich](/go/fullenrich/) and [BetterContact](/go/bettercontact/) are both waterfall aggregators, meaning they hit multiple data providers in sequence and return the best result. The practical difference: [FullEnrich](/tools/fullenrich/) prioritizes email coverage across a broad ICP, while [BetterContact](/tools/bettercontact/) is clearly optimized for mobile number retrieval. I've seen BetterContact return mobile numbers on records where FullEnrich came back empty. The reverse is much less common.

## How to choose

The core decision splits on two axes: workflow type and data priority. If you're running bulk enrichment through an automation pipeline (Clay, n8n, custom API), you're choosing between LeadMagic, FullEnrich, and BetterContact. If you're trying to improve rep-level CRM hygiene without changing anyone's workflow, Surfe is the only serious option. The others don't compete in that use case at all.

Within the API group, it comes down to what you're optimizing for. LeadMagic wins on simplicity and cost at low volumes, under 2,000 records a month, where a single-source tool is genuinely good enough. Above that threshold, or when working with EU or APAC records where US-centric databases fall apart, FullEnrich's waterfall coverage makes the per-credit premium worth paying. BetterContact is not an either/or against FullEnrich. I've run them in sequence, using FullEnrich for email and BetterContact for mobile on the same record set, and the combined match rates across both channels were materially better than either tool alone. If your [RevOps stack cost per rep](/blog/2026-08-06-revops-stack-cost-per-rep-at-10-reps-2026-breakdown/) can absorb two enrichment vendors at modest volume, that's the configuration I'd recommend for phone-first outbound teams.

<MyTake>
Most teams I see use LeadMagic as a one-stop enrichment solution when they should be treating it as a first-pass layer in a waterfall. Running LeadMagic first and routing misses to FullEnrich cuts your effective cost per match without sacrificing coverage. Single-source enrichment above 1,000 records a month is almost always a false economy, and I've never seen a team regret adding the fallback step once they run the numbers.
</MyTake>

## Fitting enrichment into your actual stack

The enrichment tool question doesn't live in isolation. If you're already [running Clay connected to n8n for a fully automated enrichment pipeline](/blog/2026-07-31-connect-clay-to-n8n-for-a-fully-automated-enrichment-pipelin/), dropping LeadMagic or FullEnrich in as a waterfall step takes about 20 minutes. Both have Clay-native integrations that handle API key auth and credit tracking without custom HTTP nodes. BetterContact is slightly more manual to configure in Clay but works fine with the HTTP request block.

For teams building in [n8n](/tools/n8n/) specifically, LeadMagic's clean REST API is friendlier to work with than FullEnrich's more complex waterfall response schema. The tradeoff: you're managing fallback logic yourself in n8n rather than letting FullEnrich's internal waterfall handle it. If you want to go deeper on n8n enrichment orchestration, the [Attio n8n CRM enrichment workflow guide](/blog/2026-08-04-attio-n8n-crm-enrichment-workflow-2026-guide/) covers the sequencing logic in detail.

One underrated use case for [LeadMagic](/tools/leadmagic/): enriching inbound leads before they hit your CRM. A webhook from your form tool fires to n8n, LeadMagic appends the LinkedIn data, and the record lands in HubSpot fully enriched. No rep touches it, no manual lookup, and the whole flow runs in under 10 seconds. I've built this for several clients and it consistently delivers among the highest ROI of any automation in a modern GTM stack.

For [Surfe](/tools/surfe/), the natural integration surface is [Pipedrive](/tools/pipedrive/) or HubSpot, and Surfe has native integrations for both. Setup is a Chrome extension install and an OAuth connection to your CRM. You can have reps live on it in an afternoon. No IT ticket required.

According to [G2's data enrichment category benchmarks](https://www.g2.com/categories/data-enrichment), the top reasons teams switch enrichment tools are match rate dissatisfaction and integration complexity, not price. That tracks precisely with what I see in the field: teams consistently underestimate how much a 15-percentage-point improvement in email match rate compounds when you're sending 5,000 records a month through an outbound sequence.

Read [LeadMagic's API documentation](https://www.leadmagic.io/docs) carefully before committing, specifically the section on credit deduction rules. Some lookups that return no match still consume a credit depending on the endpoint. That affects your true cost per successful enrichment more than the headline per-credit price suggests.

## The verdict on LeadMagic in 2026

LeadMagic is a genuinely good tool in a category full of vendors that oversell and underdeliver. The API is clean, the Clay integration works out of the box, and the pay-as-you-go model is the right pricing structure for ops teams who hate annual enrichment contracts. Where it falls short is straightforward: it is one source. In 2026, the teams getting the best outreach outcomes are running waterfall enrichment, not single-source lookups. Use LeadMagic as your first-pass layer. Route misses to FullEnrich or BetterContact depending on whether you need email or mobile coverage. That combination will outperform any single tool in this comparison at realistic outbound volumes.
