The question I get from RevOps leads evaluating meeting intelligence tools is almost never "is Laxis good?" It's "do I actually need to pay for this, or does the free Fathom tier cover my team?" The answer depends entirely on what your reps do after a call. If they need action items pushed into Salesforce, deal summaries surfaced in a CRM, or managers reviewing call snippets, free Fathom is not enough. If they just need an accurate transcript and a summary in their inbox, you may be paying for features you will never open.

## What Laxis actually costs at each tier

[Laxis](/go/laxis/) publishes its pricing directly on the [Laxis pricing page](https://laxis.com/pricing) with four tiers. Here is what those tiers mean operationally, not just what the vendor lists.

**Free:** Capped AI meeting summaries. The cap is low enough that an active AE will blow through it in the first week of a good pipeline month. No integrations, no CRM sync. Worth trying for a week, but do not build a workflow on it.

**Basic at $16.99 per seat per month:** Removes the summary cap, adds Zoom and Google Meet bot support, and gives you basic email action item export. The CRM integration here is surface-level, specifically an email-to-CRM manual log rather than an automatic write-back. If your reps manually paste notes into HubSpot or Pipedrive anyway, this tier might hold.

**Pro at $39.99 per seat per month:** This is where most revenue teams land. You get native HubSpot and Salesforce sync (read the fine print: Salesforce write-back is documented as Pro-eligible, but [G2 reviewers](https://www.g2.com/products/laxis/reviews) consistently flag that two-way Salesforce sync requires Business), keyword and topic tracking, and AI-generated CRM field population. That last feature is the main reason to buy Laxis over a free recorder.

**Business at $59.99 per seat per month:** Adds team-level analytics, manager dashboards, deal risk signals, and confirmed two-way Salesforce sync. If you are coming from an [Avoma](/go/avoma/) evaluation and want comparable conversation intelligence, this is the tier that actually competes.

## The true cost: real math for a 10-rep team

Say you have 10 AEs running 8 to 12 discovery and demo calls per week. Here is what each tool costs at that scale.

**Laxis Pro, 10 seats:** $39.99 x 10 = $399.90 per month ($4,799 per year). If you need Salesforce two-way sync and discover mid-quarter that it requires Business, you are looking at $599.90 per month instead.

**Laxis Business, 10 seats:** $59.99 x 10 = $599.90 per month ($7,199 per year).

**Fathom Team Edition, 10 seats:** $29 x 10 = $290 per month. HubSpot and Salesforce sync is included per [Fathom's pricing page](https://fathom.video/pricing). Fathom is the cheapest path to a fully synced HubSpot setup for a team this size.

**Avoma Growth, 10 seats:** $59 x 10 = $590 per month. The [Avoma review](/blog/2026-11-21-avoma-review-2026-ai-meeting-intelligence-for-revenue-teams/) I covered earlier is worth reading if you are deciding between Avoma and Laxis Business specifically, since they overlap heavily at this price point.

The arithmetic that catches teams: Laxis Pro looks $20 cheaper per seat than Avoma Growth. But if you need revenue analytics, scorecards, or confirmed two-way Salesforce write-back, Laxis Business erases that gap entirely. You end up at the same monthly spend with less conversation intelligence. That is a bad trade.

## When Fathom is genuinely free enough

[Fathom's](/go/fathom/) free tier is the most honest free plan in the meeting intelligence category. Unlimited recordings, unlimited AI summaries, no seat cap, no meeting cap. The constraint is entirely in the direction of team-level features. You cannot share call libraries, you cannot give a manager a view across all rep calls, and you cannot push structured data into your CRM automatically.

My read is that the free tier covers about 60 to 70 percent of what individual reps actually use day-to-day. The gap shows up the moment a sales manager wants visibility or a RevOps lead wants to build a workflow on top of call data. That is where you either pay for Fathom Team Edition ($29 per seat) or start taking Laxis Pro seriously.

One thing to know: if your stack already includes [Gong](/go/gong/) for your enterprise segment and you are adding a notetaker for a smaller pod or a CS team, Fathom Free stacked with a lightweight Zapier push to your CRM is a legitimate zero-cost setup. I would not build a full RevOps motion on it, but for a low-call-volume team it clears the bar.

The Fathom vs Laxis decision mirrors the pattern from the [folk CRM pricing analysis](/blog/2026-11-18-folk-crm-pricing-in-2026-plans-seat-costs-and-when-salesflar/): the free tier looks great until you need the integrations that justify the tool category, and then you are paying anyway.

## The meeting intelligence pricing picture

With [Avoma](/tools/avoma/) at $59 per seat on Growth, [Laxis](/tools/laxis/) spanning $17 to $60 depending on CRM needs, and [Fathom](/tools/fathom/) genuinely free for individuals, the category has a clear three-tier structure. Free solo use. $29 to $40 per seat for small team CRM sync. $59 per seat for revenue analytics and conversation intelligence. The mistake most teams make is buying the middle tier assuming it includes the top-tier integrations. Read the Salesforce fine print before you sign an annual contract on any of these tools.
