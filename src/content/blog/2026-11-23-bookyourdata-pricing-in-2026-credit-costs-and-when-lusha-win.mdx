The question that lands in my inbox more than any other from RevOps leads running data teams is some version of: "We're burning through Lusha credits faster than budget allows. What actually costs less per record?" [BookYourData](/go/bookyourdata/) is usually the first place I point. But the pricing structure is different enough from seat-based tools that teams consistently miscalculate what they will actually spend. This post lays out the real math, where BookYourData stacks up against [Lusha](/go/lusha/) and [UpLead](/go/uplead/), and the specific team profiles where paying the Lusha premium still makes sense.

## What BookYourData actually charges

BookYourData is a pure data vendor. You buy credits, each credit unlocks one record (email plus available firmographic fields), and credits do not expire on annual plans. No per-seat fee. No minimum user count. No platform upsell hiding behind the data.

Per [BookYourData's published pricing](https://www.bookyourdata.com/pricing), the structure as of late 2026 runs roughly like this:

The credit model rewards volume. If your data team is pulling 5,000 to 10,000 records a month for outbound sequences in [Smartlead](/go/smartlead/) or [Instantly](/go/instantly/), BookYourData's cost per record stays well below what Lusha charges for equivalent contact volume on a mid-tier plan.

The honest catch: BookYourData's strength is email lists for outbound. Phone number coverage is thinner compared to Lusha. Enrichment depth (technographics, intent signals) does not match what you get from [ZoomInfo](/go/zoominfo/) or [Cognism](/go/cognism/) at the enterprise tier. You are buying clean, deliverable emails at scale, not an all-in-one intelligence platform. I'd argue that is a feature, not a gap, for the right team.

## The true cost for a 20-person data team

Here is the actual arithmetic for the team size this question comes up around most often. Say a 20-person GTM team includes four people actively pulling lists: an ops lead, two SDRs doing their own prospecting, and a demand gen manager building ad audiences.

**BookYourData scenario:**
- Monthly pull: 8,000 records
- Bulk credit rate: $0.10 per record
- Monthly cost: $800
- Annual cost: $9,600
- No seat fees. No per-user charges.

**Lusha scenario ([per Lusha's pricing page](https://www.lusha.com/pricing/)):**
- Growth plan: approximately $80 per seat per month billed annually
- Four active seats: $320 per month base
- Lusha credits on Growth: roughly 480 credits per seat per month (varies by negotiation)
- 4 seats x 480 credits = 1,920 credits per month
- To reach 8,000 records, the team buys add-on credit packs, which at Lusha's overage rate pushes well past $1,000 per month total
- Annual cost at that volume: $12,000 to $15,000, before overage

The gap widens fast when volume goes up. At 15,000 records per month, BookYourData stays near $1,500. Lusha needs either an enterprise negotiation or significant add-on spend to match that volume, and even then the per-record math rarely beats $0.10.

This is the core reason I see teams move list-building to BookYourData while keeping a small Lusha footprint for in-workflow enrichment, specifically for the browser extension and HubSpot or Salesforce native sync. The jobs are different and the pricing reflects that.

[UpLead](/tools/uplead/) sits in an interesting middle position. It charges a seat fee starting around $99 per month for a single seat with 170 credits, which makes it expensive on a per-record basis for high-volume pulls. But real-time verification at export is a meaningful differentiator. When you are building a list you plan to run cold in [Quickmail](/go/quickmail/) or Smartlead the same week, reducing bounce rate at the source matters for deliverability. That is a different risk posture than buying bulk credits from BookYourData and running them through a waterfall verifier separately, as covered in the [Moltsets vs Fullenrich enrichment comparison](/blog/2026-11-19-moltsets-vs-fullenrich-which-covers-more-leads/).

## The add-on that surprises most buyers

BookYourData's accuracy guarantee is real but conditional. Replacement credits for bounced emails are issued against their defined bounce threshold, typically anything over three percent on a given pull. This means if your list is a niche slice (very small company size, hyper-specific geography), accuracy may dip below the guarantee threshold and replacement credits cover only the overage, not the full batch.

The practical fix is running BookYourData output through a lightweight verification pass in [Bouncer](/go/bouncer/) or [ZeroBounce](/go/zerobounce/) before loading sequences. Per published rates from both tools, this adds roughly $0.003 to $0.005 per record, which still keeps total cost under $0.11 per verified contact at bulk rates. That math holds up comfortably against Lusha's per-credit pricing.

For teams already using [Clay](/go/clay/) for enrichment waterfalls, this verification step plugs in cleanly as a column before the sequence push. The [Clay enrichment waterfall approach](/blog/2026-11-19-moltsets-vs-fullenrich-which-covers-more-leads/) applies directly here: BookYourData as the primary email source, a verifier as a gate, then sequence enrollment.

## What you are not getting

My read is that the missing piece most teams discover post-purchase is intent data. BookYourData gives you contact and firmographic fields. No buying signal layer. If your outbound motion depends on triggering off job change signals, funding rounds, or technology install events, you will need a separate enrichment source, whether that is [Apollo](/go/apollo/) for its intent filters or a signal tool like [Trigify](/go/trigify/) feeding into your sequence logic.

[Lusha](/tools/lusha/) does not solve this natively either, but it has tighter integrations with the LinkedIn workflow where reps catch intent organically. UpLead's technographic filters get you partway there on company-level signals, but it is not a replacement for a purpose-built intent layer. Don't bother trying to make BookYourData do this job. It was not built for it, and the workarounds cost more than just buying the right tool.
