## How to choose the right Mailchimp alternative

Most people migrating from Mailchimp fall into one of two buckets. The first is the creator or small operator who wants their email list to be a real business asset: segmented, automatable, connected to a product or community, and eventually monetizable. The second is someone who just wants cheaper, cleaner email broadcasts without the friction Mailchimp keeps building into its pricing tiers. Those two needs lead to different answers, and conflating them is how you end up on the wrong platform for 18 months.

If you are in the first bucket, do not bother evaluating MailerLite or [Substack](/go/substack) seriously. They solve simpler problems. The real decision is [Kit](/go/kit) versus [Beehiiv](/go/beehiiv), and it comes down to one question: is your primary surface email automation, or is it a public newsletter with built-in growth mechanics? Per [Kit's own product documentation](https://help.kit.com/en/articles/2502580-getting-started-with-automations), the sequence builder supports nested conditional branches that Mailchimp's Customer Journeys still cannot replicate cleanly. That matters if your automations involve purchase triggers, tag-based branching, or anything more than a basic welcome sequence.

<MyTake>
Most creators I talk to underestimate how much Mailchimp's list-based model is actively working against them. Managing subscribers as lists instead of tagged individuals means you pay for duplicates, accumulate segmentation debt, and end up with automation logic that collapses the moment you add a second offer or content type. Kit's tag-based model is not a UX preference. It is a fundamentally better data architecture for anyone running more than one product or audience segment. I have seen clients cut their effective contact count by 30% on day one of migration, just from deduplication that Mailchimp's list model had been hiding for years.
</MyTake>

## Make the move before your next billing cycle

The real cost of staying on Mailchimp is not the monthly fee. It is the automation you are not building because the tool makes it tedious, the segmentation you are skipping because the list model fights you at every step, and the monetization you are leaving on the table because there is no native path for digital products or paid subscriptions. [Litmus's 2024 State of Email report](https://www.litmus.com/resources/state-of-email-report) found that triggered and automated emails generate 3x the revenue per message of standard broadcasts. If your automation is broken or nonexistent, that gap compounds every week.

For creators and small operators sending more than a newsletter, [Kit](/go/kit) is where I send clients first. The free tier supports up to 10,000 subscribers with send limits, which gives you enough runway to prove the migration before you spend a dollar. Start there. Rebuild your top one or two automations in the first week using Kit's visual sequence builder. Tag your existing list by lead source or content interest before you do anything else. You will be operating at a higher level than you ever did on Mailchimp inside of a month.
