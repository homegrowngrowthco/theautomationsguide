## What each tool actually does

These three are not interchangeable. I've watched teams buy Warmforge expecting a full sending stack and then scramble when they realize they still need to connect Smartlead or [Instantly](/go/instantly/) on top. Get the mental model right first.

[Maildoso](/go/maildoso/) is a mailbox provisioning and hosting service. You buy domains and mailboxes through the platform, DNS records get configured automatically, and you pull the SMTP credentials into whatever sender you prefer. The pitch is price and speed: you can spin up 50 mailboxes across 15 domains in under an hour. The tradeoff is that the interface is minimal and domain management feels barebones if you are running complex multi-client operations.

[Mailforge](/go/mailforge/) covers the same provisioning surface but wraps it in more structured account and domain grouping. Agencies running five or ten client outbound programs simultaneously will feel the difference. You can segment domains by client, see health status across the whole account, and manage billing per workspace. It costs more per mailbox than Maildoso, but the organizational overhead it removes is real. I've seen this matter enormously at clients where one RevOps person is managing outbound infrastructure for multiple product lines.

[Warmforge](/go/warmforge/) sits in a different category entirely. It is a warming network and deliverability monitoring tool. You connect your existing mailboxes (from Maildoso, Mailforge, Google Workspace, wherever) and Warmforge runs automated warm-up sends between accounts in its network, tracks spam placement rates, and surfaces reputation alerts. It is not a sender and it does not provision mailboxes. Think of it as ongoing maintenance, not foundation. Worth pairing with either provisioning tool above, particularly as your domain ages into high-volume sends. For context on why this matters right now, see our piece on [cold email deliverability breaking in 2026](/blog/2026-07-10-cold-email-deliverability-is-breaking-in-2026/).

## How to choose

The decision here is not really Maildoso vs. Mailforge vs. Warmforge in a pure head-to-head. It is about understanding which layer of your infrastructure each tool covers. Maildoso and Mailforge compete directly on mailbox provisioning. Warmforge is almost always additive to whichever provisioning tool you choose.

My strong opinion: most solo operators and early-stage teams have no business paying Mailforge prices. Maildoso at $3 to $4 per mailbox gets you to 50 mailboxes for what Mailforge charges for 25. Save the premium for when client billing complexity actually justifies it.

In either provisioning case, if you are running 30 or more mailboxes and deliverability is a meaningful business risk, adding Warmforge (or a comparable tool like [Mailreach](/tools/mailreach/)) as a monitoring and warming layer is worth the monthly cost. I have seen teams lose entire domain pools to blacklisting because they had no early warning on reputation degradation. The cost of replacing burned domains and rewarming from scratch dwarfs a $50/month monitoring subscription.

A note on sequencing senders: neither Maildoso nor Mailforge includes a sequencing engine. You still need [Smartlead](/go/smartlead/), Instantly, [Lemlist](/go/lemlist/), or [Quickmail](/go/quickmail/) on top to actually run campaigns. If you are also evaluating AI SDR tools to automate the prospecting layer above this infrastructure, our [AI SDR comparison](/blog/2026-07-07-best-ai-sdr-tools-in-2026-aisdr-vs-11x-vs-artisan/) covers that ground.

## The bottom line

Cold email infrastructure is not glamorous, but it is the reason outbound either compounds or collapses. [Maildoso](/tools/maildoso/) wins on cost per mailbox and speed of provisioning. [Mailforge](/tools/mailforge/) wins on organizational structure for multi-client operations. [Warmforge](/tools/warmforge/) is not a competitor to either. It is the ongoing health layer you run on top of both. For most B2B SaaS teams running outbound in-house, Maildoso plus Warmforge plus Smartlead is the stack I would stand up today. For agencies, swap Maildoso for Mailforge and keep the rest.
