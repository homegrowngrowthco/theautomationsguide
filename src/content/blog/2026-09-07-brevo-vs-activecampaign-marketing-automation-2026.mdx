## Where each tool actually lives in your stack

Before comparing features line by line, it helps to understand the operating philosophy behind each product. They were built for different buyers, and that origin shapes every tradeoff.

[ActiveCampaign](/tools/activecampaign/) started as an email automation tool for SMBs and grew upmarket by bolting on a CRM, site tracking, predictive analytics, and a massive integration layer. The result is a genuinely capable marketing automation platform. If you're willing to invest in the setup, it can run sophisticated multi-channel sequences triggered by CRM deal stage, lead score thresholds, and behavioral signals. I've seen it used well at companies running 15-step onboarding automations with conditional branches tied to product usage data piped in via Zapier. That build took about three weeks to configure and debug. It held up.

[Brevo](/tools/brevo/) (formerly Sendinblue) took a different path. It expanded from transactional email into marketing automation, then added a lightweight CRM layer later. The product feels tighter and less configurable, which is either a bug or a feature depending on your ops maturity. Where it genuinely competes is on deliverability infrastructure, pricing fairness, and the combination of transactional plus marketing email under one roof. If your team ships product lifecycle emails (trial expiry, onboarding drips, billing notifications) alongside marketing campaigns, Brevo handles both without a separate SendGrid or Postmark account. That alone justifies the evaluation for most SaaS teams I work with.

## How to choose

The honest version of this decision comes down to two variables: how complex your automation logic actually needs to be, and how many contacts you're storing versus how many emails you're actually sending.

If your contact database is large but your send frequency is moderate (a 100k-contact list sending two to four campaigns per month plus behavioral triggers), Brevo's economics are dramatically better. ActiveCampaign charges for contact storage regardless of whether those contacts receive anything. A 100k-contact database on ActiveCampaign Professional runs over $1,000 per month. Brevo's Business plan at equivalent send volume can come in under $400. That delta compounds fast when you're also paying for enrichment tools like [Clay](/go/clay/) or [Apollo](/go/apollo/) to keep the list healthy.

Where I'd push back on defaulting to Brevo is when the team has real automation complexity requirements: conditional probability branching, predictive send-time optimization, or deep CRM pipeline integration tied to deal stages. ActiveCampaign's automation builder is genuinely more capable in those scenarios. [According to G2's 2026 Marketing Automation Grid](https://www.g2.com/categories/marketing-automation), it holds a "Leader" position with a significantly higher satisfaction score on automation flexibility than Brevo. For teams running GTM motions where CRM state drives nurture logic, that gap is real.

Stack context matters too. ActiveCampaign's 900-plus native integrations include [Salesforce](/go/salesforce/), [Pipedrive](/go/pipedrive/), Intercom, and Typeform with two-way sync. If you're already deep in that ecosystem and want automation that reacts to CRM events without a middleware layer, ActiveCampaign earns its premium. If you're running [n8n](/go/n8n/) or [Zapier](/go/zapier/) to wire everything together anyway, Brevo's thinner native integration set matters a lot less.

## The deliverability variable nobody talks about

One area that doesn't get enough comparison coverage: deliverability infrastructure. ActiveCampaign has invested heavily in dedicated IP options and reputation monitoring, and their [deliverability documentation](https://help.activecampaign.com/hc/en-us/articles/360000550266-Dedicated-IP-Add-On) is mature. Brevo's shared infrastructure is solid for most use cases. But I've seen teams with mediocre list hygiene take a real deliverability hit when moving to Brevo's shared IPs, because their bounce rates dragged down shared pool reputation. If you're migrating from a platform with a dedicated IP, build in a 30-day warmup period on Brevo and run your list through [Bouncer](/go/bouncer/) or [ZeroBounce](/go/zerobounce/) before the first send. Don't skip this step.

The flip side: Brevo's transactional email deliverability is excellent, because that infrastructure is their legacy. [According to Brevo's own deliverability benchmarks](https://www.brevo.com/blog/email-deliverability/), transactional emails sent via their SMTP relay consistently hit 98%+ delivery rates. For SaaS teams shipping product emails through the same platform as marketing sends, that's a meaningful differentiator. ActiveCampaign's transactional story is genuinely an afterthought by comparison.

## The bottom line for RevOps operators

The Brevo vs ActiveCampaign decision in 2026 is really a question of automation complexity versus pricing sanity. Running linear nurture sequences, transactional lifecycle emails, and basic behavioral triggers? Brevo delivers 80% of the outcome at 40% of the cost. Your RevOps motion requires automation logic that responds to CRM events, lead score thresholds, and predictive signals? ActiveCampaign still owns that territory and the premium is justified. Know which team you actually are before you sign a contract.
