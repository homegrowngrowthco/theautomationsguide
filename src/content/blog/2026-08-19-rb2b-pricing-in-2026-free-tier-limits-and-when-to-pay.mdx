The question I get most from early-stage B2B SaaS RevOps teams is not "should we use visitor identification" but "is the RB2B free tier actually enough, or is this a loss-leader designed to make us upgrade in 30 days?" Fair question. The free tier is genuinely useful. But it has real constraints that push you toward a paid decision faster than most vendors admit, and the competitive set has shifted enough in 2026 that defaulting to [RB2B](/go/rb2b/) without at least glancing at [Warmly](/go/warmly/), [Vector](/go/vector/), and [Factors.ai](/go/factors-ai/) is a mistake.

## What the free tier actually gives you (and where it stops)

The RB2B free plan identifies individual visitors by name, LinkedIn profile, and company, pushing that data directly into a Slack channel. You get up to 100 person-level reveals per month. No expiry. That is a genuinely different value proposition from the typical 14-day trial model. I have watched clients at Homegrown Growth Co. run on the free plan for six months before outgrowing it.

What you do not get on free: webhook or Zapier/Make integrations, CRM push, CSV exports, API access, or historical data beyond a rolling window. In practice, this means your workflow is Slack notification to manual copy-paste into your CRM. Fine if you have one SDR reviewing ten hot visitors a week. It breaks the moment you want automated enrichment or routing logic.

The other hard constraint is geography. Person-level data is US visitors only. Account-level identification (company name, industry, page views) works for international traffic, but you lose the LinkedIn profile match that makes [RB2B](/tools/rb2b/) worth using in the first place. If 40 percent of your pipeline comes from Europe, your effective ceiling is well below 100 reveals.

## The true cost: free tier math for a real team

Here is the arithmetic I walk clients through. Take 2,000 unique monthly visitors, a 3 percent ICP match rate (realistic for a focused B2B SaaS), and 60 percent US traffic. That produces roughly 36 person-level reveals per month. Free tier covers this comfortably. You are unlikely to upgrade for at least a year.

Flip the inputs: 8,000 visitors, same ICP rate, same US split. Now you are at around 144 person-level reveals. You hit the ceiling at month one.

The upgrade to paid at roughly $149/month is straightforward math. The part most evaluations skip is the integration work sitting behind it. Pushing reveals to HubSpot with proper contact deduplication and sequence enrollment requires either a native connector (paid tier only) or an [n8n](/tools/n8n/) workflow on top. I have built this for clients and it is not complex, but it is not zero effort either. Budget a few hours and a $20/month n8n instance if you want it done right.

That overhead matters more than people expect. By the time you account for the SDR time required to manually action free-tier RB2B data, you might be spending more in labor cost than a [Warmly](/tools/warmly/) subscription runs. Warmly bundles the orchestration layer natively. For a reference on how to build that signal-to-action motion once visitor data is flowing, the [Warmly + Make signal-based outbound workflow guide](/blog/2026-08-16-warmly-make-signal-based-outbound-workflow-guide/) covers the sequencing layer in detail.

## Where the pricing is fair and where it stings

<MyTake>
RB2B's free tier is the most honest free product in the visitor ID category precisely because the core value prop (person-level LinkedIn ID delivered to Slack) is fully intact and never expires. But the pricing architecture has a real gap: there is no $49 to $99 small-team tier. You go from free to $149/month with nothing in between. That means teams who just need the CRM push before they hit volume are paying for infrastructure they are barely using. If RB2B added a sub-$75 "integrations only" tier, I would recommend it to almost every sub-500 session/month team I work with without hesitation. Right now, I sometimes steer clients toward Factors.ai instead purely because the entry paid tier is more proportionate to early-stage volume.
</MyTake>

The [RB2B review I published recently](/blog/2026-08-18-rb2b-review-2026-is-free-visitor-id-worth-the-trade-off/) covers the overall value trade-off in more depth, including whether data quality holds up against paid alternatives. Short version: for US-focused, early-stage teams with an SDR working named accounts, RB2B free is genuinely good. For teams that need automation and attribution alongside identification, [Factors.ai](/tools/factors-ai/) or [Warmly](/tools/warmly/) justify their price faster.

On the competitive positioning: Factors.ai has the strongest account analytics story. Warmly wins on orchestration. RB2B wins on simplicity and price-to-value at low volume. Vector sits in a different lane entirely. Think of it as an intent aggregator with contact resolution, not a pure visitor ID tool, and it is [priced accordingly](https://www.factors.ai/pricing).

If you are evaluating these tools alongside other data enrichment decisions, the frameworks in the [Lusha pricing breakdown](/blog/2026-08-13-lusha-pricing-in-2026-credits-seats-and-when-to-switch/) apply here too. Always model cost per actioned lead, not just the monthly fee. A $149/month tool that produces 12 actioned leads is cheaper than a $49/month tool that produces three.
