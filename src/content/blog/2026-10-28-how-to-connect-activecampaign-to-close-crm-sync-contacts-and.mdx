There is no native ActiveCampaign Close integration. That surprises a lot of teams running outbound through [Close](/go/close/) and nurture through [ActiveCampaign](/go/activecampaign/). The gap matters: without a sync layer, reps are manually checking whether a lead opened a sequence email before calling, and marketing is completely blind to which leads a rep already touched. The fix is a webhook-and-API bridge built in [n8n](/go/n8n/) or [Zapier](/go/zapier/), and it takes about two hours to get right once you know the field-mapping landmines going in.

The core use case is lead routing and follow-up timing. Marketing qualifies a lead through an ActiveCampaign nurture sequence. That lead needs to land in Close as an opportunity the moment they hit a score threshold or click a specific link. Going the other direction, when a rep marks a lead "No Answer x3" in Close, that signal should pause the ActiveCampaign sequence and re-queue the contact into a longer drip, not keep hammering them with emails.

I'd argue the bidirectional sync is where most teams underinvest. They build the marketing-to-CRM push and call it done, leaving reps' activity completely invisible to ActiveCampaign. That one-way blindness is how you end up with a rep calling someone who already unsubscribed.

If you are evaluating whether ActiveCampaign is even the right tool for this stack, our [ActiveCampaign alternatives breakdown](/blog/2026-10-23-activecampaign-alternatives-in-2026-brevo-vs-customerio-vs-a/) covers the cases where Brevo or Customer.io make more sense.

## The field-mapping gotcha and how to fix it

<SideBySide>
  <Fragment slot="left">
    **The trap:** Close stores phone numbers as an array of objects with a `field` key and a `phone` key, not a flat string. ActiveCampaign expects a plain string in the phone field. If you pass the raw Close phone array directly into ActiveCampaign's API, the write either fails silently or stores `[object Object]` in the phone field.

    I'd also watch out for Close's lead status being a free-text string that teams have customized differently across workspaces. A status of "Qualified" in one Close account might be "SQL" in another. Hard-code a status map in your n8n workflow, not on the ActiveCampaign side, so you have one place to update it.
  </Fragment>
  <Fragment slot="right">
    ```javascript
    // n8n Function node: normalize Close phone to AC string
    const closePhones = $json.lead.phones || [];
    const primaryPhone = closePhones.find(p => p.field === 'office')
      || closePhones[0];
    const phoneString = primaryPhone ? primaryPhone.phone : '';

    // Status map: Close label -> AC tag
    const statusMap = {
      'Qualified': 'CRM: SQL',
      'SQL':       'CRM: SQL',
      'Nurture':   'CRM: Nurture Queue',
      'No Answer': 'CRM: No Answer',
      'Lost':      'CRM: Lost'
    };

    const acTag = statusMap[$json.lead.status_label] || 'CRM: Unknown';

    return [{
      json: {
        email: $json.lead.contacts[0]?.emails[0]?.email,
        phone: phoneString,
        acTag:  acTag
      }
    }];
    ```
  </Fragment>
</SideBySide>

## Triggering follow-up sequences from CRM signals

The highest-value output of this integration is not contact sync. It is automating follow-up timing based on what reps actually do in Close. Per [ActiveCampaign's API documentation](https://developers.activecampaign.com/reference/create-new-automation-entry), you can enroll a contact into any automation by POSTing to `/api/3/automationContacts` with a contact ID and automation ID. That means:

- A Close call logged as "Left Voicemail" fires an n8n step that enrolls the contact in a "post-voicemail" AC sequence, a short 3-email drip spaced 2, 4, and 7 days out
- A Close opportunity moved to "Demo Scheduled" removes the contact from any active drip and adds a tag that suppresses them from batch sends until the opportunity closes
- A Close lead marked "Lost" after a close date starts a 90-day re-engagement automation in ActiveCampaign rather than leaving the contact orphaned

This is where the [Close](/tools/close/) and [ActiveCampaign](/tools/activecampaign/) combination earns its keep. Close handles the calling and deal tracking. ActiveCampaign handles the long-game nurture. The integration is the connective tissue that keeps both tools aware of what the other is doing.

[ActiveCampaign's automation branching](https://help.activecampaign.com/hc/en-us/articles/221493867-How-to-use-If-Else-actions-in-automations) also lets you branch on custom field values. Once you are writing Close's lead status into ActiveCampaign, you can use that field to branch your nurture sequences by where the lead is in the sales process, not just by behavior.

The real value comes from revisiting the status-to-automation mappings every quarter as your sales process shifts. The integration is not a project you close out. It is a system you maintain.

## Get the sync running, then optimize

The two-hour build estimate above is realistic if your field map is clean going in. The nightly reconciliation job is not optional. Skip it and you will spend that time manually debugging mismatch reports from reps. Once the core sync is stable, the next layer worth building is passing ActiveCampaign's lead score into Close as a custom field so reps can sort their queue by marketing engagement before their morning call block. That single addition tends to change how reps prioritize, and it costs about 20 minutes to add once the base integration is live.
