Most B2B teams that land on [Brevo](/go/brevo/) treat it as a newsletter tool and leave 70% of the platform untouched. The automation builder, contact scoring engine, and CRM sync layer are all there, but the default onboarding pushes you toward simple broadcast campaigns. If you are running a real GTM motion with MQL thresholds, pipeline stages, and rep handoff workflows, you have to build the scaffolding yourself. This guide covers exactly that: how to configure scoring, build behavioral segments that actually qualify leads, wire up a bidirectional CRM sync, and understand where Brevo fits versus [ActiveCampaign](/go/activecampaign/) or [GetResponse](/go/getresponse/) when your stack needs change.

## The Architecture Before You Build Anything

The mistake I see most often on Brevo setups is jumping straight into the automation builder before defining what a qualified contact actually looks like in the account. Automations in Brevo are triggered by segment membership, score thresholds, or events. If your segments are loose and your scoring rules are undefined, every automation fires on noise.

The correct build order is scoring rules first, segments second, automations third. Here is what that looks like in practice.

## Where the Real Complexity Lives: Webhooks and Custom Events

The segment and scoring setup above handles 80% of B2B use cases. The remaining 20% requires custom events, which is where [Brevo](/tools/brevo/)'s API earns its keep.

<SideBySide>
  <Fragment slot="left">
    **The trap:** Teams use Brevo's standard email-event triggers (opened, clicked) as proxies for intent. Those signals are noisy after iOS Mail Privacy Protection killed reliable open tracking. A contact who "opened" every email because Apple pre-fetched it will score into Hot MQL with zero real intent.

    The fix is behavioral events from your product or website. Send a custom event to Brevo via the API when a contact visits the pricing page, starts a trial, or logs in after 7 days of inactivity. These are ground-truth signals and they should carry 3x to 5x the scoring weight of email engagement. I'd argue that any scoring model that still leans on raw open rates as a primary signal is effectively measuring mail server behavior, not buyer intent.

    Per [Brevo's developer docs](https://developers.brevo.com/docs/track-events), custom events are sent via a simple POST to the transactional events endpoint and appear in the automation builder as "Event: [event name]" triggers within minutes.
  </Fragment>
  <Fragment slot="right">
    ```json
    POST https://api.brevo.com/v3/events
    Headers:
      api-key: YOUR_API_KEY
      Content-Type: application/json

    {
      "event_name": "pricing_page_viewed",
      "event_properties": {
        "plan_viewed": "business",
        "time_on_page_seconds": 142
      },
      "identifiers": {
        "email": "contact@example.com"
      }
    }
    ```

    Map `event_name` as the automation trigger.
    Use `event_properties` values in conditions:
    "If plan_viewed = business AND
    time_on_page_seconds > 60, add +20 score."

    Chain this with a segment filter:
    "Score >= 50 AND event: pricing_page_viewed
    in last 7 days" = demo-ready MQL.

## Verification: How to Know Your Setup Is Actually Working

Before you call the Brevo build done, run these four checks.

**Score audit.** Pull the contact list sorted by score descending. The top 20 should be recognizable as engaged prospects or current customers. If random contacts appear at the top, your scoring weights are wrong or custom attributes are not being populated.

**Automation entry log.** In Brevo's automation reports, check "Contacts currently in automation" vs. "Contacts who exited." A healthy ratio is at least 70% completing the workflow without early exit. High early-exit rates usually mean the exit trigger is too broad or a segment condition flips too quickly.

**CRM field parity.** Pick five contacts at random and compare the Brevo contact record with the corresponding record in your CRM. The sync lag should be under 15 minutes for property updates per [Brevo's integration docs](https://help.brevo.com/hc/en-us/articles/209483885). If fields are mismatched, check the field mapping and confirm the sync direction rule is set correctly per step four above.

**Suppression coverage.** Send a test contact through the system by manually adding them to the Hot MQL segment. Confirm the automation fires, the CRM task is created, and the internal notification lands. Then mark the contact as Closed Won in the CRM and verify they exit the automation within one sync cycle.

## Build the Foundation, Then Automate

A Brevo setup that earns the label "expert" is not about using every feature. It is about having a coherent data model: clean scoring rules that map to real intent signals, segments that reflect actual pipeline stages, and a CRM sync that keeps marketing and sales looking at the same record. Build in that order and the automation layer becomes straightforward. Skip the foundation and you spend every quarter debugging why the wrong contacts are routing to reps.

For teams evaluating the full email cluster, [GetResponse](/tools/getresponse/) is worth a look if webinars anchor your demand gen, and ActiveCampaign earns consideration when your sales motion needs CRM depth inside the marketing platform itself. But for most B2B teams building a scalable, budget-conscious GTM stack, Brevo is a more capable starting point than its reputation suggests.
