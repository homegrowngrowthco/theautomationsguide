Most RevOps teams don't abandon [HubSpot](/go/hubspot/) because it stops working. They abandon it because they hit a wall: a branching condition that requires Operations Hub Pro, a multi-object sync that needs custom code, or a contact tier overage that doubles their bill for 500 extra leads. At that inflection point, the instinct is to reach for something more powerful, usually [Make](/go/make/) or a full migration. But I've watched teams overengineer their way into a worse problem by defaulting to Make when [Kit](/go/kit/) would have shipped the workflow in two hours. This post cuts through that reflex.

## What each tool is actually built for

People compare these three tools as if they occupy the same product category. They do not. HubSpot is a CRM with a workflow engine bolted on. Make is a middleware automation platform. Kit is an email-first audience platform with automation layered in. Conflating them is why I see teams either overpaying for HubSpot or building fragile Make scenarios that collapse when an API schema changes.

The workflow engine inside HubSpot is genuinely good for what it was designed to do: trigger on CRM events (deal stage changed, form submitted, property updated) and write back to CRM objects. That feedback loop, where automation reads from the CRM and writes to the same CRM, is where HubSpot has a durable advantage. [As HubSpot's own docs explain](https://knowledge.hubspot.com/workflows/create-workflows), workflows can enroll contacts based on any combination of contact, company, deal, or custom object properties without a third-party connector.

The problems start when you need to route data between HubSpot and four other tools in the same workflow, apply conditional logic deeper than three branches, or keep costs flat as your contact count scales. That is where Make or Kit enter legitimately.

Don't bother trying to force HubSpot's native workflow branching past three levels of nesting. Past that depth, the visual builder becomes unreadable, errors become hard to trace, and you're one accidental enrollment away from spamming your entire database. Use Make for that logic instead.

## How to choose when you are actually at the limit

If you are hitting HubSpot workflow limits, the first diagnostic question is not "which tool should I switch to?" It is "which specific limit am I hitting?" I have seen this play out differently enough that it changes the answer completely.

If the limit is **contact tier pricing** (you are at 50k contacts and the next tier is a significant jump), Make does not solve that. Make is not your contact database. Kit might, if you can shift some nurture flows out of HubSpot entirely and manage a segment of your audience there. If the limit is **branching complexity or custom code** (you need to write JavaScript inside a workflow step), Make is the right escape hatch. Its scenario builder handles arbitrarily complex conditional logic and connects to [over 1,800 native apps](https://www.make.com/en/integrations). If the limit is **deliverability and engagement on marketing emails**, that is a Kit conversation, not an ops conversation.

The pattern I run most often is Make alongside HubSpot. HubSpot stays the system of record for CRM data, Make handles the cross-system orchestration, and content-led nurture sequences run in [Kit](/tools/kit/) when the audience is subscriber-based rather than pure sales pipeline. As I noted in a [related post on HubSpot automations costing you deals](/blog/2026-09-09-hubspot-automations-costing-you-deals-and-you-dont-know-it/), the failure mode is usually not the tool but misaligned trigger logic. Running Make as an orchestration layer surfaces those misalignments faster because you can add logging and error handling that HubSpot's native workflows simply don't expose.

The contrarian case for Kit is real and I want to make it clearly. If your GTM motion is content-led (newsletter, PLG, community-driven), Kit's automation model is fundamentally better suited than either HubSpot or Make. Kit's sequences are built for subscriber lifecycle, not CRM pipeline. You can [compare Kit against Beehiiv and Loops for revenue teams](/blog/2026-09-10-beehiiv-vs-kit-vs-loops-best-for-revenue-teams-in-2026/) to see how the email platforms stack up when the primary channel is owned audience rather than outbound.

<MyTake>
The "just use Make" reflex in the RevOps community is often wrong for the same reason the "just stay on HubSpot" reflex is wrong: both skip the diagnostic. In my experience, teams that switch to Make without a dedicated ops owner end up with 40 broken scenarios and no one who remembers why any of them exist. Kit's simplicity is underrated precisely because most RevOps practitioners overestimate how much branching complexity their email nurture actually needs. If your sequences are fewer than six steps and the logic is tag-based, Kit ships it faster and breaks less. Full stop.

## The decision that matters more than the tool

Choosing between HubSpot workflows, [Make](/tools/make/), and Kit is ultimately a question about where your automation complexity lives and who owns maintaining it. HubSpot wins when you want automation embedded in the CRM with native reporting and low maintenance overhead, and you can afford the tier. Make wins when your stack has grown beyond what a single platform can orchestrate and you have someone technical to own scenario hygiene. Kit wins when email is your primary GTM channel and simplicity is a feature, not a compromise.

None of these are permanent decisions. I have run all three in the same stack at the same time without it being a mess, because each was doing the job it was built for. The teams that struggle are the ones trying to make one tool do all three jobs.
