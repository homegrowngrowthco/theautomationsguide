## What Bland AI's pricing actually includes

[Bland AI's pricing page](https://www.bland.ai/pricing) is refreshingly direct for this category: you pay per minute of call time. No seat fees, no platform fees on the self-serve tier, no separate charges for the number of agents you configure. The $0.09/min rate covers voice synthesis, the telephony relay, and the basic LLM inference powering the conversation. What it does not cover is phone number provisioning, which runs about $1.15 per number per month through Bland's Twilio-backed infrastructure.

Where teams get surprised is concurrent call capacity. The self-serve plan caps you at a relatively low number of simultaneous calls. If you are running a dialing campaign that needs 20 or 50 live calls at once, you will hit that ceiling before your minute bill gets interesting. Enterprise contracts unlock higher concurrency and typically negotiate a per-minute rate between $0.04 and $0.07 depending on committed volume. My read is that the self-serve tier is genuinely useful for proofs of concept and smaller programs (under 5,000 minutes per month), but anyone building a production SDR replacement workflow should be in conversation with Bland's sales team before writing a line of configuration.

## The true cost: real math for a 10-rep SDR team

Say a RevOps team is standing up an AI voice layer for a 10-rep outbound motion. Each AI agent makes 150 calls per day, average call duration of 90 seconds (most go to voicemail or hit a 20-second answer before hang-up, with a few 4-minute conversations pulling the average up). That is roughly 225 minutes per agent per day, 2,250 minutes across the team, and about 45,000 minutes per month on a 20-day working month.

At Bland AI's self-serve rate that is $4,050/month. At a negotiated enterprise rate of $0.06/min it drops to $2,700/month. At [Vapi](/tools/vapi/)'s published volume pricing for 50k+ minutes, around $0.05/min all-in per [Vapi's billing docs](https://docs.vapi.ai/billing), the same load costs $2,250/month. That $450 delta is real but not the deciding factor. The deciding factor is usually developer time: Bland AI's no-code agent builder versus Vapi's API-first approach. If your team has an engineer to own the integration, Vapi wins on cost. If this is a RevOps-led build with no dedicated eng, Bland AI's setup speed offsets the premium.

[Synthflow](/tools/synthflow/) at this volume does not make economic sense on its subscription tiers. Its $299/month "Scale" plan includes 2,000 minutes, and overages run $0.13/min. At 45,000 minutes, your Synthflow bill comes out to $299 + (43,000 x $0.13) = $5,888/month, per [Synthflow's published pricing](https://synthflow.ai/pricing). Synthflow is priced for lower-volume programs, not high-throughput dialing.

This is the same cost-at-scale tension I see covered in the [AI dialer comparison between Nooks, Orum, and Kixie](/blog/2026-11-07-nooks-vs-orum-vs-kixie-best-dialer-for-sdr-teams-2026/): the tool with the friendliest entry price is rarely the right call once you model out 90-day volumes.

## Where the pricing is fair and where it stings

Bland AI's self-serve pricing is genuinely fair for the category. A flat per-minute rate with no seat tax is the right model for teams who do not know their volume yet. The enterprise tier is competitive if you can get to a commitment conversation quickly.

What stings is the concurrency ceiling on self-serve. It is functionally a forcing mechanism to push you into enterprise, and the gap between self-serve and enterprise capability is wide enough to create real production risk if you do not catch it in scoping. The pattern that keeps surfacing in community forums: teams go live with a campaign only to discover their calls are queueing instead of dialing because they hit the concurrency wall.

Vapi's pricing is aggressive at volume but the total cost of ownership includes the developer time to maintain a more complex integration. That is a real cost that does not appear on the pricing page.

Synthflow's overage rate of $0.13/min is the worst of the three for anyone who underestimates their volume. If you buy a subscription expecting predictability and blow past the included minutes, you end up paying more per minute than Bland AI's self-serve rate. For a discussion of how that same overage-trap pattern plays out in a different category, see the [Warmly pricing breakdown](/blog/2026-11-10-warmly-pricing-in-2026-plans-seat-costs-and-when-to-upgrade/).

<MyTake>
The per-minute rate is the wrong number to anchor on when evaluating AI voice platforms. Concurrency limits and developer ownership cost are the variables that actually determine your total spend and time-to-value. Bland AI is priced to win at the pilot stage and it does. But the enterprise conversation needs to happen before you go to production, not after your first campaign queues instead of dials. Teams that skip that step are negotiating from a weak position mid-rollout, and vendors know it.
</MyTake>

## The bottom line on Bland AI pricing

Bland AI is the right starting point for most RevOps-led AI voice programs, specifically because self-serve onboarding does not require procurement or engineering sign-off to validate the use case. The $0.09/min rate is not the cheapest in the market at scale, but it is priced fairly for the speed and simplicity it delivers. When your program clears 10,000 minutes per month and you have the engineering resources to handle a more complex integration, [Vapi](/tools/vapi/) earns a serious look. [Synthflow](/tools/synthflow/) is the right fit when call volume is bounded and billing predictability matters more than cost efficiency.
