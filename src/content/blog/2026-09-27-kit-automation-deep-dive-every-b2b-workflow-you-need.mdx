## The Foundation: How Kit Automation Actually Works

Before building any specific workflow, you need a mental model of how Kit's engine fits together. Kit operates on three primitives: subscribers, tags, and sequences. An automation is a set of rules connecting triggers to actions on those three objects.

A trigger fires when something happens: form subscribe, tag added, link clicked, purchase made, or inbound webhook. Actions include adding tags, removing tags, sending emails, subscribing to a sequence, moving to a visual automation, and adding delays. Conditions let you branch. "If subscriber has tag X, go left; otherwise go right." That's the whole engine. The power comes from stacking these cleanly.

One thing I always clarify with operators new to Kit: sequences and automations are different objects. A sequence is an ordered series of emails with delays. An automation is the logic layer that decides who enters a sequence, when, and under what conditions. You build both, and the automation orchestrates the sequence. Don't conflate them or your debugging will become a nightmare.

## Connecting Kit to the Rest of Your Stack

The native Kit integrations (Stripe, Teachable, Thrivecart, Zapier) cover most direct triggers. Where Kit gets genuinely powerful for B2B is when you stop relying on native connectors and start treating Kit webhooks as event emitters into [n8n](/tools/n8n/) for multi-step logic.

<SideBySide>

    **The trap operators fall into:** They use Zapier to connect Kit to HubSpot with a single "new subscriber" trigger and call it done. That sends every cold newsletter subscriber straight into HubSpot as a contact, polluting the CRM with unqualified traffic and blowing through HubSpot contact tier limits fast. I watched this mistake cost a team an unplanned upgrade from HubSpot Starter to Pro mid-quarter. Don't use Zapier's "new subscriber" trigger for HubSpot sync. Use n8n with an intent tag filter instead.
  </Fragment>
  <Fragment slot="right">

    ```json
    // n8n webhook filter: only sync to HubSpot
    // when specific intent tags are present
    {
      "filter": {
        "tags_include_any": [
          "demo-requested",
          "high-intent",
          "customer",
          "trial-started"
        ]
      },
      "hubspot_lifecycle": "marketingqualifiedlead",
      "create_task": true,
      "task_owner": "round-robin"
    }
    ```
  </Fragment>

The n8n approach lets you add a filter step between Kit and HubSpot: only sync contacts who have crossed an intent threshold. You define that threshold via tags in Kit. This keeps HubSpot clean and makes your MQL definition operational rather than theoretical. ## Kit vs. Beehiiv vs. HubSpot Sequences: Which Owns What

This is the question I get most often from RevOps operators building a content-led inbound motion. The honest answer is they serve different jobs. Forcing one platform to do all three is how you end up with a brittle stack that nobody trusts.

[Beehiiv](/tools/beehiiv/) wins if your primary motion is newsletter monetization: boosts, paid subscriptions, referral growth. The [Beehiiv automations guide](/blog/2026-09-24-beehiiv-automations-guide-every-workflow-for-2026/) covers where that tool actually earns its keep. [HubSpot](/tools/hubspot/) wins if you need sequences tied to deal stage and want everything in one CRM record. Kit wins if you're running a content-led B2B inbound motion where nurture complexity matters and you want costs that stay predictable as your list grows.

My take: if you're debating between Kit and HubSpot Sequences for nurture, pick Kit and spend the savings on better copy. HubSpot Sequences is built for sales reps doing 1:1 outreach, not marketing automation. The tooling reflects that.

## The B2B Kit Stack I'd Recommend

Here's the setup that makes the most operational sense for a sub-100-person B2B SaaS company running a content-led motion:

- [Kit](/tools/kit/) handles all inbound email: lead magnet delivery, newsletter, nurture sequences, re-engagement
- n8n routes intent signals (tag additions) into HubSpot as lifecycle stage changes
- HubSpot owns the CRM record, deal pipeline, and sales sequences post-MQL
- [Beehiiv](/go/beehiiv/) gets considered only if the newsletter becomes a revenue center in its own right, not before

The handoff point is the "demo-requested" or "high-intent" tag in Kit. Everything before that tag lives in Kit. Everything after it lives in HubSpot. n8n is the bridge. That's a clean, auditable boundary that any RevOps operator can maintain without a developer on call.

According to [Kit's own benchmark data](https://kit.com/features/email-marketing), automations on their platform average 47% open rates versus 18% for broadcast emails. That tracks with what I've seen in practice. The gap closes when broadcast content is genuinely strong, but triggered automations almost always outperform because relevance and timing compound on each other.

For the HubSpot side of this handoff, the [HubSpot chatbot inbound lead fix](/blog/2026-09-24-your-hubspot-chatbot-is-losing-inbound-leads-fix-it/) post covers how to make sure leads arriving from Kit don't fall through HubSpot's intake gaps.

<MyTake>
Most operators over-engineer the Kit automation map and under-engineer the HubSpot handoff. Three automations in Kit that fire reliably plus one clean webhook into HubSpot will outperform fifteen automations with no CRM sync every single time. The fanciest nurture sequence in the world is worthless if sales never sees the intent signal.
</MyTake>

## Build These Workflows Before Anything Else

Kit's automation library is extensive, and the temptation is to keep building. Resist it. The five workflows above (lead magnet delivery, newsletter onboarding, CRM sync via n8n, re-engagement, and purchase trigger) cover 90% of the inbound value for a B2B SaaS company. [Kit's automation documentation](https://help.kit.com/en/articles/2502550-create-an-automation) is well-written and worth reading end to end before you start wiring conditions, because the order of operations matters when multiple automations can match the same subscriber event.

Get those five running. Tag your intent thresholds cleanly. Wire the webhook into your CRM. Then spend your time improving the copy inside each sequence rather than adding new automations. A rewritten subject line on sequence email two will almost always move more than a sixth automation ever will.
