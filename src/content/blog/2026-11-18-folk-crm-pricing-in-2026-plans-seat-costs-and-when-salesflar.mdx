## What you actually get at each tier

The folk pricing page lists Standard and Premium as the two paid tiers, plus an Enterprise path for larger teams that requires a custom quote. Here is what matters for a RevOps evaluation, not just what the tier comparison table says.

**Standard at $20 per seat per month** gets you unlimited contacts, multiple pipelines, email sync, the Chrome extension for LinkedIn enrichment, basic sequences, and a fixed monthly enrichment credit allotment per seat. The pipeline count is not literally capped at one, but template limits and sequence restrictions make Standard feel like a starter tier in practice.

**Premium at $40 per seat per month** removes most of those friction points. You get higher enrichment limits, unlimited pipeline views, more sequence steps, priority support, and access to the folkX enrichment network at a higher depth. If your team is running more than one active pipeline or doing any volume of outreach, Premium is the realistic entry point. Not Standard.

**Enterprise** is custom pricing. Folk targets this at teams that need SSO, audit logs, dedicated support, and custom enrichment or integration arrangements. Per [folk's pricing documentation](https://www.folk.app/pricing), you need to contact their sales team for a quote.

One thing worth flagging: folk's enrichment model is not purely seat-based credits. The folkX enrichment engine pulls from third-party data sources, and the credit consumption rate depends heavily on how many fields you are enriching per contact. A team enriching email, LinkedIn, company size, and industry on every new contact will burn through credits faster than a team just validating emails. I'd plan your credit budget around four fields per contact minimum if you're doing any real prospecting.

## The true cost: a 5-seat team, two scenarios

Here is the actual math for a 5-person revenue team, which is the typical folk buyer.

**Scenario A: 5 seats on Standard, light use**

- 5 x $20 = $100 per month
- Enrichment stays within bundled credits (under 500 contacts enriched per month)
- No add-ons
- Total: $100 per month, $1,200 per year

This works for a founder-led team with an existing contact base who mostly needs a clean relationship layer, not a prospecting engine.

**Scenario B: 5 seats on Premium, active outbound**

- 5 x $40 = $200 per month
- Enrichment overages likely once the team is enriching 1,000+ contacts per month
- Zapier or Make integration required for any CRM-to-outbound tool handoff (folk does not have native Smartlead or Instantly integrations)
- Total with integration tooling: $200 to $250 per month depending on add-on tiers

At $200 to $250 per month for 5 seats, you are in the same budget range as [Salesflare](/tools/salesflare/) Growth, which includes automated email and meeting logging, LinkedIn sidebar enrichment, and built-in pipeline automation without needing a middleware layer.

That comparison matters. The usual failure here is teams buying folk at the Standard tier thinking they will grow into Premium features, then hitting enrichment and sequence walls at exactly the moment they are ramping outbound. Don't buy Standard expecting Premium. The gap is real.

## Where folk pricing is fair and where it stings

Folk's Standard tier at $20 per seat is genuinely fair for what it is: a beautiful contact database with email sync and a Chrome extension that actually works on LinkedIn. The UX is better than anything in this price range, including the Essential tier of [Pipedrive](/tools/pipedrive/). I'd take folk's interface over Pipedrive Essential any day for a founder managing a network rather than a quota.

The sting is in the enrichment credit model. Unlike Salesflare, which leans on automated capture from your existing email and calendar activity, folk requires active enrichment actions to populate contact data. That means credit consumption is usage-driven, and teams doing real prospecting will hit the ceiling. The math on add-on credits is not transparent on the pricing page, which creates a RevOps planning problem: you can't accurately forecast contact data costs until you've already started burning credits.

The second sting is automation gaps. If you need folk to feed into your outbound sequencing stack (say, Smartlead or [Lemlist](/tools/lemlist/)), you are building that bridge yourself in Zapier or [Make](/tools/make/). Not a dealbreaker, but it is an ongoing maintenance cost and latency risk that Salesflare avoids by handling the data layer natively. Per the [lemlist pricing breakdown I covered earlier](/blog/2026-11-12-lemlist-pricing-in-2026-plans-limits-and-when-replyio-wins/), middleware costs accumulate fast when you are running multichannel sequences.

My read is that folk's pricing is actually simpler and more predictable than most competitors. There are no seat minimums on the paid tiers, no mandatory annual contract on month-to-month, and no surprise telephony or dialer add-ons because folk does not try to be a dialer. That restraint is worth something.

<MyTake>
Folk is not a sales execution platform and was never meant to be one. Buying it and expecting Salesflare-level pipeline automation is a category error, not a product failure. If your team's job is closing pipeline, Salesflare at $29 per seat is the sharper RevOps spend. Folk is the right tool when the work is relationship quality, not deal velocity.
</MyTake>

## The bottom line on folk CRM pricing

Folk is a well-designed CRM for teams that prioritize relationship quality over pipeline volume. The Standard tier is fine for light use, but most revenue teams will end up on Premium at $40 per seat, where the value proposition starts to get crowded by Salesflare and even the mid-tiers of Pipedrive. If you are a B2B SaaS team with a formal outbound motion, run the math against [Salesflare](/go/salesflare/) before committing. If you are a founder managing investors, advisors, and partners across a network, folk may be exactly the right tool at a fair price.
