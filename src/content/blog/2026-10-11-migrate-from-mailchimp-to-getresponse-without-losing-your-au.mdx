## Before You Migrate

Rushing the export step is the most common failure in any platform switch. Spend 30 minutes here and you will save hours of cleanup on the other side. I'd argue this pre-work matters more than anything you do inside GetResponse on day one.

Here is what to pull out of Mailchimp before you touch GetResponse:

- **All lists as CSV**, including merge fields and tags. Mailchimp lets you export per-audience under Audience > All Contacts > Export Audience.
- **Automation screenshots or flowchart exports** for every active sequence. There is no native export format that GetResponse can read, so a visual record is your rebuild spec.
- **Campaign performance reports** (open rate, click rate, revenue attribution if you have e-commerce connected). Go to Mailchimp > Reports > Export as CSV. Once you cancel, this data is gone.
- **Suppression lists and unsubscribes**. Export these separately. You must import your unsubscribes into GetResponse as a suppression list before sending a single email, or you will re-mail people who opted out. That is a CAN-SPAM and GDPR liability, not just a deliverability problem.
- **Connected integrations list**. Any Zapier workflows, CRM syncs (HubSpot, Pipedrive, Salesforce), or e-commerce connectors pointing at Mailchimp need to be re-pointed after you migrate.
- **Sending domain records**. Note your current SPF and DKIM setup. You will need to add GetResponse's authentication records to your DNS before anything goes out.

Pause any active Mailchimp automations during the cutover window. Running sequences on both platforms simultaneously means contacts get double-mailed.

## What Breaks and What Does Not Transfer

Knowing the gaps upfront beats discovering them mid-campaign.

<SideBySide>
  <Fragment slot="left">
    **The gotchas that catch teams off guard:**

    - Mailchimp's "Groups" and "Segments" do not have a direct equivalent. Groups map roughly to GetResponse tags; saved segments need to be recreated as GetResponse search conditions or saved searches.
    - Mailchimp's e-commerce integration data (purchase history, product recommendations) does not migrate. If you relied on purchase-triggered automations, you will need to reconnect your e-commerce platform (Shopify, WooCommerce) natively in GetResponse and rebuild those triggers from scratch.
    - RSS-to-email campaigns do not port. Recreate them in GetResponse under Create Newsletter > RSS.
    - Mailchimp's "Customer Journey" builder maps reasonably well to GetResponse's Marketing Automation canvas, but the branching logic uses different condition syntax. A Mailchimp "If/Else" split on tag presence becomes a GetResponse "If contact has tag" condition block.
    - Contact engagement scores (Mailchimp's star ratings) do not transfer. You start with a clean engagement slate in GetResponse.

The first thing I'd check after every import batch is the contact count. GetResponse shows a running import count with errors flagged inline. If your Mailchimp export had 8,400 contacts and GetResponse shows 7,900 imported with 500 errors, those errors are almost always malformed email addresses or duplicate records. Fix the CSV and re-import the error rows. Do not ignore the gap.

## Verify It Worked

Do not declare victory until you have checked all four of these:

1. **DNS authentication confirmed**: In GetResponse under Profile > Email Addresses, your sending domain should show a green "Verified" status for both SPF and DKIM. If either is yellow or red, no amount of great copy will save your deliverability.
2. **Suppression list active**: Send a test email to an address you know is on your Mailchimp unsubscribe export. It should not arrive. If it does, your suppression import failed.
3. **Automation triggers firing**: Manually trigger each rebuilt workflow by subscribing a test contact via your signup form. Walk through each sequence step by step and confirm the emails arrive, the delays apply, and the branching conditions route correctly.
4. **Integrations reconnected**: If you use a CRM sync (say, a HubSpot or [Pipedrive](/go/pipedrive/) native integration or a Zapier bridge), trigger a test contact creation in the source system and confirm the contact appears in GetResponse within the expected sync window.

My read is that [ActiveCampaign](/go/activecampaign/) is the right answer for B2B SaaS teams building complex lifecycle automation, while GetResponse hits the sweet spot for SMBs and content creators who want a solid automation builder without CRM complexity. If pricing is the primary pain and your list is large but infrequently mailed, [Brevo](/go/brevo/) deserves a serious look. Brevo prices on sends rather than contacts, which is a structurally better model for that situation. ## The Migration Is a Forcing Function

A platform migration is uncomfortable. It is also the best audit opportunity you will get. Every sequence you rebuild in GetResponse is a sequence you are forced to re-examine. I'd use the rebuild step as an intentional pruning exercise: if a Mailchimp automation has not driven meaningful engagement in six months, do not migrate it. Kill it and start clean. Your new platform will perform better with five sharp sequences than fifteen inherited ones nobody has touched in two years. The rebuild is not overhead. It is the actual work.
