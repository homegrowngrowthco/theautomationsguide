Fewer still deserve a recurring line item on your tech budget. This guide cuts to the ones that actually change how deals move.

## Why the Marketplace Exists (and Why You Should Ignore Most of It)

<PullQuote>The integrations worth paying for are the ones that fix a structural gap in Pipedrive's core product, not the ones that add a feature Pipedrive already does adequately.</PullQuote>

Pipedrive's core strength is pipeline visibility. It is genuinely the best drag-and-drop deal pipeline UI in its price tier, and I will die on that hill. But it was not built to be an automation powerhouse, a conversation intelligence platform, or a dialer. The Marketplace exists to paper over those gaps. Some apps do it well. Most do not.

The pattern I keep seeing: teams install five or six Marketplace apps in the first 90 days, then quietly stop using three of them by month four because the data sync is janky or the triggers are too limited. The smarter move is to pick a thin stack of integrations that each handle a distinct job, then stitch them with a middleware layer you actually control.

## The Middleware Decision: n8n vs. Make for Pipedrive

This is the fork every Pipedrive team hits within six months of getting serious about automation. Both tools connect to Pipedrive well. The decision is really about who owns the automation and at what cost.

## Categories to Skip in the Marketplace

A few categories I'd actively avoid spending money on right now.

**Data enrichment apps.** The Marketplace has several enrichment tools that are effectively white-labeling [Lusha](/tools/lusha/) or Clearbit data at a 40 to 60 percent markup. If you need enrichment in your Pipedrive workflow, pull the data directly from the source or use [Clay](/tools/clay/) as a waterfall enrichment layer via n8n. You get better match rates and lower unit costs. Don't pay a middleman for data you can buy wholesale.

**Email sequence tools inside the Marketplace.** Pipedrive's native email automation is limited but functional for simple follow-up sequences. If you've outgrown it, the real answer is a dedicated outbound tool like [Smartlead](/tools/smartlead/) or [Lemlist](/tools/lemlist/) connected via middleware, not a Marketplace email app that locks your sequences inside a vendor's UI. I covered the best options in a [separate breakdown](/blog/2026-09-22-best-email-automation-for-pipedrive-2026-picks/) if you want the full comparison.

**Generic Zapier-based connectors.** The Marketplace lists several integrations that are just pre-built [Zapier](/tools/zapier/) Zaps wrapped in a one-click installer. You're paying for convenience that evaporates the moment you need custom logic. Build it yourself in Make or n8n and own the workflow.

## Audit Before You Add

Before installing anything new, run a quick audit of what you're already paying for. The [Pipedrive automations audit](/blog/2026-09-23-pipedrive-automations-are-leaking-deals-audit-and-fix-the-ga/) I published covers the specific places where native automation breaks down and integration data leaks out of deal records. Fixing those gaps before layering in new integrations is non-negotiable. Building on a broken foundation just means you'll debug two problems instead of one.

The [Pipedrive Marketplace app directory](https://www.pipedrive.com/en/marketplace) itself is worth browsing by category rather than by popularity. Top-rated apps skew toward the largest user bases, not the highest RevOps utility. Those are very different rankings.

<MyTake>
Most Pipedrive teams are one middleware layer away from not needing 80% of the apps they're currently paying for in the Marketplace. The Marketplace is a discovery tool, not a shopping cart. Install n8n or Make first, then decide whether any Marketplace app still fills a gap that middleware cannot. In my experience, the answer is usually no.
</MyTake>
