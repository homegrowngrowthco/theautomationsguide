The trigger I see most often is pricing. [GetResponse](/go/getresponse/) bumps you to its Marketing Automation tier the moment you want conditional logic or lead scoring. For creator-focused teams or B2B operators running editorial-plus-nurture stacks, that price jump rarely pencils out. [Kit](/go/kit/) (formerly ConvertKit) solves the same problem with a flatter cost structure and a tagging model that maps cleanly to how most operators actually think about audience segmentation.

The problem is that no official migration path exists between the two tools. So most teams either stay stuck on GetResponse out of inertia, or lose their automations in a hasty cutover. This guide closes that gap.

## Before you migrate

Audit before you touch anything. I've seen teams export their full list, import it into Kit, and then realize half the segments were built on GetResponse custom field logic that does not survive the CSV flatten. Spend an hour here and save yourself a painful rollback.

The prep checklist:

- **Audit your automations.** List every active workflow in GetResponse: trigger, conditions, email sequence, and exit criteria. Screenshot or export as PDF. GetResponse has no native automation export, so this is your only record.
- **Export contacts by segment.** Do not export one monolithic CSV. Export each GetResponse list or segment separately so you can apply distinct Kit tags on import. Include custom fields in the export.
- **Pause cold or inactive sequences.** Any automation with fewer than 5% open rates in the last 60 days should be paused before migration. No point rebuilding sequences that are already dead.
- **Document your DNS and sending domain.** Note your SPF, DKIM, and DMARC records. Kit uses its own sending infrastructure, and you will need to re-verify your domain. Allow 24-48 hours for propagation.
- **Identify your form embed locations.** Audit every page or popup carrying a GetResponse form embed. These break on cutover day and need Kit replacements ready.
- **Check your API integrations.** If anything hits the [GetResponse API](https://apireference.getresponse.com/) (Zapier zaps, a CRM sync, a Webflow form), flag it now. Those connections need rerouting to Kit's API before you flip the switch.

## What breaks and how to fix it

GetResponse-to-Kit migrations have three consistent failure points. The automation logic gap is the biggest. GetResponse supports lead scoring natively; Kit does not. If your nurture tracks branch based on a score threshold, you need to rebuild that branching using tag accumulation instead. It is not a perfect 1:1, but it works for the vast majority of B2B nurture use cases I have rebuilt.

My strong recommendation: don't bother trying to approximate a numeric score in Kit with workarounds. Use tag-based engagement tiers ('engaged-30d', 'engaged-90d', 'unengaged') and branch on those. It's actually more transparent than a score nobody remembers calibrating.

The second failure point is form embeds. GetResponse forms use proprietary JavaScript that stops working the moment you cancel or switch. You need Kit forms embedded and live before you turn off GetResponse, not after.

The third failure point is API integrations. If you have a [Zapier](/go/zapier/) zap, an [n8n](/go/n8n/) workflow, or a [Make](/go/make/) scenario that calls GetResponse directly, each one needs a parallel Kit version ready before cutover. I run both in parallel for 48 hours and compare subscriber counts to confirm parity before killing the GetResponse connection.

## Verify it worked

Before you cancel GetResponse, run these four checks:

1. **Subscriber count parity.** Your Kit subscriber count (minus suppressions) should be within 2-3% of your GetResponse active contacts. A larger gap means a failed import batch.
2. **Automation enrollment.** Manually trigger your top 3 automations with a test email address and confirm the correct emails fire in the correct order with the correct delays.
3. **Form submit to Kit.** Submit each of your new Kit forms and confirm the subscriber appears in Kit with the correct tags applied within 60 seconds.
4. **Domain authentication.** Use [MXToolbox SPF lookup](https://mxtoolbox.com/spf.aspx) to confirm your Kit SPF record is live. Send a test email to a Gmail address and check the "show original" headers for DKIM pass.

## The migration is worth doing, but do it deliberately

The GetResponse-to-Kit migration is one of the cleaner email platform switches you can make. Mostly because Kit's data model is simpler and forces you to clean up the accumulated cruft in your GetResponse account. The rebuild step feels like overhead at first. Every team I've watched do it, though, emerges with tighter automations than they started with.

The key is treating the cutover window seriously. Run your [n8n](/tools/n8n/) or [Make](/tools/make/) bridge, verify domain auth before you blast, and give Kit's sending IP a proper warm-up period. According to [Kit's own deliverability documentation](https://help.kit.com/en/articles/3489494-how-to-set-up-a-custom-sending-domain), warming over 7-10 days is the standard recommendation for lists above 10,000 contacts. Do those three things and you will not lose a meaningful subscriber or a meaningful deliverability point in the switch.
