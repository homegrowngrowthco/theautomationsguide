## The two places your pipeline is definitely leaking

I've audited enough Pipedrive instances to say this with confidence: the leak is almost always in one of two places. Post-demo follow-up is the first. Inbound lead routing, when more than one condition needs to be true simultaneously, is the second.

Pipedrive's native automations are event-triggered and linear. Deal moves to stage, send email, create activity. Fine for simple pipelines. But the moment you need "if contact opened the proposal email but did not reply within 48 hours, assign a call task to the owner AND notify the manager in Slack," you are outside what native Pipedrive can do. There is no branching. No wait step with a conditional check. No cross-object lookup that says "check if this contact also has an open deal in another pipeline."

You end up with reps manually checking dashboards to compensate. Which means they are not doing it.

The [Pipedrive automation limits post](/blog/2026-09-20-pipedrive-automation-in-2026-limits-triggers-and-workarounds/) covers the ceiling in detail. What I want to focus on here is the specific patterns that cause revenue to slip through, and how to close them systematically before you decide the tool itself is the problem.

Before layering in any tooling, answer these four questions about your current setup. Pull up your Pipedrive automation list and your last 90 days of closed-lost data side by side.

## Fixing the gaps: n8n vs. Make vs. switching

Once you have your audit output, the fix usually falls into one of three buckets. I will be direct about which is which.

It is usually what triggers the "let's just switch CRMs" conversation.

  <Fragment slot="right">

    // Node 1: Pipedrive - Get all open deals

    // Node 3: IF - deal.stage_id == "demo_done"
    //   YES branch: Slack notify + create call activity
    //   NO branch:  Send follow-up email via Smartlead
    // Node 4: Pipedrive - Update deal "stale_flag" field

[n8n](/go/n8n/) handles the branching problem cleanly. A scheduled workflow polls Pipedrive's API every four hours, filters stale deals, branches on stage, and fires the right action per branch. You can see a similar multi-branch pattern in our [n8n Pipedrive integration walkthrough](/blog/2026-09-19-connect-gmail-kixie-and-pipedrive-full-sdr-integration-setup/). Self-hosted n8n costs effectively nothing beyond the server. Cloud n8n starts at $20/month. For most teams, this is the right fix. Don't bother rebuilding your CRM data model when what you actually need is a conditional branch.

[Make](/go/make/) is the better choice if your team needs a visual canvas and is not comfortable maintaining a server environment. Make's Pipedrive module covers deal, contact, activity, and organization triggers with filter support. The Starter plan at $9/month handles up to 10,000 operations, which is enough for a 5 to 10 rep team. The tradeoff: complex conditional logic in Make gets visually noisy fast. Router modules with four or more paths become hard to audit six months later.

The [HubSpot](/go/hubspot/) switch makes sense in exactly one scenario. When you need marketing attribution (UTM source, ad click, content engagement) to live on the same object as the deal, natively, without a sync layer. HubSpot Workflows support branching, delays, enrollment re-triggers, and goal-based exits that Pipedrive genuinely cannot match. But you are paying $800 to $1,200/month for Professional and committing to a data model migration that will consume your ops bandwidth for a quarter. I've seen this go well. I've also seen it destroy team productivity for six months straight. The [HubSpot workflows comparison post](/blog/2026-09-18-hubspot-workflows-vs-kit-vs-make-best-alternative-2026/) covers the tradeoffs in detail.

## What to build first after your audit

If you have run the audit and confirmed n8n or Make is the right layer, prioritize these two workflows before anything else. They address the highest-frequency leak points.

**Stale deal detection.** Scheduled trigger every four hours, pull open deals, filter by last activity date greater than 14 days, branch on stage, create a call activity and Slack-notify the owner. In my testing, this single workflow recovers more pipeline value than any other automation you can bolt onto [Pipedrive](/tools/pipedrive/). Build it first.

**Email engagement bridge.** If your sequences run through [Smartlead](/go/smartlead/) or [Lemlist](/go/lemlist/), use their webhooks to fire into n8n or Make when a reply or no-reply event fires. The workflow then updates the Pipedrive deal stage and creates the appropriate next activity. This closes the gap between your outbound tool and your CRM that every Pipedrive team running external sequences has. The [best email automation for Pipedrive post](/blog/2026-09-22-best-email-automation-for-pipedrive-2026-picks/) covers the webhook setup for both tools.

## Fix the pipes before you repipe the building

The companies that switch CRMs cleanly are the ones who first squeezed every bit of value out of their current stack and hit a genuine ceiling. The ones who switch in frustration usually find that the new CRM has different automation gaps, not fewer. Run the four-question audit, map your top two leak points, and build the n8n or Make bridge workflow before you request a HubSpot demo. If you do that and still hit the ceiling, you will at least know exactly which HubSpot feature you are actually buying.
