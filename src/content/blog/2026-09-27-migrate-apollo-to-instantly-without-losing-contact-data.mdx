The trigger is almost always the same. Your [Apollo](/go/apollo/) invoice jumped because someone on the team exported 8,000 leads in a month and blew through the credit allotment. You pull up the pricing page, do the math for next quarter, and realize you are paying Apollo for both prospecting data and a sequencer you barely touch. [Instantly](/go/instantly/) does not sell data, so the per-contact cost disappears entirely. The migration itself is not complicated, but there are three specific places where teams lose data or burn their sender reputation by skipping steps. This guide covers all three.

## Before you migrate

Do not touch the export button until you have answered four questions. I have watched teams skip this prep and spend a full day untangling a mess that fifteen minutes of planning would have prevented.

- **Audit your Apollo sequences.** Go to Sequences in Apollo and screenshot or paste every step, including the delay days and the email copy. None of this exports anywhere useful.
- **Identify which contacts are actively in a sequence.** Export that segment separately. You do not want to re-enroll mid-sequence leads at Step 1 in Instantly.
- **Check your sending domains.** If you are using Gmail or Outlook inboxes connected to Apollo, confirm you know the SMTP credentials or can reconnect those mailboxes to Instantly. If Apollo was managing dedicated inboxes, you are starting fresh and need a warm-up period.
- **Note your unsubscribes and bounces.** Apollo tracks opt-outs per contact. Export the "unsubscribed" status column in your CSV and treat it as a suppression list in Instantly before any campaign goes live.

Pause all active Apollo sequences before you begin the export. Running sequences in both tools simultaneously will double-send to contacts and guarantee spam complaints.

## What breaks and what does not transfer

The contacts themselves travel cleanly. The behavioral data does not.

Apollo tracks opens, clicks, replies, and bounce history per contact. None of that comes over in the CSV export. Instantly starts a fresh engagement record for every lead. If you were using Apollo's intent signals or AI scores to prioritize follow-up, you lose that context the moment you leave. There is no workaround for this. Build your prioritization logic fresh inside Instantly using reply and click signals from the new campaigns.

Custom fields are the other common failure point. Apollo lets you store arbitrary data on contacts, but it exports those fields with whatever column header you assigned them inside Apollo. Instantly reads custom variables by the column name in your CSV. If your Apollo field is called "Pain Point" and your Instantly sequence template references `customField1`, the variable will render blank in every email.

If you need to enrich or transform fields at scale before import, [Clay](/go/clay/) is the right tool for this. Pipe your Apollo CSV into a Clay table, run enrichment to fill gaps, rename columns with a formula column, and export a clean Instantly-ready CSV. The [Clay enrichment waterfall](/tools/clay/) handles cases where Apollo data is stale, which is common for contacts exported from searches rather than saved lists. Skip building this in Google Sheets if you have more than 2,000 rows and more than five custom fields. It becomes brittle fast.

## Verify it worked

Do not call the migration complete until you have checked all four of these.

1. **Variable rendering:** Send a test email to a personal inbox from each active campaign. Confirm every merge field populates. An empty `{{firstName}}` in a live campaign is the fastest way to kill reply rates.
2. **Suppression coverage:** Search for three known unsubscribes from your Apollo export in Instantly's lead database. They should not appear as active leads in any campaign.
3. **Bounce rate after 48 hours:** Open Instantly's analytics for the first active campaign. Bounce rate above two percent means your list needs more verification before you continue.
4. **Inbox warm-up status:** In Instantly's Accounts tab, confirm each sending mailbox shows an active warm-up health score. A score below 70 out of 100 in the first week means you should throttle sending further.

If you are syncing contact status back to [HubSpot](/go/hubspot/), verify that the Instantly-HubSpot integration (via native connector or [n8n](/go/n8n/)) is writing reply and bounce events to the contact record. The [HubSpot](/tools/hubspot/) contact timeline should show Instantly activity within an hour of a reply landing. If it does not, your attribution will drift within a week. For more on keeping deliverability clean after a platform switch, the [cold email deliverability audit guide](/blog/2026-09-25-cold-email-deliverability-audit-fix-flagged-sequences/) covers the exact checks I run post-migration.

## The migration is four hours of work and two days of clock

The actual hands-on time is manageable. Four to six hours of real work spread across two days. The timeline stretches because warm-up is not optional and because you should run your first Instantly campaign in parallel with a reduced Apollo send before fully cutting over. Give yourself two days of overlap, watch the [Instantly](/tools/instantly/) analytics, and only then pause Apollo sequences permanently.

Trying to do this in a single afternoon almost always means sending from inboxes that are not ready. That costs you deliverability that takes weeks to recover. Be patient with the clock even when the actual work is done.
