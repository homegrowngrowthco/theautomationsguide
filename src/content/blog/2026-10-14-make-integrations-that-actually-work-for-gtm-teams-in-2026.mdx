Not every Make connector is worth trusting in production. Some modules are thin wrappers around a single endpoint. Others give you full object access and reliable error handling. Here is my read on the four connectors that pull the most weight for GTM teams.

Don't bother hunting for a one-size-fits-all connector. Pick the module that matches your system of record and build outward from there.

## The Two Workflows I'd Build First

If you're starting fresh with Make as the automation layer, don't boil the ocean. Two scenarios pay back their build time fastest: the Clay-to-Smartlead push and the Smartlead-reply-to-HubSpot sync. Everything else is secondary until those two are running cleanly.

  <Fragment slot="left">
    **The merge variable trap.** The single most common failure when routing Clay rows into Smartlead via Make is shipping leads with empty personalization fields. Clay's webhook payload uses the column header as the key. If your Smartlead campaign expects `firstName` but Clay sends `First Name` (with a space), the field arrives blank. Every lead goes out with a literal empty field instead of a name. Check the raw JSON in Make's execution log against Smartlead's expected variable names before activating the scenario. Per the [Smartlead API docs](https://api.smartlead.ai/reference/add-leads-to-a-campaign), variable names must match exactly.

## What Make Still Can't Do Well

Being honest about limits matters. Make's scenario execution is asynchronous, which means it's the wrong layer for anything requiring a sub-second response, like live chat routing or real-time lead scoring during a form submission. For those cases, native HubSpot workflows or [n8n](/tools/n8n/) with a self-hosted instance give you more control over latency. The [HubSpot workflows to n8n migration playbook](/blog/2026-10-07-hubspot-workflows-to-n8n-migration-playbook/) is worth reading if you're hitting execution timing issues with Make specifically.

Make also lacks a native [Apollo](/tools/apollo/) module as of mid-2026. Anything Apollo-adjacent runs through HTTP calls to their API. Manageable, but significantly more setup than the HubSpot or Pipedrive connectors require. If Apollo is central to your stack, I'd factor that build time into your decision.

For teams running cold email at scale, pairing Make with [Smartlead](/tools/smartlead/) and [Pipedrive](/tools/pipedrive/) covers the outbound handoff cleanly. The [best cold email stack under $300 post](/blog/2026-10-09-best-cold-email-stack-under-300-in-2026/) maps out what that full stack looks like with the cost breakdown alongside the tooling decisions.

<MyTake>
Most people shopping Make integrations focus on connector breadth. I'd argue that's the wrong thing to optimize for. The real edge Make has over Zapier or native automations is the execution log: granular, step-by-step visibility into exactly where a record failed and what the payload looked like. Teams that invest in their error paths (the Google Sheet log, the bounce suppression scenario, the failed-record alert) get dramatically more reliable workflows than teams chasing more connectors. Build fewer scenarios and instrument them properly. That's where the actual return lives.
</MyTake>

## The Integration Stack Worth Your Time

Make works best as the connective tissue between tools that each do one thing well. For GTM teams, that means [HubSpot](/go/hubspot/) as the record layer, [Clay](/go/clay/) doing enrichment, [Smartlead](/go/smartlead/) running outbound, and Pipedrive or HubSpot tracking pipeline movement. [Make](/tools/make/) scenarios wire those tools together without a developer, and the native modules for HubSpot and Pipedrive are mature enough to trust in production. The Clay and Smartlead integrations require more manual mapping work, but the patterns are repeatable once you've built them once. Start with the Clay-to-Smartlead push, get the reply webhook writing back to HubSpot, and you've covered the highest-value handoffs in a typical outbound GTM motion.
