## What Pabbly actually charges you

[Pabbly Connect](https://pabbly.com/connect/) sells two ways: monthly subscriptions and one-time lifetime deals. The monthly tiers are straightforward. The LTD is where the real story is.

The monthly plan structure, annotated for what actually matters:

- **Standard ($19/month):** 10,000 tasks. Fine for a single lightweight GTM workflow, but you will hit this ceiling fast if you are syncing CRM events, enriching contacts, or running multi-step sequences.
- **Rookie ($39/month):** 50,000 tasks. This is where most small RevOps shops should start monthly. It covers a HubSpot-to-Slack notification layer, a basic enrichment router, and a few form-to-CRM pipes running simultaneously.
- **Pro ($99/month):** 250,000 tasks. Agencies and mid-market ops teams. At this level you also get priority support, which matters when a broken webhook is silently dropping lead data at 2am.
- **Business ($149/month):** 1,000,000 tasks. At this price point, benchmark seriously against [n8n](/go/n8n/) self-hosted. A VPS on Railway or Render runs roughly $50 to 100 per month with no task ceiling whatsoever.

The LTD mirrors these tiers but permanently caps the ROI calculation in your favor. A $249 LTD pays back against the $19/month Standard plan in 14 months. Against the $39/month Rookie plan? Six months.

## The true cost: running the math for a real team

Let me use a concrete example. I work with a lot of B2B SaaS teams running a five-person GTM operation: one RevOps, one SDR manager, two AEs, and one marketing manager. Their automation footprint typically looks like:

- New HubSpot contact triggers an enrichment call (3 tasks per contact)
- Enriched contact routes to a sequence in [Instantly](/go/instantly/) (2 tasks)
- Slack notification fires on deal stage change (1 task per deal)
- Weekly CRM hygiene deduplication job (variable, roughly 5,000 tasks per month)
- Form-to-CRM sync for inbound (2 tasks per submission)

At 500 new contacts per month and moderate deal volume, this team runs around 35,000 to 40,000 tasks per month. On Make, that sits in the Team plan at $34/month for 10,000 ops. Make uses "operations" rather than "tasks," but they map roughly 1:1 for comparison purposes. Getting to 40,000 ops on Make costs $68 to 80 per month depending on how you handle overage. On Pabbly monthly, the Rookie tier at $39/month covers them with headroom.

On the LTD: a one-time $649 payment for the 50,000 task/month tier covers this team indefinitely. At $39/month equivalent, that LTD breaks even in 17 months and then runs free for the life of the business. If you expect to be running automation for more than two years (and you should), the LTD math is not even close.

One wrinkle worth calling out: Pabbly counts every action in a multi-step workflow individually. A five-step workflow processing 1,000 records costs 5,000 tasks, not 1,000. [Make's pricing page](https://www.make.com/en/pricing) counts the same way with operations, so the comparison holds, but it catches people off guard the first time they build a branching workflow in Pabbly.

## Where the pricing stings (and where it is actually generous)

The generous part is obvious: unlimited seats and unlimited workflows on every plan is genuinely unusual. [Make](/tools/make/) charges per operation and carries no seat fees, but Zapier taxes you hard on both task volume and user count at higher tiers. Pabbly's decision to decouple seats from pricing means a RevOps lead can share a workspace with the SDR manager, marketing, and a contractor without the bill scaling.

The sting is task ceiling opacity. Pabbly's interface does not surface real-time task consumption in an obvious dashboard widget the way Make does with its scenario usage graph. I've had clients burn through their monthly allowance mid-month on a runaway loop and not notice until workflows started silently failing. Set up a Slack alert for task usage via [Pabbly's API](https://pabbly.com/connect/), or check the usage report weekly. It is not hard to build, but it is not automatic. Don't wait for a silent failure to discover you're at 98%.

The second sting is app coverage. If you are running sequences through [Smartlead](/go/smartlead/) or enriching via [Clay](/go/clay/), you are almost certainly relying on HTTP Request nodes and webhooks rather than native connectors. That works fine, but it adds build time and debugging complexity. For the GTM stack I described above, roughly 30% of connections end up webhook-native rather than pre-built. On Make, that number drops to around 10%.

For a broader view of how automation pricing compares across the category, the [GetResponse pricing breakdown](/blog/2026-09-08-getresponse-pricing-in-2026-plans-limits-and-when-to-switch/) and [Relevance AI pricing post](/blog/2026-09-03-relevance-ai-pricing-in-2026-agent-credits-seats-and-roi/) on this site use the same true-cost methodology if you want a framework for evaluating other tools in your stack.

## The bottom line on Pabbly pricing

Pabbly Connect wins on cost for any team with more than a year of automation runway and task volume above 10,000 per month. The LTD eliminates the monthly overhead entirely. The no-seat-fee model is genuinely rare. The core workflow builder handles 80% of what RevOps teams actually need. [n8n](/tools/n8n/) self-hosted beats it on task limits if you have the technical appetite, and [Activepieces](/tools/activepieces/) is worth watching as an open-source alternative. But for a non-technical RevOps team that wants a hosted platform with a one-time payment? Pabbly is the right call.
