## The symptom-first framework for deliverability diagnosis

Most RevOps operators treat deliverability as a binary: either emails land or they do not. The reality is a gradient, and each symptom maps to a different layer of the stack. Before you touch a single sequence setting, match what you are observing to where the failure is actually happening.

Here are the five layers I work through, in order, every time a sequence underperforms. My opinionated take: if you are not starting with Google Postmaster before anything else, you are debugging blind.

## The infrastructure layer: where Mailforge and ZeroBounce fit

The mistake I see most often when teams compare sales engagement platforms for cold email is treating [Smartlead](/go/smartlead/) or [Instantly](/go/instantly/) as the whole stack. They are not. The sending platform sits on top of an infrastructure layer you also need to manage deliberately.

[Mailforge](/go/mailforge/) is where I provision sending domains and inboxes when I need more than five or six accounts. It handles domain registration, Google Workspace or Microsoft 365 provisioning, and basic DNS record setup in bulk. What it does not do is warm those inboxes or manage sequence logic. That is Smartlead's job. Keep those responsibilities clearly separated or you will spend hours debugging a problem that is actually a process gap.

**The trap:** Provisioning 20 Mailforge inboxes, connecting them to Smartlead, and launching sequences on day one. Fresh inboxes with zero warmup sending at full volume look exactly like a spam operation to Google's filters, because functionally they are. This can obliterate an entire sending domain within weeks.

  <Fragment slot="right">
```
Mailforge inbox provisioning checklist:
1. Register domain (age it 7+ days before any send)
2. Set SPF, DKIM, DMARC on each domain
3. Connect inbox to Smartlead warmup pool
4. Run warmup at 3-5 emails/day for 21 days minimum
5. Check Smartlead health score > 80 before sequence launch
6. Cap daily sends at 30-40/inbox for first 30 days
```
  </Fragment>
</SideBySide>

[ZeroBounce](/go/zerobounce/) belongs at the front of this pipeline, not as an afterthought. Their [email validation API](https://www.zerobounce.net/docs/) integrates directly with most [Clay](/go/clay/) workflows, so you can validate at enrichment time rather than before upload. Running validation at enrichment rather than at campaign launch is the single highest-impact deliverability improvement I have made in my own outbound. Do it there, not as a last-minute scrub.

## Fix the foundation before you optimize the message

The [Litmus 2024 State of Email](https://www.litmus.com/resources/state-of-email) found that sender reputation accounts for more inbox placement variance than content filtering in most major ISPs. If you are seeing flat open rates, run the diagnostic stack in order: Postmaster reputation check, ZeroBounce list scrub, Smartlead per-inbox health audit, DNS alignment verification, then warmup review. Only after you have cleared all of that should you look at your copy. The [Smartlead](/tools/smartlead/) platform gives you more diagnostic surface area for that process than any other tool in this category, in my view. Pair it with [Mailforge](/tools/mailforge/) for infrastructure and [ZeroBounce](/tools/zerobounce/) for list hygiene and you have a stack that surfaces the actual problem instead of masking it with a sharper subject line.
