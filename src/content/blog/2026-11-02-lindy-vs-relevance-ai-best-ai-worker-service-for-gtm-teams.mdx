## What each platform is actually built for

The positioning sounds similar on both landing pages: deploy AI workers that handle repetitive GTM tasks so your human team can focus on higher-value work. But the underlying architectures pull in different directions.

[Lindy](/tools/lindy/) is opinionated. Its AI workers are purpose-built agents with defined inputs, outputs, and integrations. You pick a template (say, "SDR Agent" or "Meeting Notetaker"), connect your tools (Gmail, HubSpot, Slack), configure a handful of parameters, and you are live. The tradeoff is real: Lindy's agents do what Lindy designed them to do. If your workflow deviates from the template, you are working against the grain almost immediately.

[Relevance AI](/tools/relevance-ai/) is a builder. You get a canvas where you assemble tools, prompts, data sources, and sub-agents into custom workflows. A manager agent can receive a lead from a webhook, assign a research sub-agent to pull firmographic data, pass that to a writing sub-agent to draft a personalized email, and then trigger an action in your CRM. That is not a hypothetical; it is the documented capability per [Relevance AI's multi-agent docs](https://relevanceai.com/docs). The tradeoff is setup time. Expect days to a week per workflow the first time through, not hours.

For teams that have already compared what [GTM workflow automation can do that Zapier cannot](/blog/2026-10-25-what-gtm-workflow-automation-does-that-zapier-cannot/), Relevance AI sits firmly in the "real orchestration" tier. Lindy sits closer to a managed Zapier with a language model bolted on. That is a compliment for some teams and a hard ceiling for others.

## How to choose

The honest version of this decision comes down to one question: does your GTM team have someone who can read an API doc and write a JSON payload without needing a tutorial? If yes, Relevance AI's ceiling is meaningfully higher and the setup cost is worth it. If no, Lindy will ship faster and stick, and you can always migrate up later when the function matures.

I'd also weight your workflow complexity heavily here. If you need an AI worker to handle a single, linear task (qualify inbound leads, book meetings, update CRM fields after a call), Lindy's templates are genuinely good and the configuration overhead is low. If you need an agent that researches a target account, cross-references intent signals from a tool like [Clay](/tools/clay/), drafts a multi-touch sequence, and routes different personas to different playbooks, Relevance AI's multi-agent model is closer to what you actually need. Trying to bolt that complexity onto Lindy's template structure is a common failure mode when teams pick based on logo recognition rather than workflow fit.

One more thing worth flagging: [Bardeen](/tools/bardeen/) comes up frequently in this conversation because it sits in a related space, but it is really a browser-automation and on-demand enrichment tool more than an always-on AI worker service. If your team's core need is scraping LinkedIn or triggering enrichment flows from a browser extension, Bardeen is the better fit than either of these platforms. Don't buy a Relevance AI subscription to do what a $10/month Bardeen plan handles fine.

## The bottom line on building AI worker services in 2026

Both tools are legitimate, and both are improving faster than most RevOps teams can evaluate them. The category is real: [G2's AI agent research](https://www.g2.com/categories/ai-agents) shows significant adoption growth among mid-market B2B teams in 2025 and 2026. My read is that the teams getting the most out of these platforms are not the ones who picked the "best" tool on paper. They are the ones who scoped their first agent tightly, got it working, and iterated from there. Whether that first agent lives in Lindy or Relevance AI matters less than whether it actually runs reliably in production.
