## What each platform actually does to your workflows

I want to be specific here, because vendor marketing pages all say "powerful automation." Here is what these platforms actually do when you push them.

[ActiveCampaign](/go/activecampaign/) gives you the most expressive automation canvas of the three. You can stack goal conditions, run split tests inside automations, and set "waits" that respond to contact behavior rather than fixed timers. The CRM (Deals) is functional but thin. Where I've seen it shine: lifecycle nurture sequences that branch simultaneously on CRM stage, lead score, and last email interaction. Where it breaks: reporting. The reporting UI is fragmented, and cross-automation attribution requires either their Attribution add-on or a BI layer like Looker piped in from your warehouse.

[HubSpot](/go/hubspot/) is the only platform here where a RevOps operator can sit in one tab and see automation enrollment, deal stage movement, sequence performance, and revenue attribution without exporting a single CSV. The workflow builder is less expressive than ActiveCampaign on pure conditional logic, but the data context it operates on is richer. A HubSpot workflow can branch on deal properties, meeting booked status, and contact lifecycle stage because all of that data lives natively. When my clients ask which platform has the least duct tape, the answer is always HubSpot, assuming they can stomach the pricing.

[Brevo](/go/brevo/) positions itself as HubSpot's affordable alternative, but that framing undersells how different the product philosophy actually is. Brevo is volume-first. The automation builder handles straightforward drip sequences and transactional triggers well. It falls apart on anything requiring multi-object logic (contact plus company plus deal) because Brevo's data model is contact-flat. Teams I've worked with that started on Brevo for "just newsletter and onboarding" invariably outgrow it the moment sales gets involved and wants CRM-linked triggers.

## How to choose without regretting it

The decision comes down to one question before everything else: does your sales team live in this platform or not? If sales touches the tool, HubSpot is the only serious answer in this set. The moment a rep needs to enroll a contact in a sequence, log a call, or see contact history, you need CRM-native automation. Full stop.

Bolting ActiveCampaign onto a separate CRM works, but it creates sync lag, attribution gaps, and a support ticket every time field mapping drifts. I've debugged those gaps at two different clients. Neither of them would make the same call again. The sync problem is not a setup issue; it is a structural one. Two systems with overlapping contact records will diverge. Budget for the maintenance or avoid the architecture.

If sales is running in a dedicated CRM like [Salesforce](/tools/salesforce/) or [Pipedrive](/tools/pipedrive/) and marketing just needs a best-in-class nurture and lifecycle engine to sync data back, ActiveCampaign is the smart play. You get more automation flexibility than HubSpot's workflow builder for roughly 20% of the cost, and you keep your CRM of record intact. The integration story between ActiveCampaign and Salesforce via native connector or [n8n](/tools/n8n/) is solid enough that the sync gap is manageable with a competent RevOps setup. For context on the CRM-side decision, our [Pipedrive vs Attio breakdown](/blog/2026-08-30-pipedrive-vs-attio-which-crm-wins-for-gtm-teams-in-2026/) covers the standalone CRM angle well.

Brevo belongs in a specific scenario: PLG or e-commerce adjacent companies with a massive contact list, low send frequency, and simple drip logic. In pure email-cost terms, Brevo's pricing model wins at 500k or more contacts where HubSpot's per-contact tiers become genuinely painful. For most mid-market B2B SaaS teams, that scenario does not apply. You hit the automation ceiling before you reach the contact pricing inflection point where Brevo becomes cheaper. I've seen teams talk themselves into Brevo over a $300/mo difference and then spend $15k in ops hours rebuilding everything on ActiveCampaign eight months later.

<MyTake>
Brevo is not a budget alternative to HubSpot. It is a different product category that gets sold as one. Every mid-market team I've seen start on Brevo "to save money" ends up paying for the migration twice: once in tool costs and once in the ops hours spent rebuilding workflows they should have built right the first time. If budget is the real constraint, ActiveCampaign at $49/mo is the actual smart call. Brevo at $65/mo is a short-term save that creates a long-term project.
</MyTake>

## The automation spine you will actually keep

This is not a close race for mid-market B2B teams with any sales complexity. HubSpot is the platform you build on when you want to stop rebuilding. According to [HubSpot's own product documentation](https://www.hubspot.com/pricing/marketing), Marketing Hub Professional starts at $800/mo for 2,000 contacts, which sounds steep until you price out the BI tool, CRM sync, and attribution layer you would need to replicate that reporting elsewhere. ActiveCampaign is the platform you build on when you want HubSpot-grade automation logic without HubSpot's price tag and you already have a CRM. Brevo is a tactical email tool that gets stretched into a role it was not designed for.

The question of which platform owns your workflows without forcing migration pain has a cleaner answer than most vendor comparisons admit. HubSpot owns your workflows because it is the hardest to leave. That is a feature, not a problem. If you are not ready for that commitment, ActiveCampaign gives you the most migration-friendly middle ground. And per [G2's marketing automation category data](https://www.g2.com/categories/marketing-automation), both HubSpot and ActiveCampaign consistently outrank Brevo on satisfaction scores for teams above 50 employees, which tracks with what I see in practice.

For teams already deep in the HubSpot ecosystem and considering a move, the switching calculus only gets more expensive over time. The [migration cost breakdown we covered here](/blog/2026-08-25-migrate-pipedrive-to-hubspot-without-wrecking-your-pipeline/) applies equally in reverse.
