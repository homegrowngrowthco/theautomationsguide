Here is a failure mode I see consistently across mid-market RevOps builds. A team buys [Lusha](/go/lusha/) on the promise of fast contact data, runs one enrichment pass against a 5,000-row import, watches the credit balance crater, and then realizes the connected sequences are firing on records where maybe 40 percent of the emails will actually deliver. The credits are gone. The bounce rate is climbing. The pipeline movement that justified the spend is nowhere in sight. The fix is not to buy more credits. It is to rethink the data layer entirely.

## The actual problem with how Lusha bills

Lusha's credit model is straightforward on the surface: each reveal costs one credit. The trap is that "reveal" means the attempt, not the result. Query a record with an outdated title, a company that pivoted, or a personal email Lusha does not have indexed, and the credit is gone. For a curated list of warm inbound leads, that model is fine. For a cold prospecting list pulled from a ZoomInfo export or a scrape, you are essentially paying to discover what Lusha does not know.

Per [Lusha's published pricing page](https://www.lusha.com/pricing/), the free tier caps at 480 credits per year, and anything meaningful in bulk requires a paid plan. Their Teams plan runs roughly $49 per user per month, and credits do not roll over on annual billing in the way most teams assume. A 3-seat team running 2,000 enrichment attempts per month will hit that wall fast.

The downstream damage compounds. [G2 reviewers on Lusha](https://www.g2.com/products/lusha/reviews) consistently flag mobile number accuracy and email freshness as the two biggest pain points. Stale emails hitting sequences is not just a wasted credit problem. It is a sender reputation problem. If you are running outbound through [Smartlead](/go/smartlead/) or [Instantly](/go/instantly/) and your bounce rate spikes above 3 percent, you are in deliverability recovery mode, which costs far more than the credits did. I covered the deliverability stakes in detail in the [Instantly vs Smartlead comparison](/blog/2026-10-06-instantly-vs-smartlead-deliverability-domains-roi/).

My read is that most teams underestimate the compounding effect. You do not just lose the credit. You degrade the sending infrastructure that everything else depends on.

## The waterfall enrichment alternatives worth running

The logic of waterfall enrichment is simple. Try the cheapest provider first. Fall through to the next on a miss. Stop when you get a verified hit. You pay for success, not attempts. The four tools below are the ones I would actually evaluate in a RevOps stack audit.

Don't bother trying to replicate waterfall logic manually with two separate Lusha-style tools. The coordination overhead eats any savings. Use a purpose-built waterfall layer from day one.

## The build that actually stops credit drain

If I were scoping a fix for a team bleeding Lusha credits, here is the sequencing I would recommend. It is not complicated, but the order matters.

  {

  {

<MyTake>
The "which enrichment tool is most accurate" question is the wrong frame entirely. Accuracy varies by industry, geography, and company size across every provider. The billing model is what actually determines your unit economics. Any tool that charges on attempt rather than verified result will drain budget on cold lists, Lusha or otherwise. Waterfall tools win not because their data is universally superior but because their billing aligns with outcomes rather than activity. Optimizing for database coverage before fixing the cost model is backwards.
</MyTake>

## Stop optimizing credits, start optimizing verified contacts

The unit that matters is not cost-per-credit. It is cost-per-verified-contact that enters a sequence and does not bounce. Lusha's credit model made sense when teams were doing targeted lookups on warm accounts. It breaks down at scale on cold lists, which is where most outbound motion lives in 2026.

The waterfall alternatives above are not just cheaper on paper. They are cheaper where it counts: in the ratio of deliverable emails to dollars spent. Pick the layer that matches your volume and your technical appetite, gate your sequence enrollment on verification status, and the credit problem becomes a data quality advantage.
