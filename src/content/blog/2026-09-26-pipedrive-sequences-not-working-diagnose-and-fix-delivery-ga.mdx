You enroll 200 contacts into a [Pipedrive](/go/pipedrive/) sequence on Monday. By Friday, your sent count is 23. No errors surface in the UI. The sequence shows "active." Nothing in the activity log explains why 177 contacts are just sitting there. I've seen this exact scenario more times than I'd like to admit, and the silence is the hardest part. Pipedrive doesn't tell you it stopped. It just quietly stops.

This post is a symptom-first diagnostic. We'll work through the four most common failure modes, with specific fixes for each. And if the root cause turns out to be a platform ceiling rather than a configuration error, I'll be direct about when it's time to look at alternatives.

## The Four Failure Modes (and How to Confirm Each)

Before touching any setting, pull your sequence activity log. In Pipedrive, go to Activities > Email and filter by the sequence name and the last 7 days. If you see zero sent emails but the sequence is active, you have a systemic block, not a contact-level skip.

### Failure Mode 1: Sender Authentication is Broken

This is the silent killer. Your emails are sending from Pipedrive's perspective, but Gmail and Outlook are rejecting or junking them on arrival. Check your domain's SPF, DKIM, and DMARC records using [MXToolbox's email header analyzer](https://mxtoolbox.com/EmailHeaders.aspx). If DKIM is failing, your connected email provider (Google Workspace, Outlook 365) needs to re-authorize Pipedrive.

The fix: disconnect and reconnect your mailbox under Settings > Email Sync. If you rotated your Google Workspace password or revoked app permissions recently, the OAuth token is dead and Pipedrive will not tell you.

### Failure Mode 2: Daily Sending Limits

Gmail free accounts cap at 500 total outbound emails per day. Google Workspace caps at 2,000. Pipedrive draws from that same pool whether the send is manual or automated. If your reps are sending bulk manual emails on top of sequences, you may be hitting the ceiling before noon.

The short-term fix: stagger your sequence send times across multiple connected mailboxes. The real fix is dedicated sending infrastructure, which Pipedrive does not provide. This is where tools like [Smartlead](/go/smartlead/) earn their keep. Rotating mailboxes with built-in warm-up is something Pipedrive simply was not designed to do.

### Failure Mode 3: Enrollment Filter Bugs

Pipedrive sequences use deal or contact conditions to enroll records. I've seen enrollment conditions that look correct but silently exclude almost everyone because of a label mismatch: a trailing space in a tag value, a pipeline stage that was renamed while the filter still references the old name, or an "and" condition that should be "or."

Test this by manually enrolling a single contact you know should qualify. If manual enrollment works but automatic does not, the condition logic is the problem. Rebuild the filter from scratch rather than editing the existing one. Pipedrive's condition editor has a known quirk where edited filters do not always save the updated logic reliably. This ties into broader [Pipedrive automation bugs worth auditing](/blog/2026-09-23-pipedrive-automations-are-leaking-deals-audit-and-fix-the-ga/).

### Failure Mode 4: Missing OAuth Scope on the Connected Mailbox

When you connect a mailbox to Pipedrive, it requests specific scopes from Google or Microsoft. If an admin later restricts app permissions, or you connected before Pipedrive added the "send on behalf of" scope, sequences queue but never fire.

Confirm this by going to Settings > Email Sync > Connected accounts and checking the last-sync timestamp. If it is more than 24 hours stale on an account that should be active, the token is broken.

## The Deliverability Problem Pipedrive Cannot Solve

Here is where I have to be direct. Fix all four failure modes and sequences are still underperforming on opens and replies? You have a deliverability problem that Pipedrive is structurally unable to help you diagnose. There is no bounce-rate dashboard. No inbox placement score. No warm-up tooling. You are flying blind.

For deliverability visibility, the fastest path is to run your sequences through a dedicated tool and keep Pipedrive as your CRM. This is also a good time to run a full [cold email deliverability audit](/blog/2026-09-25-cold-email-deliverability-audit-fix-flagged-sequences/) if you have not done one recently.

## When to Stop Fixing and Start Migrating

If you have worked through all four failure modes and the root cause is a platform ceiling (no multi-mailbox rotation, no deliverability tooling, no sequence analytics beyond basic open/click), that is a signal, not a configuration error. Here is how the alternatives stack up for teams hitting Pipedrive's ceiling.

Don't bother trying to patch Pipedrive with Zapier workarounds for deliverability. Use [Reply.io](/go/reply-io/) or [Smartlead](/go/smartlead/) for the actual sending and let Pipedrive stay in its lane as a CRM.

## Fix the Config, Then Reassess the Platform

Run the six-step diagnostic before making any tool decisions. Most teams find one of the four failure modes explains their delivery gap, and a two-hour fix is faster than a three-month migration. But if you fix everything and sequences are still underperforming, you are not dealing with a broken config. You are dealing with a tool that was not built for what you are asking it to do. That is a different problem. It deserves a different solution.
