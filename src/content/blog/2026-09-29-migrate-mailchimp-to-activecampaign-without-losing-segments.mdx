## Before you migrate

This is where most teams underinvest, and it's why their segments look wrong six weeks later. Do not touch ActiveCampaign until you have a complete, audited export of your Mailchimp account. Full stop.

Here is exactly what to pull before you do anything else:

- **All Audiences, all statuses.** Export each audience separately from Mailchimp's Audience dashboard with the "All subscribers" filter set to include subscribed, unsubscribed, non-subscribed, and cleaned. Each export gives you a CSV with email, status, tags, and any merge fields you have set up.
- **Segment definitions, documented.** Mailchimp segments are dynamic queries, not static lists. Screenshot or write out the filter logic for every segment you care about. You will rebuild these as ActiveCampaign segments or tag-based lists on the other side.
- **Your automation map.** Go into Mailchimp's Automations, open each active journey, and screenshot the logic: trigger, wait steps, branch conditions, email sends. Export the email HTML too. This becomes your rebuild spec.
- **Domain authentication records.** Note your existing SPF, DKIM, and DMARC records. ActiveCampaign will give you new DKIM CNAME records to add. Do not remove Mailchimp's records until after your ActiveCampaign deliverability is confirmed.
- **Tag taxonomy.** Export your tag list and decide which tags map to ActiveCampaign tags versus which become custom fields or list membership. Mailchimp tags are flat. ActiveCampaign has tags, custom fields, AND lists. Collapsing all Mailchimp tags into AC tags works fine, but skipping this planning step causes chaos at import time.

My strong recommendation here: spend half a day on the pre-export audit rather than half a week untangling a botched import. The audit is the unglamorous part that determines whether the whole migration holds.

## What breaks and what does not transfer

Most teams are surprised that the contact data itself survives fine. The pain is structural and behavioral. Here is where the wheels come off if you are not prepared.

<SideBySide>
  <Fragment slot="left">
    **What breaks or needs manual rebuild**

    - Mailchimp's "Groups" have no direct AC equivalent. Groups become tags or custom field values depending on whether they are opt-in choices or segmentation metadata.
    - Mailchimp's RSS-to-email campaign type does not exist in ActiveCampaign. Rebuild it as an RSS trigger automation.
    - Engagement segments like "Opened in last 30 days" in Mailchimp rely on Mailchimp's own engagement data. ActiveCampaign starts tracking engagement from import day zero, so your existing engagement scores reset entirely.
    - Any Mailchimp-specific integrations (Shopify's Mailchimp app, for example) need to be swapped for ActiveCampaign's native integrations or rebuilt via Make.

One thing worth flagging specifically: Mailchimp's "Predicted Demographics" and "Customer Lifetime Value" data do not export. That enrichment is proprietary to Mailchimp. If your segments rely on those fields, you will need to rebuild equivalent scoring in ActiveCampaign using behavioral triggers, or pull enrichment from an external tool like Clearbit or Apollo. The [migrate from ActiveCampaign to Brevo guide](/blog/2026-09-26-migrate-activecampaign-to-brevo-without-losing-your-automati/) covers the reverse of this problem and has a solid section on preserving behavioral scores that applies here too.

## Verify it worked

Do not declare victory until you have confirmed all four of these. I mean actually confirmed, not eyeballed.

1. **Authentication check.** Send a test email to a [Mail-Tester](https://www.mail-tester.com/) address and confirm your score is 9/10 or higher. Anything below that is a DKIM or DMARC misconfiguration and needs to be fixed before you send to your real list.
2. **Unsubscribe suppression confirmed.** Take five email addresses you know were unsubscribed in Mailchimp, search for them in ActiveCampaign, and verify their status shows as unsubscribed. Not just tagged. Actually unsubscribed.
3. **Segment counts match.** Compare contact counts in your key ActiveCampaign segments against the Mailchimp segment counts you documented before export. A 2 to 5 percent variance is normal due to duplicates resolved on import. More than 10 percent means you have a mapping issue and need to trace it before you send anything.
4. **Automation enrollment working.** Manually trigger a test contact through each rebuilt automation and confirm the steps fire correctly, including wait timers, conditional branches, and email sends.

<MyTake>
Most teams go into this migration thinking their biggest risk is losing contacts. It is not. The real trap is that Mailchimp's proprietary engagement data, predicted demographics, customer lifetime value scores, quietly props up your best segments, and you only discover that two weeks post-cutover when your "engaged subscribers" segment comes up nearly empty in ActiveCampaign. Run both platforms through at least one full send cycle before you pull the plug. The contacts you think are engaged need time to prove it to a system that has never seen them before.
</MyTake>

## Give it the time it needs

[ActiveCampaign](/tools/activecampaign/) is meaningfully better than Mailchimp for any team that needs automation depth, contact scoring, or sales CRM integration. The [ActiveCampaign documentation on importing contacts](https://help.activecampaign.com/hc/en-us/articles/207629386-How-to-import-contacts-to-your-account) is solid and covers the status mapping in detail. The [Mailchimp export documentation](https://mailchimp.com/help/view-export-options-for-your-mailchimp-data/) covers every export type you need. Read both before you start.

The migration is straightforward if you respect the order of operations: unsubscribes first, segmented imports second, automation rebuilds third, Make sync running throughout, DNS cutover last. Using [Make](/tools/make/) as your transition bridge is what keeps new subscribers from falling into a gap while your forms and integrations catch up. For teams also evaluating simpler alternatives, the [GetResponse to Kit migration guide](/blog/2026-09-28-migrate-from-getresponse-to-kit-without-losing-your-automati/) covers comparable sequencing challenges on a different platform pair and is worth reading before you finalize your stack decision.
