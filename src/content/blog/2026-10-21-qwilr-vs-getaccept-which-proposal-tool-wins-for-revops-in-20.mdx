What used to be a category defined by PDF templates and e-signature bolt-ons has fractured into two distinct philosophies: the "beautiful document" camp and the "buyer engagement" camp. ## What each tool is actually built for

These two tools look similar in a features matrix but diverge sharply in philosophy. Qwilr is a content and design tool that happens to do e-signatures. GetAccept is a buyer engagement platform that happens to produce documents. That framing matters more than any individual feature comparison.

I'd argue most teams get this backwards. They evaluate proposal tools by counting features rather than asking which philosophy matches how their buyers actually behave. Pick the wrong philosophy and you'll have a tool your reps technically use but that doesn't move deals any faster.

## How to choose

The first question I'd ask any team evaluating these two: what does your post-proposal period actually look like? If a rep sends a proposal and then waits for an email reply, Qwilr's analytics are informative but the deal room structure of GetAccept is overkill. If your deals involve champion-to-committee selling, multiple revision rounds, and a legal approval layer, GetAccept's persistent deal room maps to that reality far more closely than a beautiful web page.

The second question is integration depth. Both tools connect to [HubSpot](/tools/hubspot/) and [Salesforce](/tools/salesforce/), but the quality of those integrations differs in practice. Qwilr's HubSpot integration syncs proposal status and view events cleanly. GetAccept's integration runs more bidirectionally at the deal room level: stakeholder engagement events can trigger deal stage updates and enrollment in HubSpot sequences. For RevOps teams building automated follow-up flows off proposal engagement signals, that depth matters. My read is that GetAccept's integration is meaningfully better for teams already doing serious workflow automation in their CRM. Qwilr is sufficient for teams whose proposal-to-close handoff is still mostly manual.

It's also worth considering [PandaDoc](/tools/pandadoc/) as a third option. The PandaDoc plus HubSpot combination is well-documented for teams with heavy form-based workflows or a need for a large out-of-the-box template library. PandaDoc sits between Qwilr and GetAccept on the design-versus-engagement spectrum, which makes it the default safe choice but rarely the best choice for either end of that spectrum. Don't buy PandaDoc because it feels familiar. Buy it only if your workflow is genuinely form-heavy and template-volume matters more than deal room engagement.

## The bottom line

Both Qwilr and GetAccept have outgrown the "fancy PDF" category. Qwilr earns the nod for high-velocity SaaS teams with strong brand standards and shorter deal cycles. GetAccept earns it for field sales, enterprise, and any motion where multi-stakeholder buying groups and longer deal cycles make buyer engagement data actually actionable.

Neither is a wrong choice. They serve genuinely different sales motions. The common failure is buying the more expensive, feature-dense tool (GetAccept) for a team whose process doesn't create the conditions to use those features. A sales team that closes in two weeks and has one decision-maker in the deal will get no value from mutual action plans or stakeholder-level engagement tracking. Match the tool to your actual deal complexity, not your aspirational one.
