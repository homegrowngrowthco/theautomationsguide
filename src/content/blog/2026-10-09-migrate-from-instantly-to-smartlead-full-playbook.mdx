## Before you migrate

Do not pull the plug on Instantly the moment you decide to move. A rushed cutover is the fastest way to damage domains that are already stressed. The prep phase is where the migration actually succeeds or fails.

I'd argue most teams underinvest here and then wonder why deliverability dips post-launch. Spend the time. Here is the pre-migration checklist to run through before touching anything in Smartlead:

- **Pause all active sequences in Instantly.** Do not let contacts receive step 3 from Instantly while you are importing them into Smartlead.
- **Export all active campaign data** (Instantly Settings > Campaigns > Export). Grab the analytics CSV for each campaign you want to preserve.
- **Export your lead lists.** From each campaign, export the contact CSV with status columns (replied, bounced, unsubscribed) intact. These statuses matter for suppression.
- **Document your sequence logic.** Copy every step, delay, and condition to a spreadsheet or Notion doc. You will rebuild these manually.
- **Audit your domains and mailboxes.** List every sending domain and its associated mailboxes. Note which ones show soft-bounce rates above 3% or which have already been flagged. Those should not transfer. Retire them.
- **Check DNS records** (SPF, DKIM, DMARC) for every domain. Smartlead will require valid authentication before you can send. Per [Google's email sender guidelines](https://support.google.com/mail/answer/81126), DMARC at minimum policy `p=none` is required for bulk senders above 5,000 messages per day.

## What breaks and what does not transfer

Most migration pain lives in three areas: sequence continuity, sending history, and warmup state. Understanding what actually moves versus what does not will save you from discovering the gaps after you have already relaunched.

    **The usual gotcha:** Teams assume that because their mailboxes were warmed in Instantly, they carry a "warmed" status that transfers to Smartlead. They do not. Warmup reputation is network-specific. Instantly's warmup pool and Smartlead's warmup pool are separate ecosystems. Reconnecting a mailbox to Smartlead and immediately sending at full volume is the single most common reason migrations cause a deliverability dip rather than a recovery.

## Verify it worked

Before you ramp volume, run through these four checks. Skipping even one is how teams end up diagnosing deliverability problems two weeks post-migration.

- **Authenticate every domain.** Use [MXToolbox](https://mxtoolbox.com/SuperTool.aspx) to confirm SPF, DKIM, and DMARC are resolving correctly for every sending domain. Smartlead will show a green checkmark in the Email Accounts panel when DKIM is confirmed, but spot-check MXToolbox independently anyway.
- **Send a test sequence.** Create a single-contact test campaign in Smartlead using an internal inbox you control. Run a two-step sequence and confirm both emails land in the primary inbox (not spam or promotions) on Gmail and Outlook.
- **Confirm suppression is active.** Search for a known bounced or unsubscribed email from your Instantly export in Smartlead's lead list. It should not appear in any active campaign. If it does, your pre-import filtering missed rows.
- **Check warmup is running.** Go to Email Accounts in Smartlead and confirm the warmup status shows "Active" for every mailbox. The daily warmup email count should be incrementing. If it shows paused or zero, re-enable it before you touch live sends.

## Make the move cleanly

Migrating from Instantly to Smartlead is a three-phase operation: export and pause, reconnect and re-warm, then relaunch conservatively. The teams that get into trouble are the ones who rush the warmup phase or skip the suppression filter on import. Give the warmup pool two weeks to establish reputation on Smartlead's network before you push volume. The platform's deliverability controls are genuinely better. They only work if you give the infrastructure time to build the signal it needs.
