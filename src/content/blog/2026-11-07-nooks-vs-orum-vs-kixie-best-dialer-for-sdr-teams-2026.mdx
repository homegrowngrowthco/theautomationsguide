## How to choose

The first thing I'd check is your team's actual constraint. Not the one in the board deck. The real one.

If reps are spending more than 30 percent of their dial time hitting voicemails and bad numbers, the throughput problem is real. Orum's parallel dialer solves it at scale, and the [35% connect rate lift](https://www.orum.com/resources) Orum cites in published case studies is the kind of number that moves pipeline math fast. If the problem is that reps go off-script and conversion rates are inconsistent across the team, the Nooks coaching layer addresses that more directly than a faster dialer will. And if the problem is that call activity is not logging properly into your CRM and leadership has no visibility into rep behavior, that is a CRM plumbing problem. Kixie's native integrations fix it without an enterprise negotiation.

Budget is often the actual deciding factor, and that is not a cop-out. Nooks and Orum are both enterprise-priced tools with custom contracts and minimums that do not make sense for teams under 15 reps. Kixie's published Professional pricing starts around $65 per seat per month billed annually. That is a real number you can put in a spreadsheet and defend in a board meeting. If you are evaluating this alongside other outbound channel spend like [cold email tools](/blog/2026-11-06-lemlist-vs-brevo-vs-smartlead-best-cold-email-tool-roi/), that budget clarity matters for planning the full stack.

## The right dialer is a stack decision, not a feature checklist

Dialers do not operate in isolation. The value of any of these tools is a function of what your CRM, sequencer, and enrichment layer look like. A fast parallel dialer pointed at a stale list is worse than a slower dialer on a clean one. Full stop.

If your contact data is coming from a waterfall enrichment setup (similar to what I covered in [the enrichment workflow breakdown](/blog/2026-11-03-does-surfe-use-waterfall-enrichment-how-it-actually-works/)), the dialer is the last mile, not the foundation. Get the data quality and CRM plumbing right first. Then pick the dialer that fits your volume and coaching culture.
