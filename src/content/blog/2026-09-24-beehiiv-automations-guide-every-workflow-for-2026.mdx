## The native beehiiv automation stack you should have running

Before you touch a webhook, there are four automations every beehiiv operator should have live inside the platform's own builder. I see operators skip these constantly, then wonder why churn is high or why new subscribers go cold.

**1. Welcome and onboarding sequence.** The trigger is "New subscriber confirmed." Build a five-email sequence over 14 days: day one is a genuine welcome and one-click survey (use a Typeform or [Tally](/go/tally/) link), day three delivers your best-performing issue, day seven asks what problem brought them here, day ten introduces the referral program, day fourteen offers your paid tier if you have one. This sequence has more leverage than almost anything else in your stack. Don't agonize over subject lines until this is running.

**2. Re-engagement on inactive subscribers.** Trigger: subscriber has not opened any email in 60 days and carries the tag "active." Send three re-engagement emails over two weeks. No open after that? Apply a "cold" tag and suppress from main broadcasts. This protects deliverability. Beehiiv's sender reputation is shared infrastructure, and cold subscribers will tank your open rates for everyone else on your list.

**3. Referral milestone celebrations.** Beehiiv's referral program is genuinely good, and most operators completely ignore its automation hooks. Trigger separately on "Referral count reaches 1" and "Referral count reaches 5." Give a real reward at each milestone: a PDF, a bonus issue, a product discount. Not a badge. Automate the delivery so you are not doing it manually at 11pm.

**4. Paid upgrade upsell sequence.** If you have a paid tier, trigger on "Subscriber has been active for 30 days AND opened more than 8 issues." Three emails making the upgrade ask with a specific value prop. In my testing, this segment converts at 2-3x the rate of a cold list blast. The timing matters more than the copy.

## The webhook layer: connecting beehiiv to your GTM stack

This is where beehiiv automations get genuinely powerful for RevOps operators. Beehiiv fires webhooks on every major subscriber event. You catch them in n8n or Make, transform the payload, and route signals into your CRM, outbound tool, Slack, or anywhere else.

<StepRow steps={[
  {
    title: "Enable webhooks in beehiiv settings",
    body: "Go to Settings > Integrations > Webhooks. Add your n8n or Make webhook URL as the endpoint. Select the events you want: subscriber.created, subscriber.updated (for tag changes), subscription.upgraded, and referral.created are the four I always enable first. Beehiiv signs payloads with an HMAC secret, so grab that and store it as an environment variable before you save."
  },
  {
    title: "Catch and validate in n8n",
    body: "In n8n, add a Webhook trigger node set to POST. Add a Code node immediately after it that verifies the HMAC signature using the secret you stored. If validation fails, return a 401 and stop. This prevents junk or spoofed payloads from polluting your CRM. Beehiiv's webhook docs at beehiiv.com/support detail the exact signing method."
  },
  {
    title: "Route by event type",
    body: "After validation, add an IF or Switch node on the event field in the payload. subscriber.created routes to your CRM contact creation path. subscription.upgraded routes to your MRR tracking and Slack alert path. Tag changes (subscriber.updated where tags changed) route to your segmentation or outbound trigger path. Keep each branch clean and independent."
  },
  {
    title: "Enrich and sync to CRM",
    body: "For subscriber.created events where the subscriber came from a B2B acquisition channel (you can tell by UTM params in the payload), run the email through a Clay enrichment call or a direct Clearbit or Apollo lookup. Newsletter reads becoming outbound triggers is the whole game."

    **The gotcha everyone hits:** Beehiiv sends subscriber.updated for every property change, including internal beehiiv-side updates you did not trigger. If you do not filter on which fields actually changed, you will create duplicate CRM records, spam your Slack channel with noise, or fire outbound sequences at subscribers who just had their timezone updated. Always check the `changed_fields` array in the payload before acting.
  </Fragment>

    ```json
    {
      "event": "subscriber.updated",
      "data": {
        "subscriber_id": "sub_abc123",
        "email": "user@example.com",
        "changed_fields": ["tags"],
        "tags": ["high-intent", "active"],
        "utm_source": "linkedin",
        "custom_fields": {
          "company": "Acme Corp",
          "title": "VP Sales"
        }
      }
    }
    ```
    Filter on `changed_fields` including `"tags"` before routing to any outbound or CRM action. If it is not in that array, skip the branch entirely.

## Connecting the full GTM loop

The highest-value beehiiv automation pattern I have built is what I call the newsletter-to-pipeline loop. A B2B prospect subscribes via a lead magnet. Beehiiv sends the welcome sequence natively. The webhook fires subscriber.created into n8n. n8n enriches the email via [Clay](/go/clay/) (job title, company, LinkedIn URL). The enriched contact lands in HubSpot as a Marketing Qualified Lead. Then, if they click a product-interest link in any issue, the tag change fires another webhook that escalates them to a Sales Qualified Lead and notifies the AE in Slack.

This is not exotic. It takes about four hours to wire up if you have n8n running. The tricky part is the enrichment step, because you need to handle emails that do not match a company (personal Gmail, etc.) without breaking the flow. I route those to a "personal email" branch that applies a tag but skips CRM creation entirely.

If you are [building a full outbound stack](/blog/2026-09-18-how-to-build-a-300mo-outbound-stack-in-2026/) alongside your newsletter, beehiiv engagement signals are your best intent data. Full stop. People who read your content before buying are warmer than anyone you scrape from Apollo. The [HubSpot workflows vs. Kit comparison](/blog/2026-09-18-hubspot-workflows-vs-kit-vs-make-best-alternative-2026/) post covers the CRM-side logic if you want to go deeper on the receiving end of these flows.

For the outbound sequencing step, [n8n](/tools/n8n/) orchestrating into Smartlead or Instantly handles the actual send. Keep the sequence tool separate from the newsletter tool. They serve different deliverability pools, and you do not want newsletter unsubscribes tanking your cold outbound reputation.

<MyTake>
Most beehiiv operators treat their newsletter as a content channel and their outbound as a separate motion. That split is the mistake. Every beehiiv engagement event is a declared-intent signal that your outbound team is paying Clay and Apollo to approximate, badly. Wire the webhook layer first, then argue about which sequences to run.
</MyTake>

## Build the webhook layer before you optimize the sequences

The native beehiiv automation builder is solid for what it does. Welcome sequences, re-engagement, referral rewards, paid upsells: all of these should be live before you touch anything else. But the real value for RevOps is the webhook layer. Once subscriber events are flowing into n8n or [Make](/tools/make/), you have a real-time intent stream that most outbound teams spend thousands per month trying to recreate with third-party intent data. [Beehiiv](/tools/beehiiv/) gives it to you as a side effect of running a newsletter. Build the plumbing first, then optimize the sequences.
