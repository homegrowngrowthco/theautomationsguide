## What You Are Actually Wiring Together

Lemlist sits at the execution layer of an outbound stack. It sends the emails, tracks the opens and clicks, handles the personalization variables, and surfaces the replies. Every integration it needs is either feeding it leads (enrichment tools, CRMs, data providers) or consuming its output (CRM activity logging, Slack alerts, pipeline triggers, reporting).

Before you wire anything, map which direction the data flows. The usual pattern: a data source (Apollo, Clay, a CRM list) exports leads into Lemlist. Lemlist runs the sequence. On a reply or a click trigger, Lemlist pushes an event back to your CRM or a Slack channel, and a rep takes over. Most teams get the "in" side right and completely neglect the "out" side.

## The Native CRM Integrations

These are the integrations I'd set up before anything else. Supported, documented, and no middleware required.

**HubSpot** is Lemlist's tightest CRM connection. The native integration supports two-way contact sync, campaign enrollment from HubSpot workflows, and activity logging (emails sent, opens, clicks, replies) back to the HubSpot contact timeline. You can trigger a Lemlist campaign from a HubSpot workflow enrollment, which means lifecycle stage changes can kick off outbound sequences without a human clicking anything. For RevOps teams already invested in [HubSpot](/tools/hubspot/), this is the connection that makes Lemlist feel native rather than bolted on. Per the [Lemlist HubSpot integration docs](https://help.lemlist.com/en/articles/6385726-hubspot-integration), you authenticate via OAuth and map contact properties on first setup.

**Salesforce** covers enterprise accounts. Lemlist's Salesforce connector syncs leads and contacts, logs campaign activity to the activity timeline, and supports field mapping for custom objects. The typical failure here is field mapping drift: Salesforce admins change custom fields and the Lemlist sync silently drops data. The first thing I'd check after any Salesforce schema change is whether the field mappings in Lemlist still resolve correctly. Don't wait for a rep to notice empty fields in their activity log.

**Pipedrive** is the lightest of the three. [Pipedrive](/tools/pipedrive/) native sync covers contact import and basic activity logging. If your team needs more sophisticated two-way sync (deal stage updates triggering Lemlist campaigns, for example), you will need an n8n or Zapier layer on top of the native connector. The native Pipedrive integration alone won't get you there.

## The Clay-to-Lemlist Pipeline

I'd prioritize this one if you are doing any ICP-targeted outbound at any real volume. [Clay](/tools/clay/) handles waterfall enrichment, pulling from Clearbit, Apollo, LinkedIn, and a dozen other sources, and personalizes rows at scale using AI columns. Lemlist is where those enriched, personalized rows become sequences.

The handoff works two ways:

1. **CSV export from Clay to Lemlist**: Clay exports a CSV with all your enrichment columns. Lemlist imports it directly and maps columns to campaign variables. Simple, no middleware needed, but manual per batch.

2. **Clay HTTP action to Lemlist API**: Clay's HTTP request action can POST directly to the Lemlist API endpoint to add a lead to a campaign in real time. This makes the workflow fully automated: a new row passes through Clay's enrichment waterfall, gets personalized, passes an ICP filter, and lands in Lemlist without a human touching it.

## The Integrations Most Teams Skip

The enrichment and CRM connections get all the attention. The high-value gaps are almost always on the output side. This is where I'd focus if you're inheriting a Lemlist setup that's already been running for a while.

**Unsubscribe sync**: When a lead unsubscribes in Lemlist, that status needs to propagate back to your CRM and to any other sequencing tools immediately. Teams that skip this re-enroll unsubscribers inside HubSpot workflows because HubSpot doesn't know Lemlist flagged them. The fix is a Lemlist "unsubscribe" webhook that writes a suppression property to the HubSpot contact record. Takes an hour to build. Saves weeks of list cleanup.

**Reply routing by sentiment**: Lemlist's native reply detection classifies replies as positive, negative, and neutral (per the Lemlist AI features documentation). You can route those signals differently: positive replies create a deal in your CRM, negative replies suppress the contact from future campaigns, neutral replies go to a human review queue. Most teams treat all replies identically, which means reps wade through out-of-office responses and miss the actual interested ones.

**Lead verification before import**: Running cold lists into Lemlist without bounce verification is the fastest way to damage your sending domain. Tools like [Bouncer](/tools/bouncer/) or [ZeroBounce](/tools/zerobounce/) should sit in the pipeline between Clay (or any list source) and Lemlist import. The [Lemlist sending best practices doc](https://help.lemlist.com/en/articles/5440806-email-deliverability-best-practices) recommends keeping bounce rate under 3%. Skipping verification makes that target nearly impossible on cold lists.

**Apollo as a lead source**: If your team uses [Apollo](/tools/apollo/) for prospecting, Apollo has a native Lemlist push in its sequence settings. You can push contacts from an Apollo search directly into a Lemlist campaign, cutting out the CSV export step entirely. Most Apollo users don't know this exists.

<MyTake>
The entire integrations conversation around Lemlist fixates on the "in" direction, how leads get into campaigns, and almost completely ignores what happens after a reply lands. That's backwards. A warm reply sitting in Lemlist unrouted for 18 hours while a rep manually checks the inbox is a more expensive problem than imperfect personalization. Wire your reply routing before you optimize your Clay waterfall.
</MyTake>

## The Integration Stack That Actually Ships Revenue

Lemlist's integration surface is broad enough to cover almost any outbound stack. The native CRM connectors handle the foundational sync. Clay covers the enrichment and personalization pipeline. n8n handles event routing at scale without ballooning your Zapier bill. The gaps most teams leave open are on the reply and suppression side, and those gaps are where deals slip and sender reputation erodes.

My recommendation: wire the HubSpot (or Salesforce) native connector first, add a Clay-to-Lemlist API push for your enrichment layer, and register n8n webhooks for reply and unsubscribe events before you run your first campaign at real volume. The setup takes a day. The alternative is cleaning up data hygiene debt for months.
