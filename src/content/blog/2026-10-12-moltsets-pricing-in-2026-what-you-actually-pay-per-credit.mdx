## What each tier actually includes

MoltSets uses a unified credit model where one credit equals one contact reveal, regardless of whether that surfaces an email, a phone number, or both. That sounds clean until you realize mobile direct-dial reveals cost two credits in some configurations. Here is my read on what the tiers give you in practice, not just the label on the pricing page.

The Pro tier is where the math starts to favor MoltSets over its cheapest competitors. At roughly 2,100 credits per month, a three-person SDR team pulling 700 contacts each per month lives comfortably inside the plan without overages. The Starter tier, by contrast, runs out of headroom fast for any team doing more than 300 to 400 touches a month. I'd argue Starter exists mainly as a trial mechanism for solo founders, not a real operational plan for anyone running sequences at scale.

## The true cost: real math for a 5-rep SDR team

Let's run actual numbers. Five SDRs each building a 500-contact sequence per month. That is 2,500 contact reveals required. You also want mobile dials on roughly 40% of those contacts, so call it 1,000 mobile reveals at two credits each, totalling 3,000 effective credits needed per month.

On Starter (330 credits per month): you need 2,670 overage credits at $0.15 each. That is $400.50 in overages on top of your $49 base. **Total: $449.50/month.**

On Pro (2,100 credits per month): you need 900 overage credits at the overage rate. That is $135 in overages on top of $149. **Total: $284/month.**

On Scale with a custom 3,000-credit block (estimated at $0.06 to $0.08 per credit on volume): roughly **$180 to $240/month all-in.**

The lesson here applies to almost every credit-based data tool: the entry tier is a trap for anyone beyond a solo prospector. More than two active SDRs almost always means Pro or a custom Scale package is cheaper once you factor in overages. Teams that skip this math usually discover the problem on their third monthly bill.

Annual billing drops each tier by roughly 20%, which matters more on Pro and Scale than on Starter. A five-rep team on annual Pro saves approximately $358 per year before overages, which covers a couple of months of extra credits.

## How MoltSets compares to Apollo and ZoomInfo on price per contact

[Apollo's](/go/apollo/) free tier includes 10,000 export credits per month, which makes it look like an obvious choice on cost alone. But [Apollo](/tools/apollo/) at volume comes with data quality caveats that [G2 reviewers flag consistently](https://www.g2.com/categories/b2b-data), particularly on mobile numbers outside North America. MoltSets positions itself on freshness and direct-dial accuracy. That distinction matters more than raw credit count if your sequences are hitting phones, not just inboxes. Don't pick Apollo because it's cheaper per credit if your team is running phone-heavy sequences into EMEA or APAC markets.

[ZoomInfo](/tools/zoominfo/) is a different category of spend entirely. Per [G2's B2B data category](https://www.g2.com/categories/b2b-data), most ZoomInfo contracts for teams under 20 reps still come in above $15,000 annually. That is $1,250 per month before you touch seat counts or intent add-ons. If your team is under 10 reps and your ICP is mid-market rather than enterprise, ZoomInfo's price-to-value ratio rarely pencils out. MoltSets at Pro or Scale is a realistic alternative for that cohort.

For teams building enrichment into their full outbound stack, the breakdown of [Lusha competitors that stop credit drain](/blog/2026-10-08-lusha-competitors-that-stop-credit-drain-dead/) and the [best cold email stack under $300 in 2026](/blog/2026-10-09-best-cold-email-stack-under-300-in-2026/) both put data-layer costs in the context of full-stack spending. Worth reading alongside this one.

One thing to understand clearly: MoltSets credits are consumed at reveal, not at delivery. Pull a contact's email, it hard-bounces in [Smartlead](/go/smartlead/) or [Instantly](/go/instantly/) downstream, and you have already spent the credit. A verification layer before enrichment, specifically [Bouncer](/go/bouncer/) or [ZeroBounce](/go/zerobounce/), is not optional at volume. Per [Smartlead's verification docs](https://help.smartlead.ai/), running emails through a verification waterfall before loading sequences significantly reduces hard bounce rates and protects sender reputation. Budget for it from day one.

<MyTake>
The credit-at-reveal model is a pricing mechanism that transfers data risk entirely onto the buyer while the vendor collects regardless of outcome. ## What MoltSets pricing is fair on, and where it stings

The Pro tier pricing is fair. For a small SDR team that needs mobile dials and CRM sync without a five-figure data contract, $149/month is a reasonable price for reasonably fresh B2B contact data. The annual discount is competitive and pushes effective monthly cost below most per-seat data tools in the same tier.

Where it stings: overage credits billed at the Starter rate ($0.15/credit) regardless of your plan is a punishing clause. Blow past your Pro allocation by 500 credits in a big campaign month and that is an extra $75. Avoidable with better credit pacing or a custom credit block negotiated at Scale pricing. The first thing I'd check before signing any MoltSets contract is whether the sales team will fix the overage rate at Pro or Scale rates rather than Starter rates. Per the [vendor's pricing page](https://moltsets.com/pricing), this is negotiable on annual plans. Get it in writing.
