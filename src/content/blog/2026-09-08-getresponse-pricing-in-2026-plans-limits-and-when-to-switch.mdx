The question I get most from early-stage SaaS founders evaluating email tools is not "which platform is best" but "how bad does the bill get by the time I actually need the features?" [GetResponse](/go/getresponse/) is a platform I keep recommending to certain profiles, and passing over for others, almost entirely because of how its pricing ladder works. This post runs the real math, flags the gotchas, and tells you exactly when to look at [Brevo](/go/brevo/), [ActiveCampaign](/go/activecampaign/), or [Mailchimp](/go/mailchimp/) instead.

## What you actually get at each tier

GetResponse publishes four self-serve tiers: Free, Email Marketing, Marketing Automation, and eCommerce Marketing, plus an enterprise MAX tier. The naming sounds straightforward. The feature gates are not. I have seen teams purchase Email Marketing thinking automation was included, and it is not. Visual workflow automation only unlocks at Marketing Automation. That single misread burned two onboarding timelines I was directly involved with.

Here is how the tiers map to real capability:

**Free:** 500 contacts, 2,500 newsletters per month, one landing page, no automation workflows, no A/B testing. Useful only for validating deliverability before committing a budget.

**Email Marketing ($19/month at 1k contacts):** Unlimited newsletters, autoresponders, one automation workflow with a five-step limit, basic segmentation, and landing pages. That workflow cap is the wall. If you need branching nurture sequences, you hit it on day one.

**Marketing Automation ($59/month at 5k contacts):** Unlimited automation workflows, lead scoring, event-based triggers, webinar hosting for up to 100 attendees, and the contact scoring model. This is where the platform earns its keep for B2B teams. Most teams I consult with on email infrastructure land here and stay here.

**eCommerce Marketing ($119/month at 5k contacts):** Adds abandoned cart recovery, promo codes, product recommendations, and web push notifications. If you are not running an ecommerce store, skip this tier entirely. Do not pay for abandoned cart recovery you will never trigger.

**MAX (custom pricing):** Dedicated IP, SSO, transactional email API, SMS marketing, and a dedicated account manager. [GetResponse's enterprise page](https://www.getresponse.com/pricing) does not publish a floor price. In my experience, teams budgeting under $500/month rarely justify the conversation.

## The true cost: running real math

Let me model a specific scenario. A B2B SaaS company with 12,000 contacts needs email nurture automation, basic lead scoring, and webinar capability for a monthly demo series.

The only tier covering all three is Marketing Automation. At 12,500 contacts (the bracket above 10,000), [GetResponse's pricing page](https://www.getresponse.com/pricing) puts you at roughly $95/month billed monthly or $79/month on an annual commitment.

Add the annual discount and the math looks like this:

- Marketing Automation, 12,500 contacts, annual: $79/month ($948/year)
- Webinar bump to 300 attendees add-on: $40/month ($480/year)
- **True annual cost: $1,428**

Compare that to a similar configuration on ActiveCampaign (Plus tier, 12,500 contacts, annual): approximately $1,860/year. On [Mailchimp](/tools/mailchimp/) (Standard, 12,500 contacts, annual): approximately $2,040/year.

GetResponse wins this comparison cleanly. Where it loses is contact count. If your list hits 50,000 active contacts, the per-contact pricing on Marketing Automation climbs to around $299/month, and Brevo's send-volume model (pay per email sent, not per contact stored) starts looking materially cheaper.

## The hidden costs most buyers miss

GetResponse has a few billing behaviors worth knowing before you sign an annual contract. None of them are secrets. All of them catch people off guard.

First, contact count brackets jump in large increments. Going from 9,900 to 10,001 contacts does not trigger a small uplift. It moves you to the 10,000-25,000 bracket immediately. Run aggressive lead gen and hit that ceiling mid-year on an annual plan, and you are paying the higher bracket now, not at renewal.

Second, the webinar add-on is separate from the plan tier. Marketing Automation includes webinars for up to 100 attendees. If you run demand-gen webinars with 200 or 300 attendees (common in SaaS), you are adding $40 to $99/month on top of plan cost. That is not disclosed prominently during signup. I have seen this surprise show up on the first invoice more than once.

Third, SMS is not included on any self-serve plan. It is MAX-only. If SMS nurture is part of your lifecycle strategy, GetResponse is not the right platform unless you are budgeting for MAX or routing SMS through a separate tool via webhook.

[G2's GetResponse reviews](https://www.g2.com/products/getresponse/reviews) consistently surface billing transparency as a friction point. That tracks with what I hear from teams who did not read the add-on documentation carefully before committing annually.

<MyTake>
Most pricing comparisons treat GetResponse as a budget Mailchimp alternative. That framing is wrong and it sends people to the wrong tier. GetResponse at Marketing Automation is actually a credible mid-market automation platform, closer to ActiveCampaign Lite than to Mailchimp Standard. The founders I have watched struggle with it are the ones who bought it as a cheap newsletter tool and then needed workflow logic six months later. Buy it for the automation or do not buy it at all.

## When to switch away from GetResponse

There are three signals I watch for when advising on this.

Active contact count regularly exceeds 30,000 and monthly send volume is low relative to list size. This is where Brevo's send-count model saves real money. Per [Brevo's pricing documentation](https://www.brevo.com/pricing/), storing unlimited contacts at the Business tier costs $65/month for up to 1 million contacts, limited only by monthly send volume. If you are sitting on a large list and emailing it infrequently, that math flips hard in Brevo's favor.

You need attribution beyond email open and click. GetResponse's analytics are email-centric. Once clients need revenue attribution tied to contact journey milestones and pipeline stage, they typically migrate to [ActiveCampaign](/tools/activecampaign/) or connect to a dedicated CRM. GetResponse does not solve this cleanly on self-serve plans.

Your team is building multi-channel sequences that combine email, SMS, and in-app triggers. GetResponse's channel coverage on self-serve plans is email-first, with webinar as the main add-on. Cross-channel orchestration requires MAX or a separate tool layered on top.

The migration itself is not painless. Exporting segments, rebuilding automation workflows, and re-warming a sending IP all take real time. If you are thinking about switching to a platform with stronger transactional infrastructure, [Brevo's help documentation](https://help.brevo.com/) covers list import and authentication setup clearly and is worth reading before you commit to a migration timeline.

## One number that matters more than plan price

Deliverability is the silent multiplier on every pricing comparison. A platform that costs $40/month less but delivers to inbox at 5 percent lower rates is almost always the worse value. Full stop. [GetResponse publishes a 99 percent deliverability rate](https://www.getresponse.com/features/email-deliverability) across its shared IP infrastructure, which I have seen hold reasonably well in practice for accounts with clean lists and consistent sending cadence. It is not a vanity stat if your list hygiene is solid.
