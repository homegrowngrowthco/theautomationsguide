## What Avoma's tiers actually include

Avoma publishes four paid tiers plus a free plan. The free plan gives you 300 transcription minutes per month and no integrations. That is roughly five to six hour-long calls, which covers light use for a founder but nothing at the rep level.

The Starter plan at $19 per seat per month adds unlimited recording and transcription, speaker identification, and basic meeting summaries. No CRM sync. No AI coaching. No deal-level insights. For most sales teams, this plan is a trial tier dressed up as a real option, and I'd skip it entirely unless you are running a proof-of-concept before committing to a higher tier.

The Plus plan at $59 per seat is where things become useful: HubSpot and Salesforce integration, CRM field auto-population from call data, and conversation intelligence basics. The limitation worth flagging is that custom field mapping and bidirectional sync are still restricted at this tier.

The Business plan at $79 per seat is the realistic target for a revenue team that wants Avoma to actually affect pipeline. AI coaching scorecards, deal intelligence dashboards, pipeline review features, and full CRM sync with custom field support all live here. Per [Avoma's pricing page](https://www.avoma.com/pricing), Enterprise pricing is custom and targets teams needing SSO, advanced permissions, and dedicated support.

## The true cost for a real team

Let me run the arithmetic for a 10-rep sales team on annual billing, which is how most teams actually buy.

**Avoma Starter (10 seats):** 10 x $19 x 12 = $2,280 per year. You get recordings and transcripts. No CRM sync.

**Avoma Plus (10 seats):** 10 x $59 x 12 = $7,080 per year. You get CRM integration but limited coaching.

**Avoma Business (10 seats):** 10 x $79 x 12 = $9,480 per year. Full feature set.

My read is that Plus is where most teams think they can land, then discover they need Business-level CRM mapping six weeks in. That mid-tier bait-and-switch is a common failure pattern with per-seat tools in this category. Budget for Business upfront or do not budget for Avoma at all.

Now compare the same 10-rep team on [Laxis](/go/laxis/). The Premium plan at $16.99 per seat covers transcription, summaries, and basic CRM sync. At 10 seats annual: 10 x $16.99 x 12 = $2,039 per year. The Business plan at around $39 per seat (per [Laxis's published pricing](https://www.laxis.com/pricing)) adds team analytics and advanced CRM: 10 x $39 x 12 = $4,680 per year.

For a team that does not need AI coaching scorecards or pipeline deal intelligence, Laxis Business at $4,680 delivers comparable CRM sync to Avoma Business at $9,480. That $4,800 annual delta is real budget you could put toward headcount or enablement content instead.

[Fathom](/go/fathom/) occupies a different position entirely. Its free plan is genuinely unlimited for individual recording and summaries, the best free tier in this category by a wide margin. The [Fathom pricing page](https://fathom.video/pricing) shows the Team plan at around $19 per seat per month, with Salesforce and HubSpot sync available on the Premium tier. For teams that primarily need transcription plus lightweight CRM logging, Fathom competes directly with Avoma Plus at a fraction of the Business cost.

## The add-ons and limits worth watching

A few things the top-level pricing does not surface prominently.

**Storage limits.** Avoma's free and Starter plans cap recording storage. Business and Enterprise offer extended storage, but confirm the specifics for your call volume before signing.

**Seats vs. viewers.** Avoma charges per active seat. If your RevOps or enablement function wants to view call libraries and dashboards without joining calls, check whether viewer-only seats are available or if they consume full seat licenses at $79 each. This one sneaks up on teams at renewal.

**Annual vs. monthly billing.** Monthly billing on Avoma carries a price premium. If you are committing to the platform, annual billing is the realistic comparison point. All figures in this post use annual pricing.

**Integrations beyond CRM.** Avoma connects to [Salesforce](/go/salesforce/), [HubSpot](/go/hubspot/), Zoom, Google Meet, and Teams. Zapier-based custom integrations require the Business tier or above. If your stack relies on middleware routing call data into other tools, that is a Business-tier requirement worth factoring into the decision before you sign.

## What Avoma is actually worth buying

The specific scenario where Avoma Business justifies $79 per seat is a team running a structured sales methodology (MEDDIC, SPICED, Challenger) where the AI scorecard grades calls against that methodology in real time and surfaces gaps for manager review. That replaces meaningful manual coaching work. For a 10-rep team where the sales manager is reviewing calls and scoring them weekly, $9,480 per year for Avoma Business is cheap relative to the manager hours it absorbs.

If you are not running a structured methodology and you just want calls recorded, transcribed, and logged to CRM, Laxis or [Fathom](/tools/fathom/) at roughly half the price delivers that outcome. The pattern I'd flag is teams buying Avoma Business because the demo impressed them, then using 20% of the feature set and resenting the per-seat cost at renewal. Methodology adoption has to precede the tool purchase, not follow it. Per [G2's meeting assistant category reviews](https://www.g2.com/categories/meeting-assistant), this is a recurring complaint across the coaching-tier products, not just Avoma.
