## What Warmly's tiers actually include

Warmly structures pricing around three variables: revealed visitor volume, seat count, and automation feature access. The free plan is a proof-of-concept tier. Startup is where real RevOps workflows begin. Business is where you get AI routing, multi-channel orchestration, and the deeper CRM field mapping that makes the tool worth its cost for a live outbound motion.

The free plan caps at 500 visitors and gives you Slack notifications plus limited CRM integration. It's enough to confirm that Warmly is picking up signal on your site. It won't power a consistent pipeline motion. Startup bumps the limit significantly and adds direct HubSpot and Salesforce sync, intent scoring, and the ability to route accounts to specific reps by territory or account owner logic.

Business is where pricing goes opaque, as it typically does with tools in this category. Per the [published pricing page](https://warmly.ai/pricing) (checked November 2026), Business requires a sales conversation and is scoped by estimated monthly unique visitors plus seat count. My read is that teams under ten reps with sub-50,000 monthly site visits should exhaust the Startup plan first before engaging on a Business quote. Don't let a vendor AE talk you into Business tier before you've proven the Startup signal converts to pipeline.

## The true cost: running the math for a real team

Put some concrete numbers on this. Say you're a 5-rep SaaS team on the Startup plan at $700/month. That plan officially seats 3 users, so you need to add 2 more. Per the Warmly pricing page, additional seats beyond the included count run roughly $50-$75 per seat per month at the Startup tier.

The arithmetic: $700 (base) plus 2 seats at $60 (midpoint estimate) lands at $820/month on annual billing, or approximately $9,840/year. If your site spikes in a launch month and you push 14,000 identified visitors instead of 10,000, you're looking at overage charges on the 4,000 excess, typically billed at around $20-$30 per additional thousand. That's an $80-$120 overage on a heavy month. Manageable, but worth factoring into the annual budget if you run regular campaigns.

At that spend level, the tool needs to close pipeline you can actually attribute. I'd argue the minimum reasonable benchmark for Warmly justifying Startup-tier spend is 3-5 influenced opportunities per month in a sales cycle where average contract value is at least $15,000. Below that ACV, the per-opportunity cost of identified intent starts looking expensive relative to outbound alternatives like [Apollo](/go/apollo/) or [Clay](/go/clay/), which surface similar account signals at lower unit costs (see the [Instantly vs Clay vs Apollo lead gen ROI comparison](/blog/2026-11-08-instantly-vs-clay-vs-apollo-lead-gen-roi-compared/) for the full breakdown on that tradeoff).

For Business tier, vendors in this category consistently price at $2,000-$5,000+/month for teams with meaningful volume. Without a quote in hand, I'd budget $3,000/month as a planning assumption if your site sees 50,000+ monthly B2B visitors and you have a full SDR team needing automated routing.

## How Warmly compares to RB2B and Factors.ai on value per dollar

The honest framing here is that these three tools are not fully substitutable. They share the visitor identification category but diverge sharply on what you do with the signal afterward.

[RB2B](/tools/rb2b/) leads on individual-level LinkedIn identification, pushing a named person (not just an account) to Slack when they visit your site. Its pricing is simpler: a flat monthly fee around $119/month at the base tier as of late 2026, per the [RB2B pricing page](https://www.rb2b.com/pricing), covering a set number of identified individuals. If your use case is "I want to know which specific person from Acme Corp visited our pricing page and reach out to them on LinkedIn within the hour," RB2B delivers that at a lower price than Warmly's Startup plan. What it does not do is route that person into a CRM workflow, trigger a sequence in [Smartlead](/go/smartlead/), or score account-level intent over time.

[Factors.ai](/tools/factors-ai/) occupies the analytical middle ground. It layers G2 intent data, LinkedIn ad attribution, and account-level scoring on top of the visitor reveal layer, as detailed on the [Factors.ai pricing overview](https://www.factors.ai/pricing). Where Warmly optimizes for real-time sales rep action, Factors leans toward marketing and demand gen teams who want to understand which channels are driving high-intent accounts. Pricing is similarly volume-based and in the same rough band as Warmly's Startup tier, but the tool requires more configuration to operationalize for SDR workflows. I'd argue most SDR teams will find Factors harder to action without a marketing ops person owning the setup.

My read: if your motion is SDR-led and you want identified accounts flowing into existing sequences in Smartlead or [Lemlist](/go/lemlist/) with minimal middleware, Warmly is the right anchor. If you want person-level LinkedIn reach-out automation, RB2B is cheaper and faster to set up. If you're a marketing-led team trying to close the attribution loop on paid spend and G2 category intent, Factors.ai makes more sense. The workflow post we published covers exactly how to pipe Warmly's webhook output into an [n8n](/go/n8n/) or [Make](/go/make/) automation for sequence enrollment, which is where the Startup tier earns its keep.

## The bottom line on Warmly's 2026 pricing

Warmly's pricing structure rewards teams that move fast on identified intent. The free tier de-risks the signal validation step. The Startup plan is the right bet for most lean outbound teams, provided you're pairing it with a CRM and sequence tool so the identified accounts actually convert to pipeline. Business tier is a real conversation to have once your site volume and rep count make manual triage the bottleneck. Go in with a benchmark ACV and volume number so you can pressure-test the ROI before signing.
