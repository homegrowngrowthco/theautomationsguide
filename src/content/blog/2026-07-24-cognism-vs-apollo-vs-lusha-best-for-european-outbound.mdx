## What actually separates these three for European teams

The US analyst view of this space focuses almost entirely on database size and CRM integrations. For European outbound, neither of those is the deciding factor. What matters: verified mobile numbers (because cold email open rates in Europe are suppressed by stricter spam filtering), GDPR-defensible data sourcing, and European-specific TAM coverage in your actual verticals.

[Cognism](/go/cognism/) built its entire product narrative around this axis. Its Diamond Data tier puts a human agent on the phone to verify a mobile number before surfacing it. That sounds slow, and it is. Match rates are lower than Apollo's because of it. But when a number rings and connects, the conversion math changes entirely. For DACH markets specifically, where cold email conversion is notoriously low, this matters more than anywhere else. I have watched teams switch from Apollo to [Cognism](/tools/cognism/) for German outbound and cut their calls-per-meeting number almost in half.

[Apollo](/go/apollo/) is the dominant global player for a reason: it has the richest intent data layer, the best native sequencing builder, and pricing that lets early-stage teams get in without a procurement conversation. But its European mobile data is crowdsourced and community-verified. Accuracy degrades significantly outside the UK and major Western European hubs. I have pulled Apollo lists for Scandinavian prospects and seen mobile bounce rates above 60%. That is not a rounding error.

[Lusha](/go/lusha/) carved its identity as the LinkedIn enrichment tool of choice before Clay made waterfall enrichment mainstream (see our breakdown of [waterfall enrichment tools](/blog/2026-07-16-bettercontact-vs-fullenrich-vs-leadmagic-waterfall-enrichmen/) for context on how that changes the calculus). Its European database is smaller than both competitors and its GDPR compliance documentation, while present, is thinner than Cognism's. Lusha is useful in the right context. It is not a primary source.

## How to choose

This decision hinges on two variables: how much of your TAM is in Europe specifically (not just English-speaking global), and whether cold calling is part of your outbound motion at all.

If your SDRs are running a pure email sequence into European contacts, [Apollo](/tools/apollo/) is a defensible choice at its price point, especially if you pair it with a dedicated email verification step through Bouncer or ZeroBounce before any send. You do not need to pay Cognism's premium for email-only sequences. Full stop. But if you are running a blended motion where an SDR sends an email and then calls within 48 hours, and your targets are in Germany or the Netherlands, Cognism's Diamond Data is not optional. The difference in connect rate between a verified mobile and an unverified one in those markets is the difference between a functioning outbound program and a volume game you cannot win.

The GDPR angle deserves a direct statement too. All three providers process data under legitimate interest as the legal basis per [GDPR Article 6(1)(f)](https://gdpr-info.eu/art-6-gdpr/). The difference is that Cognism publishes its legitimate interest assessments publicly and maintains a dedicated compliance team. Apollo and [Lusha](/tools/lusha/) treat this more as a checkbox. Your legal team will notice. In regulated verticals like fintech, healthcare, or legal services, it becomes a procurement blocker faster than most sales leaders expect.

For teams enriching LinkedIn data as part of a Clay-based workflow, the calculus shifts again. Neither Cognism nor Apollo is the right primary enrichment layer inside Clay. For that motion, check our guide on [LinkedIn enrichment tools](/blog/2026-07-22-leadmagic-vs-surfe-vs-findymail-best-linkedin-enrichment-too/).

<MyTake>
Most European sales leaders I talk to think buying Cognism solves their GDPR problem. It does not. Cognism gives you cleaner data sourced under a defensible framework, but your company's legitimate interest assessment, suppression list management, and opt-out handling are still entirely your responsibility. Buy Cognism for the mobile data quality. Then build your own compliance process around it, regardless of which tool you pick. The tool is not the compliance program.

## The split stack is the right answer for most scaling teams

The framing of this comparison as a straight head-to-head assumes you pick one provider and live with its tradeoffs. That is rarely how the best-run teams operate. The teams I see running the most efficient European outbound use Cognism as their European contact source (particularly for mobile numbers), Apollo as the sequencing layer and the source for US and UK email-first campaigns, and a [Clay](/tools/clay/) enrichment workflow to hydrate both into a clean CRM record before anything touches Salesforce or HubSpot. That is not over-engineering. It is using each tool for what it is actually good at. We rebuilt exactly this stack last quarter for a 40-person B2B SaaS team selling into DACH and Benelux, and their SDR connect rate on cold calls went from 4% to just over 9% inside 60 days.
