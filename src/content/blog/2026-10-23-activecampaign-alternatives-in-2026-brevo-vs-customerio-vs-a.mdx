## How each platform actually works

The framing that matters for RevOps operators is not "which has more features" but "which model fits how your team actually triggers and sends." ActiveCampaign is a contact-centric platform. You build automations around contact properties, tags, and list membership. That model works well for nurture sequences, lead scoring, and anything that touches a Deals pipeline. The tradeoff is that the data model is relatively flat compared to a product-led stack.

[Customer.io](/tools/customerio/) is event-centric. You instrument your product or data layer to emit events (user signed up, trial expired, feature used), and Customer.io reacts to those events to branch and sequence. That's genuinely powerful for SaaS onboarding and lifecycle work. But it means your RevOps operator either needs to write API calls or work closely with an engineer. Teams that treat it like a drag-and-drop newsletter tool will be disappointed. That's not a knock on Customer.io; it's just the wrong tool for that job.

[Brevo](/tools/brevo/) sits closest to the traditional ESP model, but has grown meaningfully since rebranding from Sendinblue. Its automation builder handles multi-step sequences, SMS, and transactional email from the same interface. The send-volume pricing model is legitimately friendlier for teams with large cold lists or infrequent batch sends. If you've been reading about [how Brevo stacks up against Constant Contact and GetResponse](/blog/2026-10-18-constant-contact-vs-getresponse-vs-brevo-best-for-b2b-2026/) for B2B, Brevo wins that three-way comparison on automation flexibility at the price point. I'd argue it's not even close.

## How to choose

The first thing I'd check is whether you actually need the Deals CRM. If you're syncing contacts into ActiveCampaign from HubSpot or Salesforce anyway, you're paying for a CRM you aren't using. In that case, the switch to Brevo is almost always justified on cost alone. You won't lose meaningful automation capability for standard B2B nurture flows, either.

If your sequences are driven by product events, user behavior, or data warehouse signals rather than contact properties, Customer.io is the more durable architecture. It handles branching logic on event payloads in ways that would require hacky workarounds in ActiveCampaign or Brevo. The catch is the $100/month minimum entry point and the expectation that someone technical owns the integration. For teams already using [n8n](/tools/n8n/) or Zapier to pipe event data around, that overhead is manageable. For a lean two-person marketing team, it can be a real barrier.

My read is that the majority of B2B SaaS teams under 50 employees have no legitimate reason to stay on ActiveCampaign past their next renewal if they aren't using the Deals CRM. Brevo covers the use case at a fraction of the cost. According to [Brevo's published pricing](https://www.brevo.com/pricing/), 100,000 emails per month runs around $65, versus $93/month just to hold 10,000 contacts in [ActiveCampaign](https://www.activecampaign.com/pricing). For most teams, that gap widens every quarter.

## The verdict

If your primary complaint about ActiveCampaign is the bill, switch to Brevo. The automation parity for most B2B nurture use cases is real, the migration is well-documented, and the send-volume pricing model stays cheaper as your list grows. If your complaint is that ActiveCampaign cannot handle behavioral event triggers at the depth your product-led sequences require, Customer.io is the right move. Go in knowing it requires technical setup and a $100/month floor. And if neither complaint applies, there is a reasonable argument that ActiveCampaign's all-in-one positioning still earns its price, particularly if you are not ready to pay for a separate CRM.
