## What each tool is actually doing

These three tools get lumped together, but they solve different parts of the same problem. LeadMagic is primarily a LinkedIn profile enrichment API and Chrome extension that extracts emails, mobile numbers, and company data from LinkedIn profiles at scale. Surfe is a CRM connector first and enrichment tool second. It lives as a Chrome extension that writes LinkedIn contact data directly into HubSpot, Salesforce, or Pipedrive without manual entry. Findymail is a purpose-built email finder and verifier that happens to have strong LinkedIn coverage.

The fourth tool worth naming here is [Prospeo](/go/prospeo/), which takes a domain-first approach. You feed it a company domain and it returns the employee list with emails. Less powerful for individual LinkedIn profile enrichment, but useful when you're building accounts-first rather than personas-first. I've seen it work well paired with [Clay](/tools/clay/) for account-based plays.

If you've been following the waterfall enrichment conversation, I covered how LeadMagic fits into a multi-vendor stack in the [FullEnrich vs BetterContact vs Surfe waterfall comparison](/blog/2026-07-13-fullenrich-vs-bettercontact-vs-surfe-waterfall-enrichment-co/). Short version: in a waterfall, LeadMagic works well as a second or third pass after a primary enrichment source fails to return a match.

## How to choose

The decision comes down to one question: where does your prospecting workflow start?

If your SDRs are browsing LinkedIn and Sales Navigator and you need data going into a CRM, Surfe is the obvious choice and the per-seat pricing reflects a rep-facing product. If you're running bulk enrichment from exported lists or feeding a Clay table, [LeadMagic](/tools/leadmagic/) is stronger because the API and CSV workflow are built for volume. If deliverability is your primary concern and you want the cleanest possible email list at the lowest per-credit cost, Findymail wins on unit economics. Full stop.

Where I see teams go wrong is buying Surfe for bulk enrichment or buying LeadMagic expecting native CRM sync. These tools do not swap cleanly. Surfe's bulk limitations are structural, not a missing feature on the roadmap. LeadMagic is not a CRM connector and it is not trying to be. Teams that run both (Surfe for rep-led prospecting and LeadMagic for programmatic enrichment via [n8n](/tools/n8n/) or Make) get the most coverage. For a sequencer like [Smartlead](/tools/smartlead/) or [Instantly](/tools/instantly/), you want LeadMagic or Findymail upstream. Surfe does not belong in that pipeline.

<MyTake>
Most teams buy Surfe when they actually need LeadMagic. Surfe has better marketing and a smoother onboarding flow, and the Chrome extension demo is genuinely impressive. But if you're enriching more than 500 contacts a week, you will hit Surfe's friction ceiling fast. ## The call

LinkedIn enrichment is not a single-tool problem for serious outbound teams. The clients I work with at Homegrown Growth Co. who run the cleanest outbound motions almost always have LeadMagic or Findymail feeding a sequencer, with [Surfe](/tools/surfe/) as an optional browser layer for AEs doing targeted account research. That stack, combined with a solid [cold email infrastructure setup](/blog/2026-07-12-maildoso-vs-mailforge-vs-warmforge-best-cold-email-infra-202/), covers the full outbound surface from data to send.

Pick based on where your workflow starts. Rep browsing LinkedIn? Surfe. Programmatic enrichment at volume? LeadMagic. Deliverability-first email list? Findymail. The answer is usually obvious once you stop shopping by feature count and start shopping by workflow fit.
