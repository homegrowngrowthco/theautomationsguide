If you are still manually exporting Zoom registrant CSVs after every webinar and importing them into [Kit](/go/kit/), you are leaking the most important signal the webinar generates: whether someone actually showed up. The attendee/no-show split is where segmented follow-up sequences live, and it is completely invisible inside a flat CSV export. This tutorial walks through the three real middleware options for Zoom-to-Kit integrations, with concrete steps and the specific branching logic that makes the automation worth building.

## Why this workflow matters more than a simple subscribe trigger

Webinar registration alone is a weak signal. The useful data is layered: registered and attended, registered and did not attend, attended for less than 10 minutes versus stayed for the full session. Kit lets you act on all of that via tags and sequences, but only if the data arrives in the right shape.

The standard failure mode here is piping every registrant into a single Kit tag with a single follow-up sequence. That treats a hot prospect who asked three questions during Q&A exactly the same as someone who registered, forgot, and never logged in. A well-structured Zoom-to-Kit workflow applies at minimum three tags per webinar event: `webinar-registered`, `webinar-attended`, and `webinar-no-show`. From there, Kit's automation rules fan out into differentiated sequences.

Zoom exposes this data through its webhook system, but there is no native Kit connector. You need a middleware layer. The three options I'd point any operator to are [Zapier](/go/zapier/), [Make](/go/make/), and [n8n](/go/n8n/). Each carries a different tradeoff that affects which one fits your stack.

## Setting up the workflow

Before writing a single Zap or scenario, get the Zoom side clean. In Zoom's App Marketplace, create a Webhook-Only app (or use the Server-to-Server OAuth app if you want tighter auth). Subscribe to three event types: `webinar.registration_created`, `webinar.participant_joined`, and `webinar.ended` (from which you can derive no-shows by cross-referencing who joined against who registered). On the Kit side, create your webinar-specific tags in advance and note the tag IDs from Kit's API. The [Kit developer docs](https://developers.kit.com) show the exact `POST /v2/tags/{id}/subscribers` endpoint you will be hitting.

## Sequence design for attendees vs. no-shows

The tagging is the infrastructure. The sequences are where the revenue actually lives. For attendees, the first email should go out within 30 minutes of the webinar ending, while attention is still warm. Reference something specific from the session rather than a generic "Thanks for attending" opener. Kit's custom fields let you pass in the webinar title so the sequence feels personalized without manual work.

For no-shows, I'd argue the conventional "send immediately" instinct is wrong. Delay the first email by two to four hours. Lead with the replay link, not a hard CTA. [Kit's automation rules](https://help.kit.com/en/articles/2502638-rules) let you set up a simple conditional: if the subscriber clicks the replay link within 48 hours, move them to the attendee sequence. That click signal is nearly as strong as live attendance for conversion intent.

This same tagging-plus-sequence pattern applies directly to course and membership funnels, which is why it clusters naturally with [connecting Kit to Kajabi](/blog/2026-10-18-connect-kit-to-kajabi-automate-your-course-and-newsletter-le/). The subscriber intelligence you build in Kit from webinar behavior compounds across every product you sell.

## Putting it together

The Zoom-to-Kit integration is about four hours of setup time across middleware configuration, Kit tag creation, and sequence drafting. The operational payoff is real: every webinar you run from that point forward automatically segments your list, starts warm follow-up within minutes of the session ending, and routes high-intent attendees into a faster conversion path than no-shows. The [Kit developer docs](https://developers.kit.com) cover the subscriber tag API in detail if you want to extend the workflow further, for example by passing custom fields like attendance duration from the Zoom Reports payload. Start with the three-tag minimum, get that working cleanly, and layer on complexity from there.
