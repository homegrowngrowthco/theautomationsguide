If you are evaluating [ActiveCampaign](/go/activecampaign/) against [Drip](/go/dripify/) for a B2B motion in 2026, you are looking at two tools that were never designed with the same buyer in mind. ActiveCampaign grew into a full marketing automation and CRM platform, used heavily by B2B SaaS and professional services teams. Drip was built for DTC and ecommerce operators who care about purchase history, Shopify webhooks, and revenue attribution inside the tool. That lineage matters when you are building nurture sequences off deal stage changes, scoring leads against firmographic signals, or routing contacts through a multi-step sales motion.

I'd argue the comparison is settled faster than most buyers expect. But there are real edge cases, so here is the full breakdown.

## What each platform actually does

ActiveCampaign and Drip both send emails and run automations. That is where the similarity mostly ends.

ActiveCampaign's automation builder lets you branch on contact field values, deal stages, site tracking events, lead scores, and custom objects. You can trigger a sequence when a deal moves to "Proposal Sent," wait 48 hours, check if the contact opened a prior email, and fork into two completely different nurture paths. That kind of logic is table-stakes for B2B marketing automation, and [ActiveCampaign's automation docs](https://help.activecampaign.com/hc/en-us/categories/115000769470-Automations) confirm all of it ships on the mid-tier Plus plan and above. My read is that the Plus plan is the realistic entry point for any B2B team doing meaningful segmentation.

Drip's automation canvas is clean and readable. Its triggers are strong for ecommerce: cart abandonment, product view sequences, Shopify order events, revenue attribution back to the email that converted. For a B2B team selling annual SaaS contracts, almost none of those triggers have an analog. You can still build behavioral email sequences in Drip, but you will be working around the tool's native assumptions, not with them.

## How to choose

The first thing I'd check is your automation trigger map. Write out the top five things that should kick off a sequence in your GTM motion. If four of them are things like "contact visited pricing page," "deal moved to Discovery stage," or "lead score crossed 40," ActiveCampaign handles all of those natively. If your triggers are mostly "customer placed second order" or "abandoned cart after viewing a specific SKU," Drip is the more honest fit.

For B2B teams watching budget, [Brevo](/go/brevo/) is the strongest alternative at the entry tier. It has a real free plan (300 emails per day, per [Brevo's pricing page](https://www.brevo.com/pricing/)), a lightweight CRM, and multi-channel coverage (SMS plus email) that neither ActiveCampaign nor Drip offers at comparable price points. The automation builder is less sophisticated, but if you are early-stage and your sequences run under five steps, Brevo will not hold you back. I covered the [Brevo expert setup workflow](/blog/2026-11-20-brevo-expert-setup-automations-segments-and-crm-sync/) in detail if you want to pressure-test it before committing.

[Kit](/go/kit/) is a different category entirely. It is the right answer to a different question: excellent for creator-led B2B (consultants, newsletter operators, fractional executives building a list), but not a fit for a team running a multi-rep sales motion. Comparing Kit to ActiveCampaign for a 10-person SaaS GTM team means you are comparing the wrong things.

For teams already in [HubSpot](/tools/hubspot/) or [Pipedrive](/tools/pipedrive/), the honest answer is that you may not need a standalone email automation platform at all. HubSpot's sequences and workflows cover a large share of what ActiveCampaign provides, especially on Marketing Hub Pro. The reason to consider ActiveCampaign separately is almost always cost: strong automation and a basic CRM at a price point HubSpot does not touch.

## The bottom line on this comparison

ActiveCampaign is the defensible default for B2B teams in 2026. The automation depth, the built-in CRM, the lead scoring, and the breadth of integrations with tools like [Salesforce](/tools/salesforce/), Pipedrive, and [Zapier](/tools/zapier/) make it the most complete package at its price tier. Drip earns its place in ecommerce stacks and has real strengths in revenue attribution and purchase-behavior segmentation. If your model has a meaningful DTC or product-led component alongside B2B, Drip's native ecommerce triggers might genuinely earn their keep.

For a pure B2B SaaS or professional services motion, I'd pick ActiveCampaign without much deliberation, then layer [Brevo](/tools/brevo/) as a secondary channel tool if multi-channel reach matters and budget is tight. The [G2 comparison data](https://www.g2.com/compare/activecampaign-vs-drip) reflects the same split: ActiveCampaign skews toward marketing agencies and SaaS; Drip's reviewer base is overwhelmingly ecommerce.
