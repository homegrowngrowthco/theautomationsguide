[Vector](/go/vector/) de-anonymizes your website traffic and scores those accounts for outbound routing. Both claim to fire better pipeline. Neither does what the other does. The real question is which signal type fits your motion in 2026, and whether you need both at all.

## What each tool actually does

These are not interchangeable products wearing the same "intent" label. I'd argue the category name is doing damage here. It lumps together two fundamentally different data layers that happen to share an outbound use case. Calling both "intent tools" is like calling a CRM and a data warehouse both "databases." Technically accurate. Practically useless.

[Trigify](/tools/trigify/) monitors publicly available social signals: LinkedIn post engagement, follower events, job title changes, and technographic shifts. When a prospect at a target account likes a competitor's post or a champion moves to a new company, Trigify fires a webhook or routes them into a sequence. The signal is behavioral and social. It works best upstream of a formal buying cycle, which is why it pairs naturally with [Clay](/tools/clay/) waterfall enrichment and tools like Smartlead or Instantly for sequencing.

[Vector](/tools/vector/) sits on the other side of the funnel. Drop a tracking pixel on your site and Vector resolves anonymous traffic to company level (and sometimes individual level), scores those visits by page depth and recency, then routes the resulting leads to your CRM or Slack. It is much closer to what [RB2B](/tools/rb2b/) does at the individual resolution layer, though Vector adds account scoring and CRM routing that RB2B leaves to you. If you have read our [Vector review](/blog/2026-11-14-vector-review-2026-visitor-signals-that-fire-outbound/), this is familiar ground.

## How to choose

The first thing I'd check is whether your problem is "who should I reach out to and when" or "who visited my site and did not convert." Those are different questions. They require different tools. Getting this wrong means paying for a category of signal that never matches your actual motion.

If your team runs a proactive outbound motion, calling on cold accounts or working a named account list, Trigify is the stronger fit. It tells you when someone on your list just did something that signals buying intent, without waiting for them to find you first. The trigger-to-sequence latency is tight enough that a well-configured Trigify workflow can get a rep-personalized email out within an hour of a LinkedIn signal firing. Per [Trigify's own integration documentation](https://trigify.io), that speed is precisely where the reply rate lift comes from.

If you have an inbound-led or product-led motion where accounts are visiting your site but not converting, Vector fills the gap that form fills miss. The [visitor identification approach](/blog/2026-11-14-vector-review-2026-visitor-signals-that-fire-outbound/) is especially valuable for mid-market SaaS where the buyer reads your pricing page three times before anyone on your team knows they exist. Vector surfaces those accounts in time to run a warm outbound touch rather than waiting for a lost-deal notification to land.

My read is that most teams shopping this comparison over-index on Vector because "website intent" sounds more sophisticated. It is not. It is just a different layer. If you do not have the traffic, you do not have the signal.

## The stack context matters as much as the tool

Neither Trigify nor Vector operates in isolation, and the tooling around them usually determines whether the signal actually converts to pipeline. A Trigify trigger that lands in a generic sequence is not materially better than a cold email. The lift comes when the trigger informs a personalized opening line. That is why the [Clay](/tools/clay/) integration is the common thread in high-performing Trigify deployments: Trigify fires, Clay enriches and builds the personalization variable, then Smartlead or Outreach sends the sequence.

Vector's output is similarly dependent on what happens downstream. If a visitor lead gets created in [HubSpot](/tools/hubspot/) and then sits in an unworked queue, the 48-hour decay window closes and the signal is worthless. The teams getting value from Vector have an SDR or a Slack-to-sequence automation that fires the moment a scoring threshold is crossed. Without that routing layer, the tool is a reporting dashboard, not a pipeline driver. I'd strongly recommend building the downstream automation before you buy the seat.

There is also a deduplication problem worth taking seriously if you are evaluating both tools simultaneously. A named account contact can appear in Trigify as a social trigger and in Vector as a site visitor in the same week. Without a contact-level suppression layer in your CRM or sequencer, you can easily fire two separate sequences at the same person from two different signals. That is a [deliverability and reputation risk](/blog/2026-11-13-smartlead-deliverability-problems-fixes-and-pivots/), not a pipeline multiplier. Per [G2's intent data category research](https://www.g2.com/categories/buyer-intent-data-tools), multi-signal overlap is one of the most commonly cited operational complaints from teams running more than one intent tool. Worth solving before it costs you a good account.

For the cold email infrastructure side of either workflow, our [Smartlead deliverability breakdown](/blog/2026-11-13-smartlead-deliverability-problems-fixes-and-pivots/) covers the sequencer piece in detail. And if you are thinking about warm outbound from visitor data more broadly, [RB2B](/tools/rb2b/) is worth evaluating alongside Vector for individual-level resolution.
