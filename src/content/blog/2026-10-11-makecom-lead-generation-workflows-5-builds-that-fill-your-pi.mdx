## Why Make.com Is Actually a Lead Generation Tool

The common frame is wrong. Make is not a task automation tool. It is a workflow orchestration layer that happens to have over 1,000 native app connectors. For lead generation specifically, that means you can express the full lead lifecycle inside a single scenario: capture, enrich, score, route, sequence, and notify, with conditional logic at each gate.

The typical failure is teams using Make for one-step integrations (form submission fires into a spreadsheet) when the real value is multi-module scenarios that encode your actual qualification logic. The five builds below are all multi-step by design. One-step integrations are what Zapier is for.

## The Gotcha That Kills Most Make Lead Workflows

The most common failure point is the missing error handler on the enrichment step. When Clay or any enrichment API returns a 429 or a partial response, Make's default behavior is to stop the scenario run and mark the bundle as failed. Your lead is silently dropped. No alert. No fallback record.

<SideBySide>
  <Fragment slot="left">
    **The trap:** You build the happy path (form fires, enrichment returns, HubSpot creates contact) and ship it. Two weeks later you notice a gap in HubSpot contacts. The Make scenario history shows 40 failed bundles from enrichment timeouts. None of those leads made it to your sequencer.

    Fix this before the first lead goes live. Add an error handler route on every HTTP or webhook module that calls an external enrichment API. Route failures to a fallback: create the contact in HubSpot with only the raw form data, tag it with a "needs-enrichment" property, and alert Slack. You can re-enrich manually or run a cleanup scenario on a schedule. The error route is not optional polish. It is the build.

<MyTake>
The error handling layer is not an afterthought. It IS the build. A Make scenario that drops leads silently is worse than a manual process, because at least a manual process has a human noticing the gap. Most RevOps operators spend 90% of their scenario-building time on the happy path and zero time on error routes, then wonder why contacts are missing in HubSpot two weeks later. Build the fallback first. The happy path is easy.
</MyTake>

## Putting the Stack Together

[Make](/tools/make/) is genuinely good at the orchestration problem in B2B lead generation: connecting a form tool to an enrichment layer to a CRM to a sequencer, with conditional routing and error handling, all without engineering involvement. The five builds above cover the most common pipeline gaps RevOps teams tend to patch manually. Start with Build 1 (Fillout to [Clay](/tools/clay/) to [HubSpot](/tools/hubspot/)) as your foundation. Add error handlers before you go live. Layer the intent and deanonymization builds on top once the inbound path is solid.

The [n8n](/tools/n8n/) swap only makes sense once you have a developer who will own the infrastructure and you are north of 50,000 operations per month, where per-op pricing starts to add up meaningfully. For most RevOps operators without that, [Make](/go/make/) is the right call.
