Cold email infrastructure pricing is weirdly opaque for a category that claims to simplify outbound. ## What Maildoso pricing actually includes

The headline number for Maildoso is approximately $3 per inbox per month. At that price, you get domain registration, DNS configuration (SPF, DKIM, DMARC), inbox creation, and automated warming. That is a meaningful bundle. Price those components separately on Google Workspace or Microsoft 365 and you are looking at $6 to $7 per inbox just for the seat, plus whatever you spend on a warming tool like [Warmforge](/go/warmforge/) or a comparable third-party service on top.

The all-in-one rate is Maildoso's core value proposition. For teams standing up 20 to 100 inboxes quickly, it is genuinely compelling. A 50-inbox deployment at $3 each runs $150 per month. The same setup through [Google Workspace Starter](https://workspace.google.com/pricing) ($6 per seat) plus a Warmforge subscription runs $300 to $400 per month before you factor in domain registration costs.

Where the pricing gets more nuanced is at scale. Maildoso offers volume pricing for larger inbox counts, but the published tiers are not as granular as what [Mailforge publishes](https://www.mailforge.ai/pricing). Teams running 150 to 500 inboxes across multiple campaigns often find the Maildoso rate stays flatter while Mailforge's volume tiers compress the per-inbox cost more aggressively. The first thing I'd check before committing at that scale is a side-by-side inbox count quote from both vendors.

One thing Maildoso does not charge separately for: DNS setup. That sounds obvious, but several cheaper alternatives make you configure records yourself or charge a one-time setup fee. Maildoso handles propagation and record verification as part of onboarding, which matters if your team does not have an ops person comfortable in Cloudflare or Route 53.

## The true cost: running the math for a real team

Say you are running a 3-person SDR team sending from 60 inboxes across 20 domains (three inboxes per domain, a sensible ratio for cold email). Here is how the monthly math shakes out across the main options.

**Maildoso at $3/inbox:** 60 inboxes x $3 = $180/month. Domains and warming included. No warmup tool needed.

**Google Workspace Starter plus Warmforge:** 60 seats x $6 = $360 for inboxes. Domain registration at roughly $12/domain/year = $240 annualized, or $20/month for 20 domains. [Warmforge's published pricing](https://warmforge.io/pricing) adds another $49 to $99 per month depending on tier. Total: $430 to $480 per month. That is 2.4x to 2.7x the Maildoso cost for identical infrastructure.

**Mailforge at comparable volume:** Mailforge's pricing is structured differently, with domain slots and inbox counts broken out. At 60 inboxes, Mailforge lands in a similar range to Maildoso, sometimes slightly higher. But it starts pulling ahead on cost per inbox once you cross 200 inboxes, where its volume discounts actually kick in and Maildoso's per-inbox rate stays flat.

My read is that Maildoso is the right default for teams under 150 inboxes who want to get running fast. Above that threshold, get a Mailforge quote before committing.

## Where Maildoso earns its price and where it does not

Maildoso earns it on onboarding speed and simplicity. If you are setting up a new outbound motion and do not want to spend a week on DNS configuration, inbox provisioning, and warming schedules, Maildoso collapses that into hours. For teams running cold email alongside tools like [Instantly](/go/instantly/) or [Smartlead](/go/smartlead/), that matters. Both senders support Maildoso inboxes directly, and [the ROI tradeoffs in cold email infrastructure](/blog/2026-11-08-instantly-vs-clay-vs-apollo-lead-gen-roi-compared/) often come down to how much ops time you are spending on setup versus sending.

Where I'd push back on Maildoso is deliverability transparency. The warmup is automated, but the reporting on inbox health, spam folder placement rates, and domain reputation scoring is thinner than what Mailforge or a dedicated tool like [Mailreach](/go/mailreach/) surfaces. For teams that track deliverability metrics closely, that is a real gap. You are trusting the warming algorithm without much visibility into whether it is actually working.

The domain limit question comes up often. Maildoso does not cap the number of domains you can provision per se, but the practical limit is whatever your budget supports at $3 per inbox. Want 100 domains with two inboxes each? That is 200 inboxes at $600 per month. Still below what a comparable Google Workspace build costs, but a meaningful commitment. At that domain density, I'd run a [deliverability comparison between your sending platform options](/blog/2026-11-06-lemlist-vs-brevo-vs-smartlead-best-cold-email-tool-roi/) to confirm your sender is extracting value from the infrastructure spend.

<MyTake>
Maildoso's bundled pricing is genuinely well-designed, but the marketing undersells one real weakness: the warmup is a black box. You get no visibility into placement rates or domain reputation drift until something breaks. Mailforge gives you actual levers to pull when inboxes start sliding toward spam folders. If deliverability is your primary concern rather than setup speed, pay the modest Mailforge premium upfront rather than debug placement issues after the sequences are already running.
</MyTake>

## The bottom line on Maildoso pricing

Maildoso at roughly $3 per inbox per month is one of the better-priced managed infrastructure options available in 2026, especially once you price in what domain registration, DNS setup, and warming would cost separately. It is the right call for teams under 150 inboxes who want a fast, low-maintenance setup. Above that threshold, [Mailforge](/tools/mailforge/) earns a serious look, particularly if you need volume discounts or deeper deliverability visibility. [Warmforge](/tools/warmforge/) is a complement, not a competitor: it makes sense when you already have inboxes elsewhere and want to bolt on warming analytics without switching infra vendors.
