Webinar leads are some of the warmest prospects in a B2B funnel. Teams waste them constantly by sending the same generic "Thanks for joining" email to everyone, regardless of whether anyone actually showed up. The fix is straightforward: connect [ActiveCampaign](/go/activecampaign/) to [Demio](/go/demio/) via webhook, branch on the `attended` field, and fire distinct follow-up sequences within minutes of the event ending. This guide covers the exact steps, the gotchas, and when to use [Make](/go/make/) versus [Zapier](/go/zapier/) as the middleware.

## Why the native integration is not enough

Demio ships a direct ActiveCampaign connector under its Business plan. It syncs registrants to a list and that is about it. You get a contact record and maybe a tag if you configure it. What you do not get: reliable attendance-status routing, the ability to fire different automations for attendees versus no-shows, or a hook that fires immediately after the webinar ends rather than on a sync schedule.

The webhook approach solves all three. Demio fires a POST request to your middleware the moment a registrant is created, and again when the event ends, with an `attended` boolean in the payload. That single field is what separates a hot follow-up from a re-engagement nurture. Everything else is just plumbing.

## Setting up the workflow

The architecture is simple: Demio webhook fires to Make or Zapier, middleware parses the payload and applies logic, then ActiveCampaign receives a create-or-update call that tags the contact and enters them into the right automation. I'd pick [Make](/go/make/) over Zapier for this if you have any volume at all. The router module in Make lets you branch on `attended` without burning an extra Zap step, and the native iterator handles Demio's registrant array without workarounds.

## Choosing your middleware: Make vs. Zapier

The webhook-to-ActiveCampaign logic is achievable on both platforms. They diverge the moment you need to iterate on the registrant array inside the `Event Ended` payload. Make's [iterator module](https://www.make.com/en/help/tools/iterator) handles the array natively. In Zapier, you need a Formatter step to loop through it, which is clunkier and slower to debug. If you are running more than one webinar a month, that friction compounds fast.

## What to build in ActiveCampaign once contacts land

Tags do the heavy lifting, but the automation logic is where most teams underinvest. For attendees, I'd argue the sequence should open with a recap email that arrives within ten minutes of the event ending (Make can fire this quickly enough), followed by a value-add email at day two and a soft CTA at day five. For no-shows, lead with the recording, not an apology. Give them a reason to watch it, not a reason to feel guilty for missing it.

Don't skip lead scoring here. If you are using [ActiveCampaign](/tools/activecampaign/) lead scoring, add a score increment (+15 for attended, +5 for registered but did not attend) inside the same automation that applies the tag. This surfaces hot leads in your CRM view without manual triage. The [ActiveCampaign vs. Drip comparison](/blog/2026-11-22-activecampaign-vs-drip-which-wins-for-b2b-in-2026/) we published goes deeper on when scoring becomes genuinely useful versus noise.

For teams also running email nurture through [Make](/tools/make/), the same Make scenario that handles the Demio webhook can branch into additional actions: creating a deal in a CRM, notifying a Slack channel when a high-intent attendee registers, or enriching the contact record via a Clay waterfall before it hits ActiveCampaign. Per the [ActiveCampaign automations docs](https://help.activecampaign.com/hc/en-us/articles/221517307-Automations-overview), a tag-based entry trigger is the most reliable way to kick off a sequence from an external tool, so the tag-first approach here is by design.

<MyTake>
Most teams bolt this together fast and never revisit the no-show branch. That is a mistake. No-shows who registered for a specific topic have already self-identified as interested. They just had a conflict. A three-email re-engagement sequence with the replay link, spaced over ten days, will consistently outperform a single generic follow-up. The attended branch gets all the attention because it feels more urgent. My read is that the no-show branch is where the incremental effort actually pays off.
</MyTake>

## One workflow, compounding returns

This integration takes under two hours to build correctly, and it pays back on every webinar you run afterward. The key decisions are using the `Event Ended` payload (not just registration), iterating over the registrants array in Make, and building two distinct automations in ActiveCampaign before you go live. Skip any of those and you end up with a flat list import, which is barely better than a manual CSV export. Per the [Demio webhook docs](https://help.demio.com/en/articles/3867793-webhook-integrations), both the `Registrant Added` and `Event Ended` triggers need to be active for the full picture.
