## What you actually get at each Circleback tier

Circleback publishes two named tiers and a custom enterprise track. Here is how I read what matters in each, stripped of marketing language.

The **Pro plan at $18 per seat per month** (annual) covers the core loop: bot joins your Zoom, Google Meet, or Teams call, transcribes and summarizes it, extracts action items, and pushes them to your connected tools. HubSpot and Salesforce integrations are included at this tier. That is the main reason to pick Circleback over a free tool. You get custom meeting templates so the AI fills structured fields rather than dumping a wall of text into the CRM notes field. That is a real operational difference if your RevOps motion requires consistent deal-stage data capture.

The **Teams or Enterprise tier** adds SSO, admin-level visibility across the org, usage analytics, and priority support. Circleback does not publish a price here. Per the vendor's own positioning, this tier targets orgs with 20-plus seats and compliance requirements. If you need SOC 2 or HIPAA alignment, you are in a custom-quote conversation.

What Circleback does not offer: a free tier, any form of call coaching or rep scoring, or revenue intelligence dashboards. It is a data-capture and CRM-hygiene tool, not a [Gong](/go/gong/)-style conversation intelligence platform. I'd argue that distinction matters more than most buyers realize when they are comparison shopping.

## The true cost: real math for a 10-rep team

Let me run the arithmetic for three common buying situations so you can pressure-test the budget case.

**Scenario A: 10 AEs, Circleback Pro, annual billing.**
10 seats x $18 x 12 months = $2,160 per year. If even two of those reps are month-to-month during a trial extension, add roughly $46 per month for those seats. If you need admin controls and SSO, you are in a custom quote. Budget $2,400 to $3,000 as a realistic annual range for a 10-rep team once you factor in the enterprise feature negotiation.

**Scenario B: 10 AEs, Fathom team plan, annual billing.**
Fathom's team plan is [published at approximately $19 per user per month](https://fathom.video/pricing) for the paid tier. That is 10 x $19 x 12 = $2,280 per year. The difference versus Circleback Pro is roughly $120 per year at that seat count, essentially a rounding error. The real question is whether you need Circleback's structured CRM template fields or whether Fathom's AI summary-to-notes-field approach is good enough for your RevOps process.

**Scenario C: Solo founder or one-rep team.**
Fathom wins this scenario outright. Free is free. Circleback asks you to pay $18 per month minimum. If you are not syncing to a CRM in a structured way, there is no rational case for paying Circleback over Fathom at the individual level. This is not a close call.

**The add-on risk on Circleback:** per the [Circleback pricing page](https://circleback.ai/pricing), the enterprise tier does not list a per-seat rate. Teams that start on Pro and then need SSO or compliance controls mid-year will face a mid-contract conversation. Plan for that if you are at a Series B or later company with an IT security review process.

For teams already running structured RevOps with [HubSpot](/tools/hubspot/) or Salesforce as their system of record, Circleback's template-to-CRM-field workflow is genuinely useful. The alternative is building that logic yourself in Zapier or n8n, which costs engineering time and breaks every time the notetaker changes its output format. For teams that are still figuring out their post-call process, Fathom's free tier is the right starting point. You can always migrate later when the structure actually matters.

If you are comparing Circleback against conversation intelligence tools rather than pure notetakers, [tl;dv](/tools/tl-dv/) is the more honest comparison. At identical price points, tl;dv adds rep coaching and pipeline analytics that Circleback does not touch. The overlap with Gong is real, though tl;dv is priced 60-70% below [Gong](/tools/gong/) for equivalent seats, per published vendor pricing.

For teams evaluating their broader outbound stack alongside notetaker costs, it is worth reading how [calling platforms factor into the total RevOps budget](/blog/2026-11-07-justcall-vs-kixie-vs-aircall-best-calling-platform-for-sdrs/) before locking in another per-seat line item.

## The bottom line on Circleback pricing

Circleback charges a fair price for what it does, but "fair" only applies if you are buying the right thing. The CRM template-to-field write-back is a genuine differentiator that saves RevOps teams from building and maintaining their own post-call enrichment workflows in tools like [n8n](/tools/n8n/) or [Zapier](/tools/zapier/). For a 10-rep team at $2,160 per year, that is easy to justify if it eliminates even a few hours of manual CRM cleanup per week. For solo operators or early teams without structured CRM processes, Fathom's free tier covers 90% of the same surface area at zero cost, and [Fathom](/tools/fathom/) is where I would start before evaluating paid options.
