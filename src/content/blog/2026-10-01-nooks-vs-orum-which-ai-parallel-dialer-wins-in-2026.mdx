The two platforms most SDR leaders are now choosing between are [Nooks](/go/nooks/) and [Orum](/go/orum/). Both promise to multiply live conversations per rep per day. Both have AI somewhere in the pitch deck. But they solve materially different problems, and I've watched teams pick the wrong one based on a 20-minute demo.

## What each platform is actually doing

[Orum](/tools/orum/) has been the throughput-first parallel dialer since its 2020 launch. The core product dials multiple numbers simultaneously per rep, detects voicemail automatically, and connects the rep only when a human picks up. It layers in local presence number management, spam-score monitoring across its carrier network, and a Salesforce-native activity sync that writes back to the record automatically. The AI story at Orum is mostly infrastructure AI (voicemail detection, spam signal avoidance) rather than coaching AI.

[Nooks](/tools/nooks/) took a different architectural bet. Yes, it has parallel dialing. But the product is built around what Nooks calls the "AI Dialer" plus the "AI Coaching" layer, which overlays live call transcription, battle card triggers, and objection-handling prompts directly into the rep's screen during a live call. It also includes a virtual dialing floor with "power hours," a social accountability feature where the whole team dials together on screen, and a blended sequence view so you can mix calls and emails in one workflow without bouncing to a separate SEP.

Don't buy Nooks expecting Orum-level throughput. Its parallel dialing depth is real but it is not the headline feature. If raw simultaneous lines is your primary metric, Nooks will disappoint you.

## CRM integration is where teams get burned

This is where I see the most expensive mistakes. Both vendors will tell you they integrate with your CRM. That is technically true for both platforms with both major CRMs. The nuance matters a lot.

Orum's Salesforce sync is genuinely deep. Call dispositions, recording links, and call outcomes write back to the Lead or Contact record automatically and in near-real-time. The Salesforce reporting you build on top of that data is reliable. Its HubSpot integration uses a connector that syncs activity but can miss edge cases around sequence enrollment state and contact ownership, which creates duplicate task noise in HubSpot.

Nooks flips that story. Its HubSpot integration handles enrollment state better and respects contact ownership rules more cleanly. If you're running [Salesforce](/tools/salesforce/) as your primary CRM with Outreach or Salesloft as your SEP on top, Orum was built for that stack. If you're running [HubSpot](/go/hubspot/) sequences natively and want the dialer to stay inside that same activity model, Nooks fits more naturally.

This matters because mismatched CRM sync means reps double-log calls, managers get inaccurate activity reports, and you lose the AI insights tied to call recordings. In my experience, this specific problem kills adoption of an otherwise solid dialer rollout faster than anything else. Nail the integration story before you sign anything.

## How to choose

The throughput vs. coaching question is the real fork. If your reps are already handling objections well and your problem is purely volume, you want raw parallel lines, clean local presence, and reliable Salesforce writeback. That is Orum's core strength. Orum also makes more sense if your team already uses [Gong](/go/gong/) or Chorus for post-call coaching and you don't want to pay for an overlapping second coaching layer.

If your reps are newer, your connect rates are decent but your conversion-to-meeting rate is soft, or you're trying to reduce reliance on a separate conversation intelligence platform, Nooks' real-time coaching layer starts to look like a more complete investment. The virtual dialing floor and power hours feature also have a measurable effect on ramp time for junior reps. They can hear senior reps' live calls on the floor and get coached in context, not three days later in a recorded review session.

## The call coaching overlap nobody talks about

Here is the thing that doesn't show up in vendor battlecards. If your team already pays for [Gong](/tools/gong/) at $100 to $200 per seat per year and you buy Nooks on top, you are now paying for overlapping conversation intelligence. Nooks gives you real-time transcription and coaching during the call. Gong gives you deeper post-call deal analytics, manager-facing coaching dashboards, and cross-call trend data. There is genuine overlap in the transcription and basic coaching layer.

A SaaS company I consulted with last quarter was running Gong plus Orum and then evaluated Nooks as a combined Gong replacement and dialer upgrade. The math came out roughly cost-neutral but saved the RevOps team one integration to maintain. That consolidation argument is underrated when you're evaluating Nooks. [G2 reviewers consistently flag this coaching overlap](https://www.g2.com/products/nooks/reviews) as both a selling point and a source of internal debate about which tool owns the coaching workflow.

Neither platform fully replaces a dedicated sequence and email tool for cold outbound, either. Nooks blends calls and emails better than Orum, but if you're running high-volume cold email at scale, you still want a dedicated deliverability-aware sender like [Smartlead](/go/smartlead/) or [Instantly](/go/instantly/) running alongside your dialer stack. One tool does not replace the other. Anyone telling you otherwise is oversimplifying the stack.

## The bottom line

Both platforms are genuinely capable parallel dialers in 2026. The choice comes down to CRM depth, coaching philosophy, and team size economics. Orum is the right answer for Salesforce-native, high-volume, experienced teams who want to maximize conversations per hour without adding a coaching layer. Nooks is the right answer for HubSpot shops, blended-motion teams, and managers who want real-time rep development baked into the tool they're already paying for.

Neither is a bad choice. Picking the wrong one for your stack is.
